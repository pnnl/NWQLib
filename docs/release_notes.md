# Release notes

## v1.0.4 (2026-11-05)

Relative to v1.0.3.post2, LCHS gains compact inputs evolved by quantum signal processing (QSP), sampled branch pairs, accuracy requests and an optional automatic choice of its kernel and grid, and its `qsp_block_encoding` backend builds different circuits. QLS builds its inverse and kernel-reflection polynomials in closed form and computes their phases by canonical synthesis to a separate target, so its degrees, phases and success probabilities change. A Run compares an estimate of the memory of its circuits with the Method's `max_bytes` before building them, and the default limits on QPE powers and steps are gone. IBM Runtime compiles one template circuit for the circuits of a Plan that differ only in rotation angles and can run IBM's fractional gates. QPE, LCHS and the Trotter helpers choose the expression of their commutator bound from a count of nested tests instead of from their work and memory limits. ADAPT's default energy thresholds follow the energy unit of the Hamiltonian, `compare` keeps a row for a Method stopped at a memory or work limit, and QHD regroups products of exponentials before evaluating them. The block encoding of a Hermitian band operator and the shared Run and Result archive formats change. This version cannot load Run or Result archives written by v1.0.3.post2.

### LCHS

The [LCHS guide](algorithms/lchs.md) describes each of these changes.

- `LinearDynamics(A=...)` accepts compact forms of $A$ that are never expanded into a matrix of the system dimension: a Pauli sum, including a Qiskit `SparsePauliOp` passed directly, its Hermitian parts $L$ and $H$ given separately with the new `ingest_cartesian`, a Hermitian periodic band operator from the new `ingest_band`, and a block-encoding circuit you supply with the new `ingest_block_encoding` ([Inputs](algorithms/lchs.md#inputs)). The default `hamiltonian_evolution_backend` is now `"auto"`, which keeps `dense_exact` for a dense $A$ and chooses `qsp_block_encoding` for a compact one. A periodic stencil still needs an explicit `"trotter"`. With the default coherent estimator, LCHS evolves a supplied encoding of a general $A$ and refuses one asserted Hermitian, as $A$ or as a part, whenever the state evolves.
- `qsp_block_encoding` chooses among shared real QSVT with or without oblivious amplitude amplification, the two-sided and one-sided Jacobi–Anger polynomials of a qubitized walk, the imaginary-time filter when $H$ is a multiple of the identity, and the product of that filter with a real-time route when $L$ and $H$ are commuting periodic band operators. These routes replace the evolution of v1.0.3.post2, which controlled each whole cosine and sine pass, so the circuits, CX counts and recovery factor of an existing `qsp_block_encoding` configuration change, and with the recovery factor the success probability and the sampling radius at a fixed number of shots. `dense_control_route` no longer chooses how this backend controls a dense-dilation child, which these evolutions control gate-wise on every route. It still chooses the controlled synthesis of each `dense_exact` branch. The new `qsp_route` names a route or leaves the choice to planning, which compares the CX counts of the routes whose count is known before any phase is synthesized, and uses a fixed order of query calls, total phase-polynomial degree and recovery when no eligible route has a known count ([Evolution backends](algorithms/lchs.md#native-methods-and-physical-coordinates)). Only the chosen route's phases are synthesized. `max_qsp_degree` and `max_qsp_evaluations` are removed. Each phase synthesis doubles its transform length until its residual meets the allowance. It stops with an error after a miss at a length that an ideal-arithmetic bound calls sufficient, or when the arrays of the next length would exceed `max_bytes`. `lcu_state_preparation="analytic"` prepares the Low–Somma kernel factor from its closed form. The derivations are on the [LCHS derivations](algorithms/lchs_derivations.md) page.
- On the shared raw and oblivious-amplitude-amplification routes, the phase tolerance per parity follows from the allowance that remains after the truncation tail, through the `residual_tolerance` of `build_qsp_evolution_encoding`. The comparison of the residual is nominal, because the residual has no arithmetic enclosure. On these routes a printed Result gives the target per parity and the saved residuals, which are coefficient one-norms.
- Each LCHS k-node removes a Pauli coefficient whose modulus is at most `1e-12` times the node's largest operand, `max(|h|, |k*l|)` over all labels with the identity included, instead of at most the absolute `1e-12`. Changing the unit of A and T together therefore prunes the same terms. A term beside a large identity can be removed, where an absolute cutoff kept it. A fixed-step run reports the removed mass as `trotter_pruning` on `plan.facts` and in the printed Result, in the frame of the physical solution, and on `result.facts` of a classical Result. It is one component, it enters no total, and the total error of a fixed-step run stays unknown. A budgeted route counts the removed mass against its allowance and refuses when it exhausts it. The content ids of fixed-step LCHS Plans change wherever the new cutoff prunes differently ([Pauli pruning](algorithms/lchs.md#pauli-pruning)).
- A periodic LCHS circuit and a sampled row are compared with `max_bytes` at preparation, by estimates with their own coefficients ([Engineering constants](ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md#prepared-circuit-memory-estimate)). Planning a periodic construction refuses on its work and not on its bytes, so a Plan used only for its estimate stays available. A template of sampled rows is built only after this comparison and one at a time.
- `LCHS(lchs_kernel="auto", k_quadrature="auto")` compares a fixed list of at most 12 kernel–quadrature pairs on the coherent QSP route and keeps the least costly for the requested output ([Automatic choice of the kernel and grid](algorithms/lchs.md#automatic-kernel-and-grid)). The default pair is unchanged. The new `apel_berntson` kernel with its `tanh_sinh` rule is one of the pairs.
- `plan`, `solve` and `compare` accept `accuracy=Accuracy(absolute_tolerance=..., component="sampling")` for an LCHS `NormSquared` or `QuadraticForm` output, and choose the shots whose sampling radius meets it ([Accuracy requests](algorithms/lchs.md#accuracy-requests)). The new `moment_failure_probability` sets the failure probability of the sampling radius that a shot-sampled scalar output reports.
- `observable_estimator="sampled_pairs"` estimates a norm or an observable of $u(T)$ from random pairs of branches, with product-formula evolutions and their Richardson, compensated or multi-product recipes, or with QSP polynomials from a finite phase bank (`pair_qsp_bank`), and reports covering radii in `result.pair_estimate` ([Sampled branch pairs](algorithms/lchs.md#sampled-branch-pairs)). `sampled_pair_progress` reads the rows of an unfinished Run.
- `quantum_resources.sample_resources`, which built representative circuits to count their operations, is removed, and so is `nwqlib.backends.SampledBlock`, whose only use was that sampling. The new `resource_breakdown(plan)` lists each measurement batch's selected blocks with their calls per shot and their per-call cost rules in a named gate basis, without building a circuit, and `estimate(plan, context=ResourceContext(basis="cx"))` gives the totals ([Resource inspection](algorithms/lchs.md#resource-inspection)).
- The concrete error components of an LCHS `Plan` and `Result`, such as `kernel_approximation`, `k_quadrature` and `duhamel_quadrature`, state that their bounds are for the ideal rule with exact nodes, weights and coefficients. The binary64 construction of the coefficient table (quadrature and Duhamel nodes and weights, kernel values) belongs to `native_floating_point`, which stays unknown and gives that construction and the floating-point execution as its reason, instead of `selected error component is unavailable`. These strings enter the content hashes, so the content ids of LCHS Plans, their constructions and error models, and Results change. The computed solutions are unchanged.
- The new LCHS coherent routes and automatic kernel–quadrature comparison require enough memory and work to evaluate every eligible alternative. If a calculation exceeds `max_bytes` (the physical memory when it is `None`) or an explicit work limit, planning reports the amount needed. With the same Method, inputs and seed, computers with enough memory return the same Plan, including routes ruled out on mathematical or structural grounds. If computing the structural bounds of a compact $A$ exceeds `max_bytes`, `compare` reports the memory requirement. See [Evolution backends](algorithms/lchs.md#native-methods-and-physical-coordinates) and [Automatic choice of the kernel and grid](algorithms/lchs.md#automatic-kernel-and-grid).
- A coherent `NormSquared` Result with finite `shots` now fills `submitted_shots`, `returned_shots` and `selected_shots`, which were `None` for this output in v1.0.3.post2. These counts change neither its value nor its sampling radius ([Outputs](algorithms/lchs.md#outputs)).
- A quantum `dense_exact` plan on the composite Gauss grid is refused before the grid's nodes and weights are computed when the node count alone, rounded up to a power of two, exceeds `max_dense_select_slots`. This does not change which requests planning refuses, and the exact check of the final layout stays ([Quadrature selection](algorithms/lchs.md#quadrature-selection)). Because the check now comes earlier, a request that also has a fault found later in planning reports the slot limit first, and `compare` gives a limited row for it even when that fault alone would give an inapplicable row. The refusal names the limit to set, and advises a shorter time or a looser `approximation_tolerance` only for a k grid chosen from those two, since an explicit grid such as `signed_binary_uniform` sets its own size.
- A `low_somma_f2` Plan now reports the value that its product-formula synthesis and preparation stages get from the chosen construction, in place of the reason `unbudgeted_stage:...`. A classical Result reports the synthesis bound from its executed applications. A direct preparation reports zero, and `trotter_error_budgeted` and the periodic Strang route report their `trotter_synthesis` bounds. A stage without a bound, such as fixed-step `trotter` synthesis or MPS coefficient preparation, stays unknown with the reason `missing_stage:...`, as with the other pairs. On the routes where v1.0.3.post2 left it unknown, including `qsp_block_encoding`, `algorithmic_approximation` now reports the converted sum when every stage has a value and the requested output's conversion conditions are satisfied. Coherent scalar outputs estimated from finite-shot counts still leave this fact unknown because their conversion needs the norm from an exact readout. `native_floating_point` stays a separate unknown term, so the sum does not bound the total physical-output error. The grid, coefficients and route that planning selects do not depend on these reported values, and `certificate_status="incomplete"` and `unbudgeted_error_stages` stay on the `compatibility` field of the coefficient table ([Selection and accuracy](algorithms/lchs.md#selection-and-accuracy)).
- `ingest_cartesian(L, H, dissipative_lower_bound=...)` states a lower bound of a Pauli or band `L` that its coefficients do not show, and planning uses the larger of it and the structural bound. For a ring Laplacian written as a Pauli sum on 5 qubits at T = 5, an accuracy request of radius .01 falls from 6.05e10 to 6.67e4 shots in the planned values. NWQLib does not verify the value. Planning refuses a value above the mean eigenvalue of `L`, or equal to it when `L` is not a multiple of the identity, because the smallest eigenvalue cannot reach it, and a false value below the mean plans as if it were true. When a value improves on the structural bound, `psd_premise` reads `"user_assertion"`, and the shift, the kernel grid and the error bounds that use them are conditional on it ([Stating a lower bound of $L$](algorithms/lchs.md#declared-lower-bound)).
- With fixed `shots`, `print(plan)` and `print(result)` report when a compared coherent route has a smaller recovery factor, and `plan.reconstruction.fixed_shot_precision` holds the values. Under `lchs_kernel="auto"` the report names the kernel and quadrature pair of each route, and a Method that names `hamiltonian_evolution_backend="qsp_block_encoding"` with that route and pair reaches the reported recovery. A particular commuting product is reported but cannot be named, because `qsp_route` has no parameter for its H factor. The report changes no route choice.
- With finite `shots` or an `accuracy` request, planning raises `ApplicabilityError` for a circuit that measures more than 64 qubits, because the analysis of the counts reads each outcome as one 64-bit integer, so `compare` gives an inapplicable row. This covers the coherent estimator with `QuadraticForm`, `NormalizedExpectation` or `Samples`, and sampled branch pairs with any output. The analysis of a coherent `NormSquared` reads outcomes of any width.

### QSP and block encodings

- `solve_wx_qsp_phases` and `solve_laurent_qsp_phases` compute phases by a canonical inverse nonlinear Fourier transform, for a real Chebyshev target and a complex Laurent polynomial ([Canonical phase synthesis](algorithms/qsp_phase_derivation.md)). `plan_jacobi_anger_laurent`, `plan_jacobi_anger_gqet`, `prepare_qubitized_target` and `build_qubitized_evolution` build the qubitized evolutions, and `imaginary_time_expansion` and `prepare_imaginary_time_filter` the imaginary-time filter. `imaginary_time_expansion` divides the stored filter coefficients by `scale`, which is one for `beta = 0`, where the filter is the identity. For positive `beta`, `scale` is one binary64 step above the rounded value of `max(1, s)/(1 - margin)`, where `s` bounds the sum of the stored coefficients from above within about two units in the last place. Every partial sum of the series is below one in exact arithmetic, so `s` exceeds one only through the rounding errors of the stored coefficients and the two upward steps of `s`. LCHS refuses the imaginary-time route at a margin, before any phase synthesis, when `scale` exceeds one binary64 step above the rounded value of `1/(1 - margin)`, the value that its synthesis allowance assumes.
- `solve_symmetric_qsp_phases` and `SymmetricQSPPhases` are removed. `solve_wx_qsp_phases` computes the phases of the same real definite-parity targets, whose norming bound must be strictly below one. Each synthesis doubles its transform length until the coefficient residual of the rebuilt polynomial meets `residual_tolerance`. It stops with an error after a miss at a length that an ideal-arithmetic bound calls sufficient, at an explicit `max_fft`, or when the arrays of the next length would exceed `max_bytes`. The error for a missed target gives the target, the attempted FFT lengths and residuals, the lengths at which no doubling reduced the residual by more than half, and the controls. `PhaseSynthesisEvidence` records the attempted lengths and residuals. `chebyshev_polynomial_sup_bound` takes no `max_degree`.
- `prepare_qsp_evolution` computes both parity phases with `solve_wx_qsp_phases` under a common cap `target_cap` (default 0.999) instead of the two retry margins, and takes `max_fft` instead of `max_degree`, `max_evaluations` and `limit_name`. `QSPPreparedEvolution` no longer has `attempted_margins` and `margin`. Its `max_bytes` default changes from 10 GB to `None`, the physical memory, as do those of `jacobi_anger_expansion` and `chebyshev_polynomial_sup_bound`. `jacobi_anger_expansion` takes no `max_degree` and chooses its analysis terminal from the Kapteyn and Watson Bessel bounds before the degree ([Proposition 24](mathematics.md#r24)), so its degree and tail bound can differ from v1.0.3.post2.
- The block encoding of a Hermitian band operator is now a Hermitian involution: it pairs each band $b$ with $-b$ and exchanges their labels (Sünderhauf, Campbell and Camps, arXiv:2302.10949v2, Sec. 2.4). The exchange costs at most $a-1$ multi-controlled X gates for $a$ address qubits. For QLS on the periodic three-band stencil it is one CX with an open control per encoding query, so its CX estimate grows by the polynomial degree. The band label exchange does not change the QLS polynomial or its phases.
- A dense or Pauli input that is proved Hermitian and detected as a circulant within the roundoff window is encoded through the Hermitian projection `(C + C^dagger)/2` of its circulant, and `error_bound` adds the bound of that projection. A band specification that you supply already paired is returned unchanged, and a circulant that is not Hermitian stays unpaired and is refused on the routes that need the pairing.
- `access_summary`, `build_access_query` and the joint-generator, node-register and diagonal-table encodings are new public functions of `nwqlib.subroutines.block_encoding` ([Block encoding API](api/subroutines/block_encoding.md)).
- An explicit `implementation="banded"`, including `QLS(block_encoding_implementation="banded")`, compares the entries of a dense matrix within `1e-12 max|A|` instead of `1e-12 max(1, max|A|)`, so in exact arithmetic a nonzero finite factor on `A` does not change which bands it finds. The periodic second difference at scale 1e-12 lost its off-diagonal bands, with `alpha` at half its value and a large `error_bound`, and at scale 1e-13 it was refused as a zero matrix. Results for `max|A| >= 1` do not change. A matrix with `max|A| < 1` whose wrapped diagonals vary by more than the smaller window now raises `ApplicabilityError` where it was accepted with its `error_bound`. For such a matrix use `"auto"`, `"pauli_lcu"` or `"dense_dilation"`, at a higher circuit cost.

### QLS

The [QLS guide](algorithms/qls.md#inverse-polynomial-accuracy) describes the polynomials and the numerical check of the stored inverse table.

- The inverse polynomial of `qsvt_inverse` is the relative minimax polynomial of the smallest odd degree whose residual meets `epsilon_inv`, computed in closed form, and the kernel reflection of the shortcut solvers is computed from Dalzell's closed form by a cosine transform. The degree, rescale and success probability of an existing configuration can change in either direction.
- QLS uses the inverse table only when the binary64 check value of its stored coefficients is at most `epsilon_inv`, and otherwise builds one more table at a larger degree. A tolerance that this check cannot resolve, near the rounding error of the stored coefficients, raises `ValueError` that states the check value and its terms, also when the stored table meets the tolerance.
- `InversePolynomial` records `ideal_degree`, `minimax_error` and `residual_check_value` instead of `candidate_degrees`, `certificate`, `certificate_basis`, `certificate_grid_points` and `lsq_node_count`. `ReflectionPolynomial` no longer has `fit_points`. `QLSReconstruction.phase_fft_lengths` replaces `phase_evaluations`, and `phase_error` is the coefficient residual of the phase synthesis.
- `QLS` no longer has `max_degree` or `max_qsp_evaluations`. In `nwqlib.subroutines.qsp`, `InverseChebyshevPolynomial` replaces `InverseChebyshevFit`, and `inverse_degree_law_bound` is removed. `QLS_FIT_CERTIFICATE_GRID_PER_DEGREE`, `QLS_FIT_LSQ_NODE_MULTIPLIER` and `QLS_INVERSE_DEGREE_LAW_FACTOR` are no longer exported.
- `epsilon_inv` stays the target of the polynomial, so the degree does not depend on the phases. The new `QLS.phase_residual_tolerance` sets the coefficient residual of the QSP phases separately. With its default `None`, `"qsvt_inverse"` takes `max(1e-12, 0.1 * epsilon_inv / (polynomial_kappa * s))` for the rescale `s`, and a shortcut solver takes `1e-12`, so default phases, their transform lengths and small results change. `QLSReconstruction.phase_tolerance` records the target, and `print(result)` gives the actual residual. The phase statement is nominal, because the residual has no arithmetic enclosure, and neither target is a bound on the total error. A shortcut solver reports its phase term beside the direction and has no total-direction assessment.
- Without `relative_tolerance`, `QLSVerification`'s `"inverse_relative_error"` reports the raw relative error divided by `e + polynomial_kappa * s * delta / ||y_ref||`, with `e` the saved `residual_check_value` and `delta` the phase residual, and compares that ratio with 1. The denominator uses `e` in place of the nominal `epsilon_inv` used by v1.0.3.post2, so a Result can fail this component check while its raw error is below `epsilon_inv`, which is neither a new accuracy guarantee nor necessarily a failure of the solver ([Error components](algorithms/qls.md#error-budget)). A check's status concerns that component and is separate from the assessment of the total, which stays `INCONCLUSIVE`. The automatic window of `"inverse_success"` is a heuristic.
- A QLS circuit is compared with `max_bytes` before it is built, by operation class, and the workspace of the shortcut actions is counted, including LAPACK's internal workspace. A large Plan stays available for its estimate, and a `max_bytes` that planning needs can refuse at preparation instead.
- Classical QLS with supplied `alpha` and `kappa` compares them with the endpoints of the factorization it computes, within the relative window `1e-12`, with no added decomposition, and refuses at `solve` where quantum planning refuses at `plan`, so `compare` still gives this route a row with a Plan. A false `kappa` or a singular `A` used to give a Result on this route. The comparison checks consistency and is not a certified enclosure. A shortcut with a numeric `t` holds no factorization, so only a requested `spectral_domain` comparison checks its premises. With `grid` or `noisy_binary_search`, a reference solve that meets a zero pivot raises `ApplicabilityError`, where it raised `LinAlgError`.
- A QLS refusal at a memory or work limit, and a failed phase synthesis, state `polynomial_kappa` and the singular endpoints that planning computed, with a qualified remark at a small `sigma_min/sigma_max`.
- `QLSVerification` gives an unknown fact with the reason when its reference solve fails, instead of raising `numpy.linalg.LinAlgError`.

### Trotter and QPE commutator bounds

- QPE and `bound_variant="auto"` choose the second-order Pauli-triangle expression from its count of nested word tests, available after the pair scan: the full expression at or below 1,300,000,000 tests and the degree-capped relaxation above ([Trotter API](api/subroutines/trotterization.md#the-error-bound)). LCHS contracts the magnitudes of its K k-nodes with the same nested masks and compares the larger of the word tests and the K·N(A) weight products of its K k-nodes with the same threshold, so a table with many nodes can take the relaxation where one node would take the full expression ([Product-formula planning](algorithms/lchs.md#product-formula-planning)). In v1.0.3.post2, QPE and LCHS used the relaxation whenever the full expression did not fit their work and byte limits. A limit now refuses the chosen expression and states the work or bytes it needs, and QPE and LCHS have no option to request the relaxation, so a refused plan needs a larger `max_work`, `max_select_work` or `max_bytes`, or fewer kept terms. Step counts change in both directions: a table that took the relaxation under its limits now takes the full expression when the count allows, and a table above the count takes the relaxation even when the full expression would fit. A 400-term, 12-qubit QCELS Hamiltonian that took the relaxation and 870 common steps under `max_bytes=50_000_000` now takes the full expression and 630 steps both under that limit and under the default.
- `trotter_bound_coefficient`, `evaluate_trotter_bound` and `select_trotter_step_count` default to `bound_variant="auto"`, `max_work=None` and `max_bytes=None`, the physical memory, instead of `"exact_census"`, 1,000,000,000 and 10 GB. A refusal of the full expression also states what `bound_variant="relaxed_prefix"` would need.
- The full expression forms its nested commutation masks in tiles from the stored pairs instead of storing a table of triples, so its memory grows with the number of anticommuting pairs. The byte estimate charges the largest tile that the stored pairs can form, nothing for a table without anticommuting pairs. The reductions of both expressions are grouped by row of pairs, so `W_up` can differ from v1.0.3.post2 in its last bits.
- `QPEReconstruction.nested_word_tests` replaces `census_block` and records the count that chose `bound_variant`.

### QPE, QHD and GCiM results

- The QPE methods default `max_power` and `max_trotter_steps` to `None`, no limit, instead of 100,000 and 1,000,000. A limit you set still refuses, and the refusal names it, its value and the count needed. These defaults change Method and Plan content hashes. Before it builds circuits, a Run compares their memory estimate with `max_bytes`, so a schedule that plans can refuse at `prepare`. Plans with very long powers now plan: at q=26, `max_time=1024` and budget 0.001, planning found a maximum of 13,683,338 steps in 0.017 s (Python 3.12.14, macOS), with no circuit prepared.
- The retired `max_power` default used to limit the number of distinct QPE powers indirectly. The records of a Plan are not counted against `max_bytes`, and nothing refuses for them, so a very large query count can exhaust memory during planning. No exhaustion has been measured, and the size at which it would happen is only a forecast. On sampled quantum RFE and QCELS with small Pauli inputs and the product-formula route, the traced heap peak of the whole plan was about 30 to 31 KB per logical query, which does not describe other routes or bound process memory ([Engineering constants](ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md#qpe-selected-numerical-controls), [open work](ROADMAP.md#qpe-plan-record-scale-on-the-query-count-axis)). A larger `max_bytes` used for a plan-only schedule also applies to `prepare` on that Plan and does not make execution affordable.
- A QPE signal from a unitary target uses `exp(1j*p*angle)` with the phase angle of its eigenvalue, instead of raising the eigenvalue to the power p, so a large power keeps unit modulus. A mixed-state input at large powers keeps the limitation that the [QPE guide](algorithms/qpe.md) describes.
- `QPEVerification.group_atol` defaults to `None`, which groups eigenvalues within `1e-8*(E_max - E_min)` for a Hamiltonian and `1e-8` radians for a unitary, instead of an absolute `1e-8`. Clusters therefore follow the scale of H and do not depend on an energy offset, so the groups of a spectrum whose span is far from one change. A value you set keeps its meaning. The facts `group_tolerance` and `estimated_resolved` report the tolerance used and whether it exceeds a heuristic rounding scale. The new default changes the content hash of `QPEVerification`, whose source version goes from 3 to 4.
- On sampled QCELS, SPE and RFE settings and RWPE, a step count whose exact check misses its budget by rounding tries up to two larger counts before planning refuses. The exact trajectory now does the same on its common grid, which keeps every power position on one step. It checks the next count and then one count from a conservative upper bound on the rounding terms, and it uses a count only when every complete subtotal fits its budget. Exact QPE trajectories that were refused at budgets such as `1e-10` and `1e-11`, or at long evolution times, can now plan, and a `Plan` whose first count passes is unchanged. A refusal by this rule states that it confirmed no count, not that the target error is unattainable, and a larger budget is not guaranteed to pass. A refusal by an explicit limit states the count needed.
- The estimate of an RWPE Plan now covers every step: `settings`, `shots` and CX are the totals over `max_steps`, equal to what the Run submits, where the estimate before counted the first step alone. T, logical depth and arbitrary-rotation counts are unavailable, because feedback angles are not known before running. `estimate_physical` refuses this estimate and names `compile_logical`. The content hashes of RWPE Plans change.
- QHD keeps each exponential of an objective whole when it expands the objective, so `exp(-c*(x-a)**2)`, with exact or `Float` parameters, does not split into factors that overflow separately or pull out a constant below the binary64 range. A resolved off-center Gaussian well now plans where it was refused. This corrects the range of those intermediate values and does not establish numerical stability for every symbolic expression. An exponent is evaluated as written, so on a box far from the origin write `-x**2 + 2000*x - 1000000` as `-(x - 1000)**2`. QHD's preparation workspace is an estimate of the whole circuit, compared with `max_bytes` before any block is derived, also after loading a Plan. The tables of a Plan are checked at their known size before the first one forms.
- A QHD record's rotation count, taken before angles are classified, is an upper bound in both encodings. Classified counts appear in `circuit_resources` and `run_resources`.
- QHD regroups a product of exponential factors, including powers of positive finite numerical bases, before numerical exponentiation. It does so for a product with at least two such factors, and for a single factor when a constant factor, a constant subproduct or the base leaves the normal binary64 range. Objectives such as `exp(-50*x**2 + 300.0*x - 450.0)*exp(-50*y**2 - 300.0*y - 450.0)` and `exp(-800*x)*exp(400*y)*exp(400*z)`, whose tables could be silently zero or wrong with `grid_minimum` agreeing, now tabulate at the values of the objective. A positive non-integer rational base also stays whole in the expansion, which corrects such a base very near 1 beside another exponential factor. Ordinary products such as `2*exp(x)*exp(y)` change rounding, and near a tie or a stopping boundary a selected point or a refinement history can change. Some regrouped products lose a little accuracy. Cancellation, large sums of exponents and operations around a product can still give inaccurate results. A product outside this rule, such as `exp(-800*x)*sinh(400*y)*sinh(400*z)`, can still tabulate as zero with no error, and `.rewrite(sp.exp)` gives its correct table where planning distributes the product ([QHD guide](algorithms/qhd.md#scientific-model-and-ordering)).
- A QHD objective can use `besselj`, `bessely`, `besseli`, `besselk`, `airyai`, `airyaiprime`, `airybi`, `airybiprime`, `LambertW`, `Si`, `Ci`, `Ei`, `erfi`, `erfinv`, `erfcinv`, `digamma`, `trigamma` and `polygamma`, evaluated by SciPy. `loggamma` is also evaluated by SciPy and refuses a nonpositive argument, where it returned the real `log|gamma(x)|`. Write `log(Abs(gamma(x)))` for `log|gamma(x)|`. The guide states the order, branch and arguments of each function.
- `theory_flavor="ir_product"` runs on one binary variable with K = 2, where it raised `TypeError`.
- ADAPT no longer has `max_basis_size` or `max_pool_size`, and passing either raises a validation error. Bounded growth takes this limit from the new `BoundedRitzGrowth.max_basis_size`, default 64, so a bounded run that set it on `ADAPT` moves it there. The default growth is limited by `2*min(pool size, max_iterations)`. The scan of a query's labels is linear in their number instead of quadratic, with the same decisions: 10,000 labels took 2.155 s before and 3.3e-4 s now (Python 3.12.14, macOS). `FixedGCIM` and `QFD` keep `max_basis_size=64`, which limits their settings and kept circuits and is not a limit of the algorithm.
- ADAPT's energy thresholds follow the energy unit of the Hamiltonian ([Energy thresholds and units](algorithms/gcim.md#energy-thresholds-and-units)). `pauli_coefficient_cutoff`, `gradient_norm_floor` and `energy_change_tolerance` default to `None`, which planning replaces by $10^{-14}\Omega$, $10^{-8}\Omega$ and $10^{-6}\Omega$, with $\Omega=\lVert H-\operatorname{tr}(H)I/d\rVert_F/\sqrt d$ on the original dimension. They were the absolute values 1e-14, 1e-8 and 1e-6, so a molecular Hamiltonian in joules lost every term and returned energy 0 as if it had converged. A number you pass keeps its absolute meaning, and `plan.reconstruction` records the values in use. H2, H4 at 1.5 Å and LiH(2,5) in Hartree keep their selected generators and energies, and other inputs can stop at a different round. To keep the absolute 1e-6 Ha of Zheng et al. (arXiv:2312.07691v3) for an operator in Hartree, pass `energy_change_tolerance=1e-6`. $\Omega$ is a scale for these defaults, not an error bound.
- `stop_criterion="residual_norm"` requires `residual_norm_tolerance`, whose default was 1.6e-3, chemical accuracy in Hartree. Pass the value you intend, in your energy unit. `AdaptVerificationOptions.residual_tolerance` also has no default number. Without it the residual check still reports the residual and its interval, and `residual_threshold_met`, `pool_limited` and `stalled` are unavailable, so pass it if you read those flags.
- ADAPT's identity coefficient stays whole under any `pauli_coefficient_cutoff`. With an explicit cutoff above it, energies now include that coefficient and `hamiltonian_dropped_l1` no longer counts it. `print(result)` shows a nonzero `hamiltonian_dropped_l1`, which bounds the removed operator's norm, not the error of the adaptive energy.
- A multiple of the identity ends ADAPT after the reference with the new `stop_reason` `constant_hamiltonian`, under default and bounded growth. Bounded growth no longer measures overlaps for such an operator.
- The mean diagonal that pads a dense or sparse Hamiltonian to a power-of-two dimension, and that classical GCiM removes before its projection, is exactly that constant when the diagonal is constant. A multiple of the identity on a padded quantum route of GCiM or Lanczos therefore has one Pauli term and runs no circuit. QPE pads a constant diagonal with that constant too. Where the earlier rounded mean differed from the constant, for example 0.9 at dimension 3, energies move by rounding.
- ADAPT's product-state optimization gives BFGS the dimensionless $(E-c)/\Omega$ and its gradient, with $c=\operatorname{tr}(H)/d$, a stated gradient tolerance of $10^{-5}$ on that function and recorded energies unchanged in the unit of the Hamiltonian. In joules BFGS previously returned at its first point and reported success. Optimized runs in any unit can take a different path. H4 at 2 Å in STO-3G in Hartree, with `max_iterations=4`, `optimize_every_m=1` and `optimize_rounds_n=20`, went from 32 to 27 BFGS rounds with the same energy to ten digits. BFGS can also end at an iteration or evaluation limit or a failed line search. The test is a stationarity heuristic and bounds neither the residual nor the ground-energy error.
- `OverlapNoiseEstimate` gives a Frobenius root-mean-square variance diagnostic of the sampled overlap matrix for FixedGCIM, QFD and ADAPT, with its measured, structural and observed-zero parts. It changes no rank, cutoff or assessment, and it is not a bound on one realization ([GCiM](algorithms/gcim.md)).

### Comparing Methods

- `compare` records a Method whose planning stops at a memory or work limit as a row with no Plan, `resource_limited=True` and the refusal in `reason`, and the other rows keep their Plans. This covers the physical memory, default caps such as `max_dense_select_slots` and `max_basis_size`, and limits you set, on every built-in Method. It used to raise and return no row. Running such a row raises `ResourceLimitError`, a `ValueError`, which `plan` and `solve` with a Problem also raise. A numerical or accuracy failure in planning, such as a phase synthesis that fails its residual test at a length its own rule calls sufficient, still raises from `compare`. `ObjectiveValue.resource_limited` carries this state into `scan` and saved selections. `comparison_id` values change, so `validate_comparison` refuses a selection saved by an earlier version.
- `nwqlib.algorithms.ResourceLimitError` is public. A Method raises it for its own limits, and `compare` records a limited row. Any other `ValueError`, apart from `ApplicabilityError`, still raises from `compare`.
- A limit refusal states the requirement beside the limit. When LCHS plans block encodings for a QSP generator, a refusal also states what was already drawn from `max_select_work` or held against `max_bytes`, because the limit must cover both.
- ADAPT with `BoundedRitzGrowth(basis_depth_limit=...)` when the reference's CX count bound exceeds the limit or the reference has no CX count, classical Lanczos and Expectation above 64 qubits, and classical ADAPT at 63 and 64 qubits raise `ResourceLimitError`, so `compare` gives a limited row. They raised plain `ValueError`.
- A false premise of a Method for its input raises `ApplicabilityError`, a `ValueError`, so `compare` gives an inapplicable row. This now covers QLS with a singular `A` or an `alpha`, `kappa` or numeric `t` that singular endpoints computed at planning contradict, `QLS(block_encoding_implementation="banded")` on a non-circulant matrix, a dense-dilation `alpha` below the matrix norm, Lanczos with `krylov_dimension` above the dimension, dense LCHS with `make_l_psd=False` and a computed negative eigenvalue of `L`, one-hot QHD with a register above 14284 qubits, ADAPT above 64 qubits, and sampled QLS above the 64 bits that the analysis of its counts can read.
- The `failure` text of an augmented-Lagrangian or box-refinement record whose later round stopped at a limit begins with `ResourceLimitError`.

### Runs, backends and resource estimates

- A Run compares a coarse estimate of the memory of its circuits with the Method's `max_bytes`, or with the physical memory when that is `None`, before it builds them, so a request that exceeds it is refused with nothing built or counted. The refusal states what the estimate counts, what it left out and, for a refusal of one circuit, that `run.release_native()` can make room on Aer. `prepare(plan)` compares the first circuit and the first execution compares the rest of the `Plan`, so `submit` or `run.resume()` can refuse a `Plan` that `prepare` accepted. The estimate is calibrated on Aer, other backends use the same count, and it is not a bound on process memory. Some routes and allocations have no estimate ([Run on a backend](prepared_execution.md#memory-of-the-prepared-circuits)). An explicit `max_bytes` that admitted such circuits can now refuse them. A Run on Aer also compares a saved circuit with the limit before reading it.
- A failed cancellation request no longer stops the refresh that sent it. In v1.0.3.post2 it raised at once, before its job was read and before the other jobs received their requests, and it stayed recorded as sent. The Run now sends every pending job its request and reads that job, saves what the read found, and raises the first such error from `cancel`, `resume` or `wait` after all of them. A request that failed is sent again until the job is recorded as ended, and one interrupted while being sent is not ([Cancel, close and handle failures](prepared_execution.md#cancel-close-and-handle-failures)). The Nexus compile job follows the same rules. These behaviors were checked offline, and no live provider's refusal has been exercised.
- A job that a provider or the local system confirms as ended with no result makes `run.wait()` raise `RunFailed` with `stage="recovery"` instead of polling without end, and a later `run.resume()` can still collect a result that arrives afterwards. A running Slurm job no longer carries failure text. An interrupt during a refresh keeps its original exception and what was saved, and a reopened Aer failure keeps the original failure text.
- A request that a backend can refuse before submitting is refused without being counted. IBM Runtime, IonQ and Nexus check what a readout fixes before the preparation charge, and IonQ, Nexus, NWQ-Sim and Slurm check a job's saved request before its submission intent. `run.extend_limits(...)` then leads past a data-limit refusal, where such a request was recorded as an uncertain attempt with its shots counted before. On a paid path, a Run with no attempt yet also compares its known workload with its limits before the first attempt.
- `logical_depth`, `t_depth` and `non_clifford_depth` read the Program's order as a serial schedule and are labeled `upper_bound` instead of `exact`, with the same values. A memory peak built from declared `Workspace` bytes is labeled `estimate`. A declared peak above a grant makes the capacity check `conditional` with a reason that says it exceeds the grant, instead of proving `infeasible`.
- `adaptive_rounds` and `classical_work` count the `AdaptiveLoop` rounds and classical stages that the Program declares, as their `population` now says. Their values and labels are unchanged. `ModelDomain` limits say where a `TimeModel` applies and are not device capacity limits.
- `estimate_physical` admits a full `clifford_t` compilation that keeps the fixed gates `sx` and `sxdg`, which add nothing to the T count, so a compiled query such as a sampled QCELS one now projects. Other refusals are unchanged. A projection covers one compiled circuit, and another query or RWPE feedback angle can have another T count. A `clifford_reduction` compilation reports its T count as unavailable, because its output writes non-Clifford gates as rotations.
- Exact zeros in an input operator or state are stored as `+0.0`, without changing the bits, dtype or order of other entries, so inputs that differ only in the sign of a zero have the same content id. The extra copy counts against `max_bytes`.
- `Run.resume(rebuild_preparations=True)` finishes a `Plan` with fixed settings whose local preparation was counted but not saved, for example when `max_data_bytes` stopped the Run while it saved a circuit ([Rebuild an interrupted preparation](run_archives.md#rebuild-an-interrupted-preparation)). Each such setting is prepared once more under its saved seed before anything new is submitted. The earlier attempt stays counted, no submitted job is sent again, and a second failure needs another call. `run.wait()` never rebuilds. A Method whose later settings depend on earlier outcomes keeps its own refusal and needs a new Run, which can repeat earlier jobs. In an offline IBM check, a QCELS `Plan` of 22 jobs stopped after 14 finished with 22 jobs in total, where a new Run would have brought the total to 36.
- `resume_augmented_lagrangian` and `resume_box_refinement` still refuse a round or level whose preparation stopped, and their note now names the way on. Open that Run with `load_run`, call `run.resume(rebuild_preparations=True)` and resume again.
- A relative `directory` is resolved when a Run is created or reopened, so changing the working directory no longer stops an open Run.

### IBM Runtime and Method extensions

- When the circuits of a static Plan differ only in rotation angles, as the rows of `LCHS(observable_estimator="sampled_pairs")` do, IBM Runtime preparation compiles one template circuit with those angles as parameters for each group with the same gates, and one job sends all unsubmitted circuits, each group as one Sampler PUB. Every circuit keeps its own preparation record, with its parameter values in the new field `PreparedArtifact.template` (a `TemplateBinding`), its own attempt and its requested shots ([IBM Runtime](ibm.md#ibm-templates), [Backends API](api/backends.md)).
- `IBMRuntimeBackend(use_fractional_gates=True)` opens the device with IBM's fractional gates, RX(θ) at any angle and RZZ(θ) with 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2, and compiles for that device. For a compiled circuit whose RZZ angles are parameters, it folds each circuit's angles into that range without compiling again. Preparation refuses the options that IBM documents as unsupported with fractional gates. The default `False` opens the device as before.
- A Method groups the circuits of a static Plan that differ only in rotation angles with the new hook `Method.row_templates(plan, points)`, which receives each point with its key, its `Experiment` and its range-axis values, and returns `RowTemplate` records ([Extending NWQLib](api/extending.md)). The new hook `Method.has_row_templates(plan)` tells a Run whether to collect a Plan's points for `row_templates`. By default it returns True for a Method that overrides `row_templates`. A Method overrides it to return False for a Plan whose points it never groups, and the Run then generates and runs that Plan's points one at a time.
- A Method's `reconstruction` record can define the optional `_plan_summary_lines`, whose lines `print(plan)` adds after its first line ([Algorithm protocol](algorithm_protocol.md)).
- `IBMRuntimeBackend` no longer takes `max_input_bytes`, so remove it from your calls. A Run compares each circuit's QPY copy with what is left of `max_data_bytes`, and twice its size with the Method's `max_bytes` or the physical memory.

### Other changes

- The roundoff derivation of exact Aer readouts also covers qiskit-aer 0.18.2, whose statevector, fusion and gate-matrix sources are those of 0.18.0. With 0.18.2 installed, a preparation record does not list "unchecked qiskit-aer version". An exact readout then has a state error unless another exclusion applies, such as a supplied matrix, and each bound built on that error still needs its own premises to hold ([Dependency issues](dependency_issues.md#roundoff-of-exact-readouts)).
- On Linux with Qiskit 2.6.0, importing `qiskit_aer` can fail with "cannot allocate memory in static TLS block" even with the OpenMP runtime of PySCF preloaded, as on the GitHub Actions image of Ubuntu 26.04. Install Qiskit 2.6.1 or later on such a host ([Dependency issues](dependency_issues.md#openmp-runtime-with-pyscf-on-linux)).
- The error for a gate that NWQLib cannot copy from a supplied circuit, or for a subcircuit added with `append`, names it and points to `circuit.decompose()`, and the error for a `QuantumCircuit` subclass gives `QuantumCircuit(c.num_qubits).compose(c)`. Which circuits are accepted does not change. [Gates to expand first](own_circuit.md#gates-to-expand-first) shows the two comparisons to keep apart when a definition, such as that of a `PauliEvolutionGate` of noncommuting terms, is an approximation. One compares the result with the decomposed circuit, and the other compares that circuit with the propagator you want.
- An ADAPT Run with `sampling=PencilSampling(total_shots=B)` starts each query of its growth only while `max_total_shots` leaves room for the query's shots and $B$ more, because the Plan spends those shots in its two final stages once growth stops. In v1.0.3.post2 a Run without that room paid for the whole growth and was then refused before the first stage shot. The refusal states the shots needed, and `run.extend_limits(max_total_shots=...)` and `run.resume()` continue the same Run ([GCiM](algorithms/gcim.md#pilot-and-main-shot-allocation)).
- `lower_qiskit` and the circuit construction of a Run set no limit on the visits to Program nodes, `Repeat` multiplicity included, instead of 100,000, which a Run had no control to raise. A Program with a long repetition, such as many Trotter steps, is built. Before any Qiskit gate is built, lowering counts 256 bytes for each instruction the circuit will hold against the new `lower_qiskit(max_bytes=...)`, the physical memory by default and in a Run. `lower_qiskit` no longer takes `max_operations`, `max_qubits` or `max_clbits`, whose defaults refused more than 100,000 visits or 4096 quantum or classical bits, and passing one raises `TypeError`.
- `compile_logical` and `estimate_physical` set no default deadline (`timeout_seconds`), instead of 60 seconds, and `compile_logical` and `export_qasm` set no default limit on the input operations (`max_operations`), instead of 100,000. `compile_logical` no longer compares the compiled gate count with `max_operations`, a comparison that at the default precision refused a Clifford+T compilation of about 400 rotations after the compiler had run. A full compilation instead counts its output QASM text four times against `max_bytes`, and a count-only compilation returns its gate counts without an output check. `estimate_physical` no longer compares `measurement_count` with the compilation's `max_operations`. The `max_operations` and `timeout_seconds` fields of `LogicalCompilation` and the `timeout_seconds` field of `PhysicalProjection` can be `None`.
- `estimate_physical` also projects a `WorkloadEstimate` in the `clifford_t` basis that counts one circuit execution, without compiling ([Estimate fault-tolerant resources](fault-tolerant-resources.md#project-a-resource-estimate-without-compiling)). Its T count is exact when every block has an exact Clifford+T gate list. With the new `TYPICAL_ROTATION_SYNTHESIS` and a precision eps in `ResourceContext`, each arbitrary single-qubit phase counts `ceil(3*log2(1/eps))` T gates, labeled an estimate. Estimates with Qiskit circuit inputs, direct preparations of general vectors or LCU encodings have an unknown T count and are refused, and such circuits go through `compile_logical`. `PhysicalProjection` names its source with `source_id` instead of `compilation_id` and adds `count_source`, `count_population`, `t_interpretation`, `synthesis_source`, `rotation_count` and `rotation_error_sum`, its `requested_synthesis_epsilon` can be `None`, and its last limitation names rotation synthesis error instead of unverified NWQEC synthesis error. A projection saved by v1.0.3.post2 does not load in this version.
- On Aer, the counts circuits of a static Plan that differ only in rotation angles, such as the rows of `LCHS(observable_estimator="sampled_pairs")`, are built and translated once per group with the same gates instead of once per circuit ([Aer](aer.md#shots-seeds-and-limits)). Each circuit binds its angles and runs as its own attempt with its own row's seed, and its preparation record keeps that seed and its values in `PreparedArtifact.template`. A reopened Run binds its remaining circuits to the saved template circuit. The shared translation keeps rotations whose angle is zero, so such a record's `native_operations`, and its counts for the same seed, can differ from v1.0.3.post2, and with a noise model that assigns an error to a kept rotation's gate, so can the distribution of its counts. A group shares a translation only when every operation that carries one of its angles is a Qiskit standard gate. The circuits of other groups, of groups without angles and with exact readouts are built one by one, as in v1.0.3.post2.
- A resource estimate keeps a count exact across a `Branch` whose alternatives have the same exact count, where v1.0.3.post2 labeled it an upper bound. A branch inside one circuit execution therefore leaves its zero setting, shot, evaluation and adaptive-round counts exact, and `estimate_physical` accepts it.
- `ExecutionLimits.max_total_circuits` and `max_total_shots` default to `None`, and a Run on Aer or the local NWQ-Sim runner has no circuit or shot limit unless one is given, instead of 512 circuits and 1,000,000 shots. A Run on IBM, IonQ, Nexus, Slurm or another backend that may charge sets 512 and 1,000,000 when the given limits leave them `None`, and `run.extend_limits(max_total_shots=None)` removes such a budget. The QHD augmented-Lagrangian and box-refinement layers set these budgets once for all their rounds.
- A Plan of a static Method whose `MeasurementBatch` has range axes (`RangeAxis`) runs one circuit for each point of its axes, in increasing order, and a reopened Run submits each point at most once. In v1.0.3.post2, preparation refused an experiment with range axes unless the Program bound every axis parameter, and then ran only that one point. A Program that binds an axis parameter, for example with `program.bind(row=2)`, now fails its structural check, as one whose batch setting binds it already did, because the axis takes every value of its grid. `program.select_experiment(batch_id, setting_index, row=2)` gives the Program of one point, and a one-value axis such as `RangeAxis(parameter="row", start=2, stop=3)` fixes one axis while the others vary. A forecast with a device profile, from `estimate`, `compare` or `scan`, listed range axes as unpredicted in v1.0.3.post2. It now assesses each point, except for a Method whose experiments are query templates (`Method.query_catalog_reason`), whose axes it still lists as unpredicted.
- On a synchronous backend such as Aer, a static Run with a folder makes four run-log commits for each point instead of six. A point's workflow row and runtime seed are saved with its preparation charge, and a synchronous outcome with its collection. The seeds and results are unchanged.
- NWQ-Sim preparation, local or through Slurm, refuses a readout whose result values and binary files alone exceed `max_output_bytes`, and IonQ preparation refuses a circuit whose raw histogram entries, written as compact JSON, could exceed `max_response_bytes`. In v1.0.3.post2 the NWQ-Sim runner refused such a readout after the job was launched or queued, and IonQ refused the response after the paid job had completed. IonQ with 100,000 shots on 20 qubits now needs `max_response_bytes` of at least 3,000,044 ([NWQ-Sim](nwqsim.md), [IonQ](ionq.md#byte-limits-and-timeouts)).
- The byte check of a supplied Qiskit circuit, in `state_input`, `bind_preparation_circuit`, `prepare_qiskit`, `ingest_block_encoding`, `select_block_encoding` and NWQEC compilation, is a coarse estimate of the copy. It counts 256 bytes per instruction and qubit of the circuit and of each copied gate definition, the parameter data of gates other than Qiskit's standard gates, and 256 bytes per annotation modifier, instead of fixed allowances per Python object. For a long circuit of standard gates the estimate is about five times the earlier count, so a `max_bytes` that admitted such a circuit can now refuse it. The `payload_bytes` of such a state or operator input and the `snapshot_data_bytes` of a selected block encoding change accordingly, and so do the content ids of the records that hold them. `state_input` checks 256 bytes per qubit of a circuit before copying it, instead of 16 ([Engineering constants](ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md#supplied-circuit-snapshot-estimate)).
- `estimate`, `compare`, `nwqlib.backends.assessment.estimate_plan` and `assess` no longer take `max_assessments`, and `scan` no longer takes `max_candidates`, `max_values`, `max_assessments` or `max_pair_comparisons`. A forecast of a Plan with more than 4096 assessment and prediction rows, or a scan of more than 256 Plans, 4096 objective values or 1,000,000 coordinate comparisons, is no longer refused, and passing one of these arguments raises `TypeError`. `SearchSelection` no longer has the fields `max_pair_comparisons` and `max_values`, so its content id changes and a saved selection that contains them no longer validates.
- `scan` no longer takes `max_integer_bits`, and `SearchSelection` no longer has that field, so passing it raises `TypeError`, the content id of a selection changes and a saved selection that contains the field no longer validates. Shot and width counts are summed and compared as exact integers. Predicted seconds are the correctly rounded binary64 sum (`math.fsum`) of the forecasts, and a sum beyond the largest binary64 number is unavailable with its reason, which places the row in `incomparable`. In v1.0.3.post2, `scan` stored the exact rational sum of the predictions and refused a value whose exact arithmetic needed integers wider than 4096 bits. Rescoring a saved Comparison can therefore change predicted seconds by one unit in the last place, and rows whose exact sums round to the same binary64 number now tie.
- `ErrorModel.assess`, `assess_result`, `assemble_check`, `Certificate.with_verification`, `linear_variance`, `resolve_scalar_bound`, `infer_binary`, `correct_binary`, `verify_number_sector` and `verify_projected` no longer take `max_integer_bits`, and passing it raises `TypeError`. Exact arithmetic in evidence and in Expectation has no adjustable integer limit, and a value saved in a record must be representable there. In v1.0.3.post2 these functions refused an exact operation whose integers exceeded 4096 bits.
- Binary counts have no 2**53 limit. A `BinaryPopulation`, and the count of a setting that Expectation adds over distinct measurements, hold any count below 10**4300, which a saved record holds. One measurement of a setting, including each calibration circuit of `BinaryReadoutMitigation`, requests at most 2**63-1 shots, and `solve` refuses a larger request before submission. `infer_binary` accepts any family size, rounding `alpha = delta/F` once from the exact quotient, and gives the point estimate and the Hoeffding intervals for every n, rounding a Hoeffding radius below the resolution of the mean outward. A Beta interval is unavailable with its reason when its posterior shapes cannot hold the counts, and its 5e11 shape-total domain is unchanged. An exact empirical variance whose integers exceed what a saved record holds is unavailable with its reason. Sampling planning of an Expectation selects any count below 10**4300. The planned count n exceeds the exact ceiling by at most `1 + n*1e-78` shots, which is at most one shot for n below 10**78 ([Choose shots for a target accuracy](algorithms/expectation.md#selecting-absolute-sampling-accuracy)). In v1.0.3.post2 a population or planned count above 2**53-1 was refused.
- The `projected_backward_error` of FixedGCIM, ADAPT and Lanczos results is the binary64 quotient `||Hc - ESc|| / ((||H||_F + |E| ||S||_F) ||c||)`, with value 0 for a zero residual. For a nonzero residual it is None when the denominator or the quotient is not a finite positive number. In v1.0.3.post2 it was formed from logarithms, and a solve whose logarithmic denominator was not finite raised `RuntimeError`. The value can change by several units in the last place, which changes the content id of a Result that holds it. A projected solve or a classical GCIM projection forms the Hermitian part `(A + A^dagger)/2` directly. If an entry of the sum `A + A^dagger` overflows binary64 before division by two, the calculation raises `ValueError`.
- QHD evaluates each support table, the grid checks of the augmented-Lagrangian layer and the grid-minimum references of `QHDVerification` and `constrained_grid_minimum` in blocks of at most 2**16 = 65,536 entries, instead of the largest block that `max_bytes` allowed. NumPy's array and scalar evaluations of a function can differ in their last bits, so the stored tables and the computed minimum no longer depend on the byte limit. A table of more than 2**16 entries can differ from v1.0.3.post2 in its last bits, which changes the content id of its Plan, and an explicit `max_bytes` that left room only for smaller blocks now refuses the Plan and states the bytes it needs. The binary model keeps at most 2**30 bytes of optional cached constructions.
- `QHD.max_bytes`, `QHDVerification.max_bytes` and the `max_bytes` of `constrained_grid_minimum` default to `None`, the physical memory of the computer, instead of 10 GB, so the content ids of QHD Plans change. A refusal under the default names the physical memory and the bytes a computer needs. The refinement readout of a kept state uses the dense grid for at most 2**20 grid points and streams larger grids whatever the limits, so the `joint_mass_reads` that a level records is the same on every computer, and a limit that left room only for streaming a small grid now refuses. `AugmentedLagrangianRecord.admitted_bytes_bound` is `None` when `qhd.max_bytes` is `None`.
- A record's content hash covers each nested record by its type and content hash and each `FrozenArray` by the SHA-256 digest of its elements, and a Plan's content hash follows the same rule as other records. Computing the hash of a Plan or of a record that holds arrays no longer encodes the arrays as base64 or serializes the nested records again. QHD planning charges this smaller content-hash workspace (`method._support_table_bytes`), so a QHD Plan near its `max_bytes` can be accepted where v1.0.3.post2 refused it.
- A Pauli input reserves `3*max(2q+120,150)+112` bytes per term for the content hashes of the Plan records that will hold its terms, instead of `6q+360`. A per-term record enters its parent's content hash as a 150-byte reference and keeps its own 112-byte hash, so up to q = 15 the share is 562 bytes, against 366 to 450 bytes before. A Pauli input of q qubits near its `max_bytes` can therefore be refused where v1.0.3.post2 accepted it ([input contracts](development/input_contracts.md)).
- A forecast from `estimate`, `compare`, `scan` or `assess` gives the exact `native state body` memory of a register, and the exact amplitude count and bytes of an amplitude readout, whenever a saved record can hold the integer, that is up to 4300 decimal digits, as for a complex128 state vector of 14,280 qubits. A larger value is recorded as unknown with its reason, and the forecast still reports that such a state body exceeds the memory grant. In v1.0.3.post2 the state body was unknown above `AdmissionLimits.max_integer_bits` (default 4096 bits), and a forecast refused an amplitude readout of more than 4091 qubits and a probability marginal on 4096 or more qubits, alone or as a trajectory point. The content ids of forecasts for registers wider than 4091 qubits change.
- The energy-shift check of Lanczos and FixedGCIM (`EnergyShiftOptions`, `verify_energy_shift`) keeps the operator of each Result in arrays, `EnergyEndpoint.table_values` with `table_keys` where entries need coordinates, and the kind of table in `table`, instead of the tuples `terms` and `matrix_entries`. A dense matrix with at least half of its entries nonzero is kept without coordinates. The exact `H+cI` relation and its refusals are unchanged, except that the imaginary parts of Pauli coefficients must now also agree. For a dense 1024-by-1024 matrix, building the options and checking the relation take about 0.1 s and 0.4 s instead of 1.5 s each, and the saved options take 22.4 MB instead of 25.8 MB. The content ids of `EnergyShiftOptions` change, and options saved by v1.0.3.post2 no longer load. Build them again with `EnergyShiftOptions.for_result` from the reopened baseline Result.

## v1.0.3.post2 (2026-10-25)

Relative to v1.0.3.post1, this release adds QFD, an eigenvalue method in a basis of time-evolved states, and new measurement, shot-allocation and basis-growth options for GCiM. ### QFD

`QFD` (`nwqlib.algorithms.QFD`) estimates the smallest eigenvalue of a Hermitian operator $A$ in the basis $V^{p}|\psi\rangle$ of integer powers $p$ of one fixed product-formula step $V$ applied to a reference state $|\psi\rangle$. It solves the Hamiltonian pencil of Parrish and McMahon, arXiv:1909.08925v1, Eqs. (5)–(8), with the measurement and projected solve of FixedGCIM, by exact readout or with grouped shots. You replace $A$, the reference, the time step and the powers. By default $V$ is built from the Pauli terms of $A$, and `evolution="fermionic_blocks"` builds it from complete number-conserving fermionic blocks of a separate generator. `chemistry_evolution_terms` (in `nwqlib.algorithms.qfd`) builds such a generator from the integrals of `build_gcim_chemistry_problem` data. The [QFD guide](algorithms/qfd.md) describes the workflow, its limits and what the result does not establish.

### GCiM measurement and its defaults

`PencilAcquisition`, passed as `acquisition=` to FixedGCIM, ADAPT and QFD, chooses how $H$ and $S$ are measured ([How matrix elements are obtained](algorithms/gcim.md#how-matrix-elements-are-obtained)). Its defaults change what an existing sampled script measures:

- Sampled ADAPT measures grouped joint states by default (`matrix_measurement="grouped"`), with one setting per qubit-wise commuting group and part, as FixedGCIM does. v1.0.3.post1 measured one Hadamard test per Pauli term and part. `PencilAcquisition(matrix_measurement="termwise", pair_preparation="controlled")` keeps that measurement as a comparison.
- `quadratures="auto"` measures only the real parts of the off-diagonal pairs when a proof from the stored inputs shows that $A$ and every state the method can prepare are real, and records the imaginary parts as exact zeros. Otherwise it measures both parts, which v1.0.3.post1 always did. Real-only measurement does not show that the imaginary parts are zero on a noisy device.
- `pair_preparation="prefix"` runs the blocks that both preparations of a pair begin with once without control and controls only the rest, with the same joint state. v1.0.3.post1 controlled both complete preparations of every pair, which `"controlled"` still does.

For ADAPT, `pair_preparation="vacuum_reference"` and `"vacuum_restore"` control only the reference preparation, when planning can show from the Pauli terms of every pool generator that its exponential maps the all-zero state to itself up to a phase. FixedGCIM and QFD refuse both values in quantum execution unless the Plan measures nothing.

### Shot allocation from a pilot stage

`sampling=PencilSampling(total_shots=..., pilot_fraction=...)` of FixedGCIM, ADAPT and QFD splits a shot budget into a uniform pilot stage and a main stage whose shots per setting the pilot pencil decides. Only the main-stage measurements form the final $H$ and $S$, the pilot shots count toward the total, and `result.shot_allocation` records the decision ([Pilot and main shot allocation](algorithms/gcim.md#pilot-and-main-shot-allocation)). For ADAPT the two stages measure the basis that growth ended with, with a budget separate from the `shots=` of the growth. Without `sampling`, every setting gets `shots` shots, as before.

### ADAPT basis growth

`ADAPT(basis_growth=BoundedRitzGrowth(max_chain_length=L))` grows a basis of generator chains of at most $L$ generators, one chain per round, chosen by a score from the current Ritz pair, and `basis_depth_limit` bounds the CX count of each chain ([Bounded Ritz growth](algorithms/gcim.md#bounded-ritz-growth)). It changes the trial space, so its energies are those of a different projected problem from the default growth. With shots, an extension is scored only when it passes an empirical eligibility rule on the sampled overlaps, and growth stops with `noise_inconclusive` when none of a round's extensions passes. The default growth, `basis_growth=None`, is unchanged.

### Circuit depth on IBM Runtime

`max_native_two_qubit_depth=D` of FixedGCIM, ADAPT and QFD refuses, before its submission, every circuit whose two-qubit depth after compilation for the device exceeds $D$ or is not qualified ([Native two-qubit depth](ibm.md#native-two-qubit-depth)). Only IBM Runtime computes this depth, and it qualifies it only for counts circuits with dynamical decoupling and twirling off, under qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.49.0. With any other installed version a depth limit refuses every IBM circuit, so after an upgrade of qiskit-ibm-runtime the limit is accepted again only once the re-check in [Maintenance](MAINTENANCE.md#dependency-follow-ups) has qualified the new version. The default `None` sets no limit.

### Other changes

### Mathematical and planning changes

- Periodic LCHS uses a Strang error coefficient derived for the order in which its circuit applies the operators. When steps are selected from a synthesis tolerance, pure diffusion on one or two system qubits selects one step with zero ideal product-formula synthesis error. Other error components, including quadrature and execution, remain separate.
- The `relaxed_prefix` Pauli bound uses weighted anticommutation degrees to tighten the previous suffix relaxation. The work and memory it counts against the limits include the additional degree passes and contraction work. A plan close to `max_work` or `max_bytes` can therefore be refused where v1.0.2 accepted it.
- The normalized-expectation error bound changes from `4*w*delta/r` to the sharp `2*w*delta/r`, where `r` is the norm of the computed physical vector, `delta < r` is its complete error budget, and `w` bounds the observable norm. Scaled evaluation avoids intermediate overflow and false zero from underflow. An unrepresentable positive bound is reported as unavailable evidence.
- Jacobi–Anger selection uses a tighter real-argument Bessel remainder and chooses the smallest usable polynomial degree within its finite search horizon. Ties select the smaller computed complete tail, then the earlier terminal. The search examines at most 202 terminals. A lower degree can have a larger reported tail. When the only passing candidates have an analytic remainder that underflows to zero, the polynomial remains available with unknown tail evidence. Phase fitting is unchanged.
- Five default planning limits increase as shown below. Independent byte and execution limits still apply, so passing the planning checks does not establish that a plan can be executed.

| Planning limit | v1.0.2 | v1.0.3 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `AdmissionLimits.max_definitions` | 16,384 | 1,000,000 |
| `FixedGCIM.max_admission_steps` | 1,000,000 | 10,000,000 |
| `LCHS.max_select_work` | 100,000,000 | 1,000,000,000 |
| `LCHS.max_dense_select_slots` | 256 | 4,096 |
| `LCHSRefinement.max_structural_work` | 100,000,000 | 1,000,000,000 |

**Linear dynamics (LCHS)**

- `nwqlib.algorithms.lchs` no longer exports `resolve_hamiltonian_evolution_backend` or `LCHS_HAMILTONIAN_EVOLUTION_IMPLEMENTATIONS`. The `LCHS.hamiltonian_evolution_backend` field chooses the backend of each branch (`"dense_exact"`, `"trotter"`, `"trotter_error_budgeted"` or `"qsp_block_encoding"`), as its description states.
- `LCHSAnalysis` no longer has the `references` field, which was always `"not_run"`. Reference checks are explicit verification calls.
- `LCHSCoefficientPlan.record()` no longer returns `lchs_kernel_quadrature_compatibility` or `lchs_kernel_quadrature_certificate_status`. Read the certificate status from `solution_error_certificate_status`. The compatibility key recorded only that an allowed provider combination was selected and has no replacement.
- `LCHSQuadraturePlan` and `LCHSQuadratureData` no longer have `effective_range_k`, which equaled `range_k` for every built-in quadrature rule, and `lchs_quadrature_summary` no longer returns the matching `effective_K` key. Read `range_k`, or the summary's `K` key.
- `LCHSProductFormulaSelectPlan` no longer has `max_step_count`. Read `repetitions`, which holds the same value.
- `generate_lchs_product_formula_select_plan` takes the quadrature data as `quadrature=` in place of `matrix=` and `_quadrature_plan=`. Build that data with `generate_lchs_quadrature(matrix=..., final_time=..., method=...)`. The function still refuses a dimension that is not a power of two.
- `nwqlib.algorithms.lchs.time_independent_common` no longer exports `complex_to_dict` or `is_power_of_two`. They have no public replacement.
- The LCHS exact-readout work refusal gives the requested work, the limit and the minimum sufficient `LCHS.max_readout_work`, without the clauses about planning units and completed reduction units.

**Optimization (QHD)**

- QHD archives preserve unevaluated SymPy `Sum` expressions through Result loading and refinement or augmented-Lagrangian continuation.
- `solve_augmented_lagrangian`, `plan_augmented_lagrangian`, `refine_box`, `constrained_grid_minimum` and the `ConstrainedQHDResult` constructor no longer raise a dedicated `TypeError` for an argument of the wrong type. Such an argument now fails where the layer first uses it, typically with an `AttributeError` that names the missing attribute. `refine_box` still raises `TypeError` for a problem that is not an `Optimization`.
- The description of `QHDVerification.max_bytes` states that, for observed counts or probabilities without a kept state, the bytes that `grid_minimum` counts against the limit omit histogram construction or loading, decoding and passing-weight storage at every readout width. This limit therefore does not bound the `minimum_success_mass` step.

**Subroutines**

- The undocumented functions `nwqlib.subroutines.lcu.lcu_preparation_implementation_metadata` and `nwqlib.subroutines.block_encoding.block_encoding_implementation_metadata` are removed. Every block encoding records its selected implementation under `BlockEncoding.metadata["implementation_metadata"]`, and every LCU circuit under `LCUCircuit.preparation_metadata["implementation_metadata"]`. The registries `BLOCK_ENCODING_IMPLEMENTATIONS` and `LCU_PREPARATION_IMPLEMENTATIONS` list each selectable implementation's record.
- `coefficient_arithmetic` is removed from `TrotterStepSelection` (its attribute and `to_dict()` key), from QPE reconstructions and from LCHS Trotter node records. NWQLib evaluates the coefficient one way, which the records' descriptions now state. `bound_variant` identifies the enclosed expression, and QPE reconstructions keep the exact coefficient in `bound_coefficient`.
- `nwqlib.subroutines.trotterization.trotter_error_bound` is removed. Call `evaluate_trotter_bound(hamiltonian, time=t, steps=1, order=o)`, which returns the same bound under the same limits.
- Banded block encodings no longer repeat the band offsets as `metadata["band_offsets"]` or set `metadata["toeplitz"]`, which was always `True` and has no replacement. Read the offsets from `metadata["band_specification"]["offsets"]`.
- `resolve_number_projector_lowering` is removed from `nwqlib.subroutines.hamiltonian_evolution.pauli_evolution`. Call `structured_number_projector_provider(support_size)` from the same module, which returns the provider record that the removed function wrapped, with its CX count under `"cx"`. `wrapped_projector_phase` in the same module takes the angle alone and keeps no cache, and `wrapped_phase_workspace_bytes` and `WRAPPED_PHASE_CACHE_ENTRIES` are removed with that cache.
- `nwqlib.subroutines.qsp.evolution` no longer exports `_reflection_about_ancilla_zero`, the reflection `I - 2|0><0|` on the ancilla register. It has no public replacement. `nwqlib.blocks.select_zero_reflection` builds the reflection of the opposite sign, `2|0><0| - I`.

**Backends, device profiles and forecasts**

- Resource forecasts compare by their declared fields, so saving and loading a `PlanEstimate` preserves equality regardless of cached lookups. `PlanEstimate` and `AssessmentContext` also allow the other object's equality comparison when its type differs.
- `IonQBackend` no longer takes `debiasing`. Requests are sent with debiasing disabled, as before.
- `TimeModel` and `TimePrediction` no longer have `form`. The model is the linear model that its coefficients, scope, domain and uncertainty describe.

**Documentation and examples**

- The guides, the API reference and the docstrings are rewritten to describe the behavior of this release, and the API reference renders the records it names.
- The `resource_estimation_at_scale` notebook is regenerated and executed with this release.

Package and citation versions are synchronized at `1.0.2`.

## v1.0.0.post3 (2026-10-02)

Counts-based QHD box refinement and augmented-Lagrangian runs with refinement now include selected region mass lower bounds in `print(result)` and `result.report()`. The default 95% confidence covers the configured run, and the mass is conditional on valid decoding of the level's backend-sampled distribution. The report compares Hoeffding and one-sided Clopper–Pearson bounds after allocating half the failure budget to each method.

`BoxRefinementResult.report()` now defaults to `failure_probability=0.05`, and `ConstrainedQHDResult.report()` accepts the same parameter. Pass `None` to omit the statistical report. Each completed level records `region_axis_counts` and `region_count`. The outer archive formats are `qhd.refinement/4` and `qhd.constrained/4`, and the formats of the outer record of a run directory (`controller.json`) are `qhd.refinement_run/6` and `qhd.constrained_run/5`. Earlier outer formats cannot be loaded or resumed by these readers.

## v1.0.0 (2026-09-30)

NWQLib applies quantum algorithms to scientific inputs and returns the computed quantities with their methods, resources and evidence. This is the first public release. The pages linked below hold the details.

**Algorithms**

- Linear systems: [QLS](algorithms/qls.md) with the QSVT inverse polynomial and the Dalzell kernel shortcut.
- Linear dynamics: [LCHS](algorithms/lchs.md) with the An–Childs–Lin, Cauchy and Low–Somma kernels on product-formula and QSP block-encoding routes.
- Eigenvalues and phases: [Chebyshev Lanczos](algorithms/lanczos.md), [fixed and adaptive GCiM](algorithms/gcim.md), and [QPE](algorithms/qpe.md) with QCELS, RWPE, SPE and RFE.
- Expectation values of finite Pauli sums: [Expectation](algorithms/expectation.md).
- Optimization over a box: [QHD](algorithms/qhd.md) in one-hot and binary encodings, with box refinement and an augmented-Lagrangian route for equality and inequality constraints.

**Subroutines** ([API](api/index.md)): block encodings, LCU, QSP and QSVT phase selection, coherent QPE, Hamiltonian evolution, Trotterization with error budgets and Pauli commutation tables, state preparation including matrix product states, fermionic pools, and Pauli decomposition.

**End to end**

- One workflow for every method: `plan` selects the construction, `estimate` and `compare` give its resource counts before any circuit is built, `solve` runs it, and `prepare` and `submit` run it as a Run that can be saved, reopened and continued ([Plan, compare and solve](scientist.md), [Run on a backend](prepared_execution.md), [Continue an interrupted run](run_archives.md)).
- Resource estimates come from the gate-count formulas of the selected construction, are checked against compiled circuits at small size, and extend to sizes no simulator holds ([Estimate resources](resources.md)).
- Every limit on work, memory and stored data is checked before the work starts, and a refusal names the parameter and the amount ([Supply inputs](inputs.md)).
- Results carry their error evidence and references. Saved Results and Runs reload without planning or measuring again, a reopened Run can continue its unfinished work, and `result.analyze` reanalyzes the saved observations with new settings without new measurements ([Save, load and reanalyze results](saved_evidence.md), [Check accuracy and verify a result](verification.md)).
- Backends: local Aer, NWQ-Sim with Slurm submission, IBM Runtime, IonQ, Quantinuum Nexus, and QASM export ([Choose a backend](backends.md)). A command line lists the methods and prints their cards ([Use the command line](cli.md)).

**Documentation**: a [quickstart](quickstart.md), the user guides, one guide per algorithm with its source map from code to paper equations, the [mathematics page](mathematics.md) with the proofs and resource formulas, the backend guides, the [API reference](api/index.md), and the development pages from the [contribution policy](FRAMEWORK.md) to the [code tour](CODE_TOUR.md) and the [engineering constants](ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md).

**Examples** ([overview](examples.md)): eight executed notebooks. Four introductions cover a linear system, linear dynamics, eigenvalues and optimization. Three paper-derived studies cover collision relaxation with QLS, heat flow with a boundary penalty with LCHS, and constrained optimization with QHD. One notebook plans five problems at 80 to 100 qubits and estimates their resources without simulating a circuit.
