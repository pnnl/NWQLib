# Dependency issues

This page lists defects and version-specific behavior of NWQLib's dependencies that change results or constrain installation, and what NWQLib does about each. The stable environment in [`docs/ENVIRONMENT_LOCK.txt`](https://github.com/pnnl/NWQLib/blob/main/docs/ENVIRONMENT_LOCK.txt) uses Qiskit 2.6.2, qiskit-aer 0.18.2, PySCF 2.14.0, SciPy 1.18.1 and urllib3 2.8.0. Each entry names the versions its evidence covers. The NWQ-Sim revision is `efd0226` or a descendant with the same roundoff source files, such as `202f5ca` ([NWQ-Sim](nwqsim.md)).

## Qiskit 2.6.0

### Two-qubit synthesis replaces Weyl coordinates

`TwoQubitBasisDecomposer` always builds its `TwoQubitWeylDecomposition` with fidelity `1 - 1e-9`, whether or not `approximate=False` is passed (Rust sources of the 2.6.0 tag, `crates/synthesis/src/two_qubit_decompose/basis_decomposer.rs`, `call_inner`, and `common.rs`, `DEFAULT_FIDELITY`). The decomposition then replaces the Weyl coordinates by those of the first special class, starting with the identity class, whose average gate fidelity reaches that value (`weyl_decomposition.rs`, `new_inner`). Average gate fidelity is quadratic in the dropped coordinates, so coordinates up to about 3.5e-5 can disappear together with the entangling part of the gate. For `U = exp(-i t G)`, with `G` the Hermitian part of a 4-by-4 matrix whose entries have standard normal real and imaginary parts, the definition of `UnitaryGate(U)` had entry errors between 0.5 t and 3.0 t for `t` up to 1e-5 over 60 generators, and at most 4.2e-5 for larger `t` (Qiskit 2.5.2). A coordinate just above the identity threshold can go to another special class, so `exp(i a XX)` lost its entangling part for `a` up to 3.5e-5 and still had entry error `a` at `a = 4.2e-5`. Qiskit uses this decomposer for two-qubit `UnitaryGate` definitions and for the two-qubit leaves of its Shannon decomposition.

NWQLib synthesizes dense unitaries with `dense_unitary_circuit` in `subroutines/_dense_synthesis.py`, which computes the KAK decomposition without that replacement. Only deviations within `ROUNDING_WINDOW` (2^-46) are rounded to a special value. [Exact dense synthesis](development/dense_synthesis.md#controlled-dense-unitaries) lists the constructions that use it: LCHS `dense_exact` branches, dense block encodings in QLS and QSP, dense LCU SELECT, QPE dense powers, coherent QPE and the MPS preparation. `exact_dense_unitaries` in `subroutines/qiskit_compat.py` applies it before compilation for NWQ-Sim, IonQ with its QIS gateset and IBM Runtime at optimization levels 0 and 1, for Nexus, and for Aer when a noise model's gate basis omits the unitary instruction. Noiseless Aer, and Aer whose noise basis includes it, apply the matrix directly. At optimization levels 2 and 3, NWQ-Sim, IBM Runtime and IonQ resynthesize two-qubit blocks with Qiskit's own synthesis, so their circuits carry the synthesis errors above ([optimization level](backends.md#optimization-level)). Recheck with `tests/test_dense_synthesis.py`, which bounds the entry error of the synthesis by 64 unit roundoffs times the dimension for `t` from 1e-10 to 10.

### The Shannon decomposition omits small rotations

Qiskit's quantum Shannon decomposition drops multiplexed RZ rotations of at most 1e-10 rad and treats a block within 1e-12 of the identity as the identity (`crates/synthesis/src/qsd.rs`, `get_ucrz` and `quantum_shannon_decomposition`). NWQLib's block-ZXZ synthesis in `subroutines/_dense_synthesis.py` keeps every nonzero multiplexed rotation, and `tests/test_dense_synthesis.py` covers it with the same sweep.

### Controlled `UnitaryGate`

`UnitaryGate.control` (`qiskit/circuit/library/generalized_gates/unitary.py`) decomposes the whole controlled matrix with `qs_decomposition` and keeps the result when `matrix_equal` holds with `atol=1e-7` and the default relative tolerance 1e-5. Otherwise it uses `Isometry`. `UnitaryGate._define` does the same for an uncontrolled gate on three or more qubits. The check accepts entry errors of about `1e-7 + 1e-5 |C_ij|` for entry `C_ij` of the controlled matrix, and a sweep of X, Y and Z rotations by angles from 1e-8 to 1e-2 rad with one to three controls measured errors up to 7.9e-6 (Qiskit 2.5.2). The fallback raised `ValueError("Input matrix is not unitary.")` for RX(1e-7) with three controls, which is a valid input.

`controlled` in `subroutines/qiskit_compat.py` builds a `ControlledGate` whose definition is `controlled_unitary_circuit`, the exact synthesis of the closed-control matrix. It uses no check and no fallback. For generic inputs its CX count equals Qiskit's or is lower. For some inputs with one system qubit and two controls, Qiskit 2.5.2 took 8 CX where the exact synthesis took 10. Every count stays within the formula in [Exact dense synthesis](development/dense_synthesis.md#controlled-dense-unitaries). The base gate stays the `UnitaryGate`, so `inverse()` of the result still goes through Qiskit's `UnitaryGate.control`, and `inverse_realized_gate` in the same module reverses the exact definition instead. `tests/test_dense_synthesis.py` includes the three-control RX(1e-7) case.

The cost of Qiskit's path lies mostly in its check, not in its decomposition. For a random 512 × 512 unitary with one control, measured with Qiskit 2.5.2 on macOS arm64 with one thread, Qiskit's `qs_decomposition` of the 1024 × 1024 controlled matrix took 4.9 s and gave 272,044 CX in 751,616 instructions. `controlled_unitary_circuit` took 5.3 s and gave the same 272,044 CX in 718,848 instructions. In a separate run, `UnitaryGate.control` did not finish within 120 s, because its check forms the operator of the whole definition, a few M² multiply-adds for each of its roughly 750,000 instructions with M = 1024. The two decompositions scale alike, as M³ in classical work and as M² in CX. NWQLib relies on the exactness of each factorization, checked by `tests/test_dense_synthesis.py` at small sizes, and forms no operator on each call.

### The `UnitaryGate` constructor

`UnitaryGate(U)` checks that `U^dagger U` equals the identity within `atol=1e-8` and `rtol=1e-5` (`qiskit/quantum_info/operators/predicates.py`). A power of a unitary that NWQLib accepted accumulates the input's defect, so the constructor rejected the higher powers of a unitary with defect 8e-9. QPE checks the unitarity of a supplied matrix once when it plans the method and builds the dense and coherent QPE powers with `check_input=False`. `test_coherent_qpe_accepts_powers_of_an_admitted_near_unitary_input` in `tests/test_qpe_subroutines.py` builds the powers of such an input up to the eighth.

### Instructions of a controlled gate

When `qiskit_compat.controlled` controls a composite gate, Qiskit's `add_control` (`qiskit/circuit/_add_control.py`, `apply_basic_controlled_gate`) unrolls it and replaces each gate by a multi-controlled one. With k >= 2 controls a U gate becomes two multi-controlled RZ, a multi-controlled RY and an MCPhase, and each multi-controlled RZ is the circuit of Qiskit's `_mcsu2_real_diagonal`, composed inline. Its dirty-ancilla X blocks are synthesized in Rust (`synth_mcx_n_dirty_i15`), and their size has no published closed form. `CONTROLLED_U_INSTRUCTIONS` and `CONTROLLED_RZ_INSTRUCTIONS` in `subroutines/_dense_synthesis.py` store the number of instructions that Qiskit 2.6.0 emits for 1 to 64 controls. `gatewise_control_counts` reads them for the gate-wise control formula ([exact dense synthesis](development/dense_synthesis.md#admission-of-the-exact-synthesis)). The gate-wise route of the control-route option (`DenseExact.control_route` of LCHS, `dense_control_route` elsewhere) depends on these counts for each dense unitary it controls. The whole-matrix route synthesizes the controlled matrix with `controlled_unitary_circuit` instead, and the counts still give the cost of a later gate-wise control of that circuit, such as the parity control of a QSP pass ([exact dense synthesis](development/dense_synthesis.md#dense-control-route)). `test_gatewise_control_tables_match_installed_qiskit` in `tests/test_synthesis_admission.py` recomputes every entry and fail when the installed Qiskit gives other counts.

### Sign of the unspecialized two-qubit circuit

`TwoQubitWeylDecomposition(U, fidelity=None).circuit()` adds the global phase of the wrong one-qubit factor (`weyl_decomposition.rs`, where the sum uses `c2r.global_phase` in place of `c1r.global_phase`). It returned `-U` for 45 of 100 random two-qubit unitaries, and for near-identity inputs. NWQLib does not call this method.

### QPY and `UCGate`

QPY in Qiskit 2.6.0 writes a Qiskit `UCGate` but cannot load it, because it passes the gate's matrix table as separate positional constructor arguments, whereas `UCGate` expects one list. This is a constructor reconstruction defect, not evidence of a reversed qubit order.

NWQLib saves such a gate as a compact QPY instruction carrying its matrix table and explicit reconstruction fields, in a versioned container whose file prefix is `NWQLIB-QPY-UC1`. The reader restores the nominal number of qubits, the existing simplified table, the active controls, `up_to_diagonal`, the label and any definition already built. Circuit phase and wire associations remain attached to their original circuit. Under Qiskit's convention, the target is wire 0 and simplified control indices are one-based within the gate, from 1 through `num_qubits - 1`. Loading does not rerun multiplexor simplification or expand the table to all nominal controls, and saving does not synthesize a UCGate or construct its full matrix.

Load these `.qpy` entries with NWQLib's loaders `load_run` and `load_result` ([Circuit files](run_archives.md#qiskit-ucgate-compatibility)). [Circuit entries and Qiskit UCGate compatibility](development/execution.md#qiskit-ucgate-compatibility) describes the container. The prefix makes them containers rather than standalone input for `qiskit.qpy.load`. Circuit files without this adaptation remain ordinary QPY. An old raw QPY entry affected by the defect still reports its original Qiskit failure with the entry path, because the loader cannot infer missing reconstruction fields from it.

`test_native_ucgate_qpy_limitation_canary` in `tests/test_qpy_archive.py` exercises raw QPY on one two-qubit UCGate, with no simulation or synthesis, and fails when loading with `qiskit.qpy.load` starts to succeed or its failure changes. A Qiskit fix does not by itself end support for existing `UC1` files. The `UC1` storage is not switched off automatically on the basis of a Qiskit version number.

### Multi-controlled X counts

Without ancillas, Qiskit synthesizes a multi-controlled X gate with up to four controls by its dedicated CX, Toffoli, C3X and C4X circuits, with 5 to 32 controls by the construction of da Silva and Park ([Phys. Rev. A 106, 042602](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.106.042602)), with `4k**2 - 4k + 2` CX for k controls, and a larger one by that of Huang and Palsberg ([PLDI 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3656436)), whose CX count has no published closed form. `MCX_CX_BY_CONTROLS` in `subroutines/_mcx_counts.py` stores the counts measured with Qiskit 2.6.0 for 1 to 64 controls, and the resource formulas of QLS and LCHS read them. `test_multi_controlled_x_cx_table_matches_installed_qiskit_synthesis` in `tests/test_mcx_counts.py` synthesizes the gates again and fails when the installed Qiskit gives other counts.

### Standard-gate instructions

`dense_unitary_circuit` appends up to 11 M²/8 gates for an M-square unitary. `QuantumCircuit.u`, `cx`, `rz` and `h` check and broadcast their arguments for every gate in `QuantumCircuit._append_standard_gate`, which took 0.27 s for the 179,200 gates of a 512 × 512 controlled matrix (Qiskit 2.5.2, macOS arm64, one thread). `_dense_synthesis._standard_gate_appender` builds each instruction with `CircuitInstruction.from_standard`, as `_append_standard_gate` does, and adds it with `QuantumCircuit._append`, which Qiskit documents as a fast path for callers that have checked their arguments. The same gates took 0.055 s. `blocks._qiskit_intake.snapshot_circuit` copies each stored standard gate of a supplied circuit in the same way, so that the copy reads no Python gate object that the caller may still hold, and `tests/test_encoding_selection.py` compares that copy with one made through Python gate objects. `from_standard` is undocumented, and its first argument is a member of `StandardGate`, which the private module `qiskit._accelerate.circuit` defines. `qiskit.circuit.quantumcircuit` also imports it, but neither that module nor `qiskit.circuit` lists it in `__all__`, and the Qiskit 2.6.0 API reference documents no public location for it. The exactness tests in `tests/test_dense_synthesis.py` compare each synthesized circuit with its target matrix, so they fail when either interface changes. A missing name stops every synthesis, and a changed meaning of the parameters or qubits of an appended gate changes the operator of the circuit.

## SciPy 1.18.1

### Triangular matrix exponential

`scipy.linalg.expm` is implemented in `scipy/linalg/src/_matfuncs_expm.c`. For a triangular input that needs squarings, `matrix_exponential_z` and its real and single-precision variants recompute the diagonal and the first superdiagonal after each squaring, as Code Fragment 2.1 of Al-Mohy and Higham (2009), doi:10.1137/09074721X, prescribes ([References](references.md#linear-combination-of-hamiltonian-simulation-lchs)). They evaluate the superdiagonal entry as `t (exp(l2) - exp(l1))/(l2 - l1)` and switch to `t exp(l1)` only when the two diagonal entries are exactly equal. When the entries are close but distinct, the difference cancels, and the relative error grows like the unit roundoff divided by their distance. For the augmented matrix `[[-A*T, b*T], [0, 0]]` of the LCHS closed form with `A = diag(40, s)`, `T = 1`, `u0 = (0.5, i)` and `b = (0.2, -0.1+0.3i)`, the relative error of the solution against a 60-digit mpmath evaluation was 3.9e-12, 2.6e-10, 5.4e-6 and 2.7e-2 at s = 1e-6, 1e-8, 1e-12 and 1e-16. The same spectrum rotated to a matrix that is not triangular gave at most 1.5e-15, and `A = [[40, 1], [0, s]]` behaved like the diagonal case. The homogeneous exponential of the upper triangular `-[[40, 1], [0, 40 + 1e-10]]` had a relative entry error of 4.5e-7.

`scipy.sparse.linalg.expm` evaluates that superdiagonal with `_eq_10_42` in `scipy/sparse/linalg/_matfuncs.py`, Eq. (10.42) of Higham's *Functions of Matrices*, doi:10.1137/1.9780898717778, which does not cancel. On upper and lower triangular inputs with diagonal spreads up to 4000 and gaps down to 1e-16, its solution error stayed at or below 6.5e-16. On stiff inputs that are not triangular it is less accurate, because `_solve_P_Q` forms the Padé quotient as `(V - U)^{-1}(V + U)` where the C implementation forms `I + 2 (V - U)^{-1} U`, and the difference grows with the number of squarings. At `||A|| T` of about 1e4 its closed-form error was 2.3e-13, against 3.1e-16 for `scipy.linalg.expm`.

The LCHS `expm` and `closed_form` references therefore evaluate a triangular matrix with `scipy.sparse.linalg.expm`, a lower triangular one through its transpose, and every other matrix with `scipy.linalg.expm` (`_exponential` in `algorithms/lchs/references.py`). The call runs inside the function that seeds and restores NumPy's global random state, described [below](#random-draws-of-the-norm-estimates). `test_closed_form_is_accurate_for_close_diagonal_entries_and_singular_a` in `tests/test_lchs_verification.py` fails when a triangular input reaches `scipy.linalg.expm`. The other `scipy.linalg.expm` calls in NWQLib exponentiate a skew-Hermitian matrix, the LCHS branch unitary `exp(-i t (k L + H))`, or a real antisymmetric one, a fermionic generator block. Such a matrix is diagonal when it is triangular, and the C implementation exponentiates a diagonal matrix entry by entry without squaring.

### Random draws of the norm estimates

`onenormest` in `scipy/sparse/linalg/_onenormest.py` draws the +-1 starting columns of its 1-norm estimate and their resamples with `np.random.randint`, from NumPy's legacy global generator. `scipy.sparse.linalg.expm` calls it from dimension 200, and `expm_multiply` for a shifted 1-norm above 63.36, condition (3.13) of Al-Mohy and Higham (2011), doi:10.1137/100788860. A call therefore advances the caller's random stream, and the estimated norms, which choose the Taylor or Padé parameters, depend on the caller's state. `_linalg_laws.seeded_norm_estimates` seeds the generator for the call and restores the caller's state afterwards, so the same input gives the same draws. The LCHS references and the classical QHD evolutions, including their fidelity references, make these calls inside it. `test_classical_host_evolution_keeps_the_global_random_state` in `tests/test_qhd_workflow.py` and `test_triangular_reference_exponential_keeps_the_global_random_state` in `tests/test_lchs_verification.py` check both properties. `scipy.linalg.expm` estimates its norms with LAPACK `zlacn2`, which draws nothing.

### Overflowing norms in the matrix exponential

Both implementations choose the Padé degree and the squaring count from 1-norms of `M**k` for k up to 10 and of `|M|**k` for k up to 27, formed before scaling. When one of them overflows, `pick_pade_structure_z` in the C implementation converts the infinite or NaN value to its integer squaring count, a conversion that C leaves undefined. On macOS arm64, 3 x 3 inputs did not return within 20 s at 1-norm `2**39` (a nilpotent block `2**38 [[1, -1], [1, -1]]` with a source column) and at 1-norm 1.4e41 (a dissipative matrix), although both exponentials are representable in binary64. `scipy.sparse.linalg.expm` raises `OverflowError` or `ValueError` on the same inputs. The bound `||M||_1 <= 2**37` keeps every one of those norms below `2**999`, and the LCHS references report a matrix above it as unknown before calling either implementation. `fermionic_circuits.build_generator_circuit` refuses an angle whose product with one of its shared-index occupation blocks has a 1-norm above the bound.

## qiskit-aer 0.18.0

### Roundoff of exact readouts

An exact Aer readout, computed without shot sampling (`shots=None`), has a probability window, a first-order bound on how far binary64 roundoff can move its total probability from one. The window uses a per-instruction constant derived from the fusion pass, kernels and gate matrices of qiskit-aer (the comment above the constants in `_validation.py`). The derivation was read against 0.17.2, and the statevector, fusion and gate-matrix sources of 0.18.0 and 0.18.2 are identical to those of 0.17.2. Aer's statevector method fuses only circuits wider than its default `fusion_threshold` of 14 qubits. A 14-qubit circuit reported no fusion, and a 15-qubit circuit did. Another installed Aer version adds "unchecked qiskit-aer version" to the preparation record's `probability_window_exclusions` (`_ROUNDOFF_CHECKED_AER_VERSIONS` in `backends/qiskit_aer.py`, [Aer](aer.md)).

### OpenMP runtime with PySCF on Linux

On Linux x86-64, the wheels of qiskit-aer 0.18.0 and PySCF 2.14.0 each bundle their own build of the GNU OpenMP runtime, `qiskit_aer.libs/libgomp-6a29c32d.so.1.0.0` and `pyscf/lib/libgomp-a34b3233.so.1.0.0`. Both builds use static thread-local storage. A library loaded after the process starts takes that storage from a reserve that the C library sets at startup. Qiskit 2.6.0 fills much of that reserve, because its extension module requires static thread-local storage ([Qiskit issue 17213](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit/issues/17213)), and Qiskit 2.6.1 builds the module without that requirement ([Qiskit pull request 17253](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit/pull/17253)).

With Qiskit 2.6.0 on the Ubuntu 24.04 hosts of NWQLib's CI, the reserve did not hold both builds. A Python process there that used both packages failed when it loaded the second build, with "cannot allocate memory in static TLS block", and a process on another Linux system can fail the same way. If qiskit-aer comes second, importing `qiskit_aer` raises `ImportError`. If PySCF comes second, importing `pyscf` raises `AttributeError` naming `pyscf/lib/libnp_helper.so`, after an `OSError` with that message. The eigenvalue notebook (`examples/gcim_lanczos_qpe_eigenvalue_intro.ipynb`) loads both packages in one kernel, and so does a test process that runs Aer and chemistry tests. The CI failed this way with Python 3.12.14 and 3.15.1. With qiskit-aer 0.17.2, PySCF 2.14.0 and Python 3.12.14, the CI ran the Aer and chemistry tests and the eigenvalue notebook without this error.

A library named in `LD_PRELOAD` is loaded when the process starts and does not draw on that reserve, so the variable has to be set before Python starts. Activate the environment that has NWQLib and PySCF installed, so that `python` below is its interpreter, and set the variable to the OpenMP runtime of PySCF in the shell that then starts Python or Jupyter:

```bash
export LD_PRELOAD="$(python -c 'from importlib.util import find_spec; from pathlib import Path; (path,) = Path(find_spec("pyscf").submodule_search_locations[0], "lib").glob("libgomp*.so*"); print(path)')"
```

A Jupyter kernel inherits the variable from the Jupyter server, so start or restart Jupyter from that shell. The Full CI and Mutation Probes workflows set the same variable. On macOS arm64, in the validated environment with Python 3.12.14, the full test suite, which executes the example notebooks, passes without it.

The preload is not enough on every host. On the GitHub Actions image of Ubuntu 26.04 (version 20261101.186.1) with Qiskit 2.6.0, qiskit-aer 0.18.0, PySCF 2.14.0 and Python 3.12.14, importing `qiskit_aer` raises the same `ImportError` with the OpenMP runtime of PySCF preloaded. On that image, with the same preload and with Qiskit 2.6.2, qiskit-aer 0.18.2, PySCF 2.15.0 and Python 3.15.2, the test suite without the notebooks reports no such error. On a host where the preload is not enough, install Qiskit 2.6.1 or later.

## NWQEC 0.1.2

In NWQEC 0.1.2 (tag `v0.1.2`, commit `d93299c2a0fe47fb7758bff02b456acfb3ac4416`), `RemoveTrivialRzPass` removes Rz angles within a fixed `1e-4` of trivial values, angle grouping keeps four significant digits, and GridSynth receives each angle as `std::to_string(angle)`. These steps lie outside the tolerance requested through `rz_err` and `epsilon`. Compiling H followed by `Rz(pi/2 + 5e-5)` through `compile_logical` with `rz_err="total"` and `epsilon=1e-8` gave H followed by S, with no T gate and a phase-aligned operator error of `2.5e-5`, about 2500 times the request (Qiskit 2.5.2, macOS arm64). `LogicalCompilation` therefore records the epsilon as requested and states that it is not a verified achieved precision, and the QHD error sources keep the achieved synthesis and compiler errors unavailable ([QHD guide](algorithms/qhd.md#fault-tolerant-resources)).

Repeated compiles of one circuit give different Clifford counts. The one-step QHD circuit of the [QHD guide](algorithms/qhd.md#fault-tolerant-resources), on `(x - 1/5)**2` over `[-1, 1]` with three grid points, compiled with `rz_err="total"` and `epsilon=1e-4` from the same input QASM digest, gave 347 or 355 H gates, 168 to 210 S gates and 6 or 7 X gates in four compiles, two in each of two processes, while the 347 T and 1 T-inverse gates and the 10 CX gates stayed the same (Qiskit 2.5.2, macOS arm64). The QHD documentation quotes only the T count of a compiled circuit. A statement about a compiled Clifford count needs the counts of repeated compiles.

## NWQ-Sim

At revision `b35763d`, the CPU constructors increment an uninitialized memory counter, and the U matrix factory can overflow the sum of finite phases. `backends/nwqsim.py` rejects that revision before preparing any circuit for it. Upstream revision `efd0226` fixes both, and the roundoff constant of NWQ-Sim readouts was derived for it. The preparation record of a runner whose revision does not descend from `efd0226` lists "unchecked NWQ-Sim revision" in `probability_window_exclusions`, and a descendant that changed any file the derivation reads lists "changed NWQ-Sim roundoff sources". Those files are the value type and fusion switch (`include/config.hpp`, `include/nwq_util.hpp`), the circuit's gate records (`include/circuit.hpp`, `include/private/sim_gate.hpp`), the fusion pass, the U/CX matrices and the CPU/SV kernels (`_ROUNDOFF_SOURCES` and `_ROUNDOFF_BASE_REVISION` in `backends/nwqsim.py`). The NVGPU_MPI route stays blocked, because its constructors increment an uninitialized `gpu_mem` at both revisions ([NWQ-Sim](nwqsim.md)).

## urllib3

The IonQ response reader relies on `HTTPResponse.read(amt)` returning at most `amt` decoded bytes, which holds from urllib3 2. Versions 2.6.0 through 2.8.0 fix streaming-reader security issues: decompression into the library buffer on small reads (GHSA-2xpw-w6gg-jr37), a bypass of that fix, and unbounded chunk-size lines in chunked responses. The `ionq` extra therefore requires urllib3 2.8.0 or later ([IonQ](ionq.md), [Install and first result](quickstart.md#install)).

## qiskit-ibm-runtime

NWQLib submits IBM jobs through the client-side `SamplerV2` and `EstimatorV2`, which qiskit-ibm-runtime exports at the top level of the package from version 0.52.0, and its `ibm` extra requires 0.52.0 or later. These primitives take the option classes of `qiskit_ibm_runtime.options_models`. They build a job on the user's computer and send it to IBM's `executor` program, and they process its result on that computer, the Estimator with qiskit-mitigation ([IBM Runtime](ibm.md)).

NWQLib's error budget, the error bounds and checks that it reports as [error evidence](glossary.md#error-evidence), covers only NWQLib's own computation. It excludes what the IBM SDK and service do to a job, such as error mitigation, twirling, the processing of observables and shots and the uncertainties they report, and it excludes hardware error. NWQLib saves the options of each job with its preparation record, keeps the returned counts and estimates without correcting them and saves with each estimate the job metadata that the SDK returns. It records the version of qiskit-ibm-runtime with each preparation and, for an estimate, the version of qiskit-mitigation. It neither corrects nor bounds the provider's processing, and it does not check that processing again for each SDK release. The behavior below changes reported values and is listed with the versions in which it was read and run.

### Client-side primitives

The client-side `EstimatorV2` and `SamplerV2` of qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.52.0 prepare a job on the user's computer and process its results there, and the Estimator does both with qiskit-mitigation 0.2.0. Four of these steps change the values that the primitives report. The descriptions below come from the sources of these two packages and of Qiskit 2.6.0. The examples ran without contacting IBM, in the local mode of qiskit-ibm-runtime, which simulates the job with qiskit-aer 0.18.0, except the shot count for 1000 Pauli terms, which was read from the job that `EstimatorV2.run` prepared for submission. NWQLib submits through these primitives, so its estimates go through the Estimator's steps and its twirled counts through the Sampler's.

**Small observable terms (Estimator).** While the client-side Estimator prepares a job, Qiskit's `EstimatorPub.coerce` converts the observables of each PUB into an `ObservablesArray`, unless they already form one (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/executor_estimator/prepare.py`, line 89). qiskit-mitigation then converts the observables again with `ObservablesArray(observables)` and default arguments (`qiskit_mitigation/mitigation_task.py`, line 500), also when they arrive as an `ObservablesArray` built with `validate=False`. With its default `validate=True`, Qiskit's `ObservablesArray` passes each observable through `coerce_observable`, which calls `SparseObservable.simplify()` with its default tolerance of 1e-8 (`qiskit/primitives/containers/observables_array.py`, line 280). This removes every term whose coefficient, after equal Pauli terms are summed, is at most 1e-8 in absolute value, so a term with coefficient 1e-8 was removed and one with 1.0000001e-8 was kept. When every term is removed, `EstimatorV2.run` raises `ValueError: Empty observable was detected.` before the job is sent. The client-side Estimator has no option that changes this tolerance. NWQLib passes every nonzero coefficient of the observable to `EstimatorV2.run` (`_mapped_observable` in `backends/ibm_runtime.py`), and the terms that the Estimator then removes lie outside NWQLib's error budget. When it removes every term, the submission stays uncertain, as for any request that the SDK refuses while it builds the job ([IBM Runtime](ibm.md#ibm-runtime-prepared-execution)).

**Precision and shots (Estimator).** The Estimator turns the precision `p` of a job into `ceil(1/p**2)` shots for each measurement configuration (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/executor_estimator/prepare.py`, line 98), whatever the coefficients and the number of terms in the configuration. A measurement configuration is a group of qubit-wise commuting Pauli terms measured together (`qiskit_mitigation/mitigation_task.py`, line 431), so the total number of shots grows with the number of configurations. For one draw of 1000 random Pauli terms on 20 qubits at precision 0.05 and resilience level 0, preparation formed 968 configurations of 400 shots each, 387,200 shots in total. With twirling, a configuration gets the number of randomizations times the shots per randomization (the same `prepare.py`, lines 232 to 239), which the split below sets. NWQLib passes the precision of the Plan and leaves the shots to the Estimator, so `ExecutionLimits.max_total_shots` does not count them.

**Uncertainty fields (Estimator).** Without zero-noise extrapolation, as at resilience levels 0 and 1, the Estimator returns `evs`, `stds` and `ensemble_standard_error`. With zero-noise extrapolation, which resilience level 2 turns on, it returns `evs` and `stds` without `ensemble_standard_error`, and that `stds` comes from the extrapolation (`qiskit_mitigation/zne/gate_folding.py`, line 1082). Without it, qiskit-mitigation computes the two uncertainties term by term (`qiskit_mitigation/mitigation_task.py`, lines 1001 to 1010, and `qiskit_mitigation/utils/expectation_values.py`, lines 718 to 741), and qiskit-ibm-runtime renames qiskit-mitigation's `stds` to `ensemble_standard_error` and its `twirl_stds` to `stds` (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/decoders/executor_estimator/post_processor_v0_1.py`, lines 171 to 224). With probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) off, for an observable with Pauli terms `P_j` and coefficients `c_j`, measured with `N` shots in each measurement configuration, in `R` randomizations of `N/R` shots when twirling is on, the code computes

```text
ensemble_standard_error = sqrt(sum_j c_j**2 * v_j / N)
stds                    = sqrt(sum_j c_j**2 * w_j / R)    (R > 1 randomizations)
```

where `N` is the number of shots run in a configuration, which with twirling can differ from the `ceil(1/p**2)` shots that the precision `p` requests, `v_j` is the variance of the ±1 values of `P_j` over these `N` shots and `w_j` is the variance of the `R` means of `P_j`, one for each twirling randomization, both with `ddof=0`. With measurement mitigation, each `c_j` is multiplied by the readout-correction factor of its term. The sums contain no covariance between terms measured in the same shots. With one randomization, as without twirling, `stds` equals `ensemble_standard_error`. Resilience level 1 turns on measurement twirling, and the automatic split described under the Sampler below gives two randomizations of 64 shots for a precision of 0.1, so `N` is 128 while the precision requests 100. For a Bell state and the observable ZZ + 0.5 XX at precision 0.1 and resilience level 1, on the simulated device `FakeManilaV2` with the simulator seed 5 (`simulator={"seed_simulator": 5}`), `stds` was 0.0061 and `ensemble_standard_error` 0.0531, and both values change with the seed.

NWQLib keeps `stds` and `ensemble_standard_error` of each estimate as reported, in `EstimateValue.provider_metadata_json`, and does not check their statistical interpretation. `EstimateValue.standard_error` holds `stds` only when NWQLib knows that zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) was off. It reads this from `resilience.zne_mitigation` at the top level of the job metadata and otherwise from the request, where `resilience.zne_mitigation` set to `false`, or resilience level 0 without that option, turns ZNE off. The client-side Estimator of qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.52.0 reports its options under `options` in the job metadata, so with it the request decides. In every other case `standard_error` is `None`, and `uncertainty_unavailable` gives the reason.

**Twirled shot count (Sampler).** With gate or measurement twirling turned on, the Sampler runs `num_randomizations` randomizations of `shots_per_randomization` shots each and returns all `num_randomizations * shots_per_randomization` shots (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/executor_sampler/prepare.py`, lines 179 and 202, and `qiskit_ibm_runtime/decoders/executor_sampler/utils.py`, lines 51 to 77). The two numbers come from the requested shots (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/executor/calculate_twirling_shots.py`, lines 41 to 58):

- Both `"auto"`, the default: `shots_per_randomization = max(64, ceil(shots/32))` and `num_randomizations = ceil(shots/shots_per_randomization)`. A request for 1000 shots returned 1024 (16 × 64), and one for 2000 returned 2048 (32 × 64).
- Only `num_randomizations` set to `"auto"`: `num_randomizations = ceil(shots/shots_per_randomization)`.
- Only `shots_per_randomization` set to `"auto"`: `shots_per_randomization = ceil(shots/num_randomizations)`.
- Both given as numbers: the Sampler uses them as given, so 3 randomizations of 100 shots returned 300 shots for a request of 1000.

Without twirling, the same requests returned 1000 and 2000 shots. The Sampler's `default_shots`, 4096 for the client-side Sampler (`qiskit_ibm_runtime/options_models/sampler.py`, line 28), applies only to a request that gives no shot count. For counts, NWQLib writes `twirling.enable_gates` and `twirling.enable_measure` as an explicit `false` unless `options_json` sets them to `true` (`_options` in `backends/ibm_runtime.py`). With either set to `true`, preparation requires `num_randomizations` and `shots_per_randomization` as integers whose product is the requested shots, so the Sampler returns the requested number (`IBMRuntimeBackend.prepare`, [IBM Runtime](ibm.md#ibm-runtime-prepared-execution)).

### NWQLib 1.0.4 with qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.52.0 {#nwqlib-104-with-runtime-052}

NWQLib 1.0.4 submits to IBM's server-side `SamplerV2` and `EstimatorV2`, which take the `SamplerOptions` and `EstimatorOptions` of `qiskit_ibm_runtime.options`, and it imports them from the top level of the package. Before version 0.52.0, qiskit-ibm-runtime exports these primitives there. From 0.52.0, the top-level names are the client-side primitives, so every IBM submission of NWQLib 1.0.4 raises, before the job is sent,

```text
TypeError: Expected SamplerOptions or dict, got <class 'qiskit_ibm_runtime.options.sampler_options.SamplerOptions'>
```

or the same message with `EstimatorOptions` for an estimate. With NWQLib 1.0.4, install `qiskit-ibm-runtime<0.52`. A Run whose submission raised this error sent no job, but the submission stays uncertain, its work stays counted and it is not submitted again. After installing the older package, start a new Run of the Plan ([Interrupted attempts](run_archives.md#interrupted-attempts)). IBM has deprecated the server-side primitives since qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.50.0, and from that release on, constructing one issues a `DeprecationWarning` stating that the class "will be removed no sooner than 3 months after the release date".

## Platform math libraries

QHD's binary angle-formation bound (`algorithms/qhd/circuit_errors.py::binary_angle_formation`) encloses the finite-difference kinetic energies with at most one ulp of error in `math.sin`, `math.cos` and NumPy's float64 `sin`, and the AQFT bound (`algorithms/qhd/binary.py::qft_error_bound`) assumes the same of `math.sin`. Python takes these functions from the platform C library, NumPy may use its own SIMD implementations, and neither documents a uniform error bound. The bounds were tested against independent exact evaluations on macOS arm64 and Linux aarch64, by the full test suites on both platforms (Python 3.12.14 with the same package versions on both hosts). NumPy's SIMD `sin` on Linux x86-64 hosts, where it can dispatch to AVX-512 code, has not been checked, so there the `bound` status of these entries rests on an unverified assumption. The spectral enclosures use no trigonometric function.
