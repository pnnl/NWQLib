# Expectation and quadratic form

`ExpectationMethod` computes `psi†Opsi/(psi†psi)` by default. Select `QuadraticForm` for `psi†Opsi`, which also uses the state's physical magnitude. The default quantum path accepts power-of-two dimensions with compact Pauli access or an explicit dense Hermitian observable of dimension at most 16. Its bounded I/X/Y/Z transform does no eigensolve, symmetrization or nonzero coefficient pruning. `execution="classical"` uses existing dense/CSR/CSC/Pauli matvec access in the original dimension, including dimension 3, without quantum PREP or Pauli conversion.

```python
from nwqlib import Expectation, QuadraticForm, solve
from nwqlib.algorithms import ExpectationMethod

problem = Expectation(state=[2., 2.], observable=[[2., 0.], [0., 0.]])
result = solve(problem, method=ExpectationMethod())
print(result.value)  # approximately 1
physical = solve(problem, method=ExpectationMethod(),
                 output=QuadraticForm(observable=problem.observable), execution="classical")
print(physical.value)  # approximately 8
```

The first `solve` executes this one-qubit example on Aer through the default quantum path. Replace the state and Hermitian observable to evaluate another normalized expectation. Exact simulator readout does not establish a hardware accuracy claim.

Let `s=||psi||²`. The quadratic form multiplies the normalized point and interval by s, and its estimator variance by s². Negative observables can give negative quadratic forms. Zero-state quadratic forms return zero without measurement. Normalized expectation at zero is undefined. `PhysicalScale` keeps mantissa and exponent, so intermediate norm squaring need not overflow when the final value is representable. Scale evidence remains numerical, not a proven norm bound. An unrepresentable result remains unavailable rather than becoming zero.

Classical work checks the matvec, reductions and known preparation products against the limits before materializing a supplied circuit or product state. `max_classical_products` defaults to 100,000,000; `max_bytes` controls known arrays. Standard circuit gates have a state-action cost law; opaque/custom preparation work remains unknown. A host invocation is synchronous and is not preempted by `Run.wait(timeout=...)`. Classical shots, binary inference, mitigation and provider precision are conflicting choices. Quantum dimension 3 is not supported by this method.

`ExpectationMethod(max_admission_steps=1_000_000)` bounds the kept field slots of each Program that ExpectationMethod builds and the admission work of one check of that Program. A larger Program is refused with the field name, the refused stage and its count. For the kept field slots that count is complete, and raising the field to it passes the slot count. For admission work it is the count reached when the check stopped, a lower bound. In both cases later admission, preparation and lowering can need more. The field changes the metadata-work allowance, not the selected quantum operations.

Classical planning recognizes an observable equal to cI by testing its stored nonzero support and constant diagonal, or its identity-only Pauli table. Such an observable has normalized expectation c for every nonzero state, so it needs no measurement. A general observable's mean diagonal does not establish this property.

Classical nonconstant observables use one invocation-local copy scaled by a binary exponent before matvec. The stored `scaled_observable` is `v†(O*2**(-operator_exponent))v`, where v is the normalized direction. Analysis combines that exponent with the physical norm scale before converting the requested value to binary64. Thus an overflowing normalized intermediate can still give a finite quadratic form. The archive checks the exponent against the original operator storage without rerunning matvec. CSR/CSC remain sparse and Pauli masks stay compact. If a single binary64 scaling would erase a nonzero component, the scalar is unavailable with that reason and is never silently dropped or clipped.

The default `ExpectationMethod()` keeps the exact grouped local Pauli readout. Exact `ExpectationMethod` readout prepares the normalized state direction once and returns all requested nonidentity Pauli expectations in one experiment. Identity coefficients are algebraic. The weighted sum gives the normalized expectation, and the state's physical norm squared supplies the quadratic form. Each readout label refers to that shared experiment.

A positive `shots` selects measured counts. Unmitigated sampled readout measures qubit-wise commuting Pauli groups. Each group uses one basis and a fresh shot population, from which the required parities are decoded. Weighted uncertainties include covariance between labels in the same group. Binary pivot mitigation uses its selected parity circuits and calibration populations.

The groups form by first fit in term order over the nonzero nonidentity labels. A label joins the first group whose accumulated basis has the same single-qubit Pauli on every qubit where both act, and `max_classical_products` bounds the number of these comparisons. A group experiment prepares the state on one register that holds every qubit of the state and applies the group's basis block, which gives each X site H and each Y site S† then H to map the Pauli eigenbasis to the computational basis. It then measures the whole register into one outcome with one bit per qubit, bit i holding qubit i. The parity of label j is `(-1)**popcount(outcome & support_j)`, where `support_j` marks the qubits on which the label is not I. Measuring the qubits outside the group's support adds physical measurements but no basis rotation or CX gate, and summing over their outcomes gives the distribution that measuring only the support would give. With mitigation, each nonidentity term has its own parity experiment. After the same site rotations, a CX network collects the parity of all active sites on the highest-index active qubit, called the pivot. The measured pivot bit is 0 for the +1 eigenvalue of the Pauli term and 1 for the -1 eigenvalue. `shots` counts the shots of each group or parity experiment. An identity-only observable requires no preparation, measurement or calibration, including when general shot options are selected.

## Selecting measured inference

Use the normal accepted inputs and `nwqlib.plan`/`nwqlib.solve` workflow. The Method controls are ordinary typed records; select `shots=256` separately in `nwqlib.plan(problem, method=method, shots=256)`:

```python
from nwqlib.algorithms import ExpectationMethod
from nwqlib.evidence.binary import BinaryInferenceOptions, BinaryReadoutMitigation

method = ExpectationMethod(
    preparation_choice="native",  # "hzh" also supports occupation inputs
    inference=BinaryInferenceOptions(
        method="hoeffding",
        failure_probability=0.05,
        sampling_model="iid_bernoulli",
        independent_populations=True,
    ),
    mitigation=BinaryReadoutMitigation(
        calibration_shots=512,    # for each zero/one calibration population
        minimum_contrast=0.05,
    ),
)
```

`mitigation=None` adds no calibration work. The exact default has `shots=None`, the default point-only inference options, and no mitigation. A statistical inference or mitigation selection without positive shots rejects during planning. `nwqlib.plan(problem, method=method, shots=256)` computes the `Plan`, the selected construction and its costs, before any circuit exists. It selects the science and calibration experiments, listed in the `Plan`'s `experiments`, without measuring data.

Without mitigation, G qubit-wise commuting groups use G preparations/measurements and `G*shots` shots. For K nonidentity terms and P distinct parity pivots, one completed mitigated workload uses K+2P preparations/measurements and `K*shots + 2*P*calibration_shots` shots. Science and calibration are actual selected experiments in the same Program. The resource fold includes their preparation, basis change, parity and measurement operations. Common solve checks the whole selected workload against ExecutionLimits before its first preparation. Failures and repeated attempts keep their actual cost. Scalar requests keep counts and inference records, without a statevector artifact.

## Selecting absolute sampling accuracy

For `O=c0 I+sum_j cj Pj`, let L be the number of nonzero nonidentity labels, G the number of nonempty qubit-wise commuting groups and `C=sum_j abs(cj)`. An absolute sampling request with tolerance epsilon and confidence `1-delta` selects `n=ceil(2*(C/epsilon)**2*log(2*L/delta))` shots per group. One group shot supplies every parity in that group, so the selected science workload uses `G*n` shots. For a quadratic form, use `s*C`, where s is the input's squared physical norm. An identity-only observable requires no measurement.

For each label, n independent outcomes in `[-1,1]` with a fixed mean satisfy `Pr(abs(zj-muj)>r)<=2*exp(-n*r*r/2)`. Applying this inequality with failure probability `delta/L` and taking a union bound gives simultaneous bounds for all labels. The triangle inequality then bounds the weighted error by `C*r`. Correlation between labels measured in the same shot does not affect this argument. The sampling premise concerns successive shots, and successful execution does not establish it. The bounded-outcome inequality is Hoeffding (1963), DOI 10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830, Theorem 2.

The analyzed fixed-time radius in the normalized frame uses the returned group sizes, `sum_j abs(cj)*sqrt(2*log(2*L/delta)/n_g(j))`, under the selected complete and fresh population requirements. Multiply it by s for a quadratic form. Partial returns, data-dependent stopping and reused measurements follow the chosen inference's eligibility rules. The explicitly selected anytime rule has its own time-uniform radius. A group's measurement is counted once even though several labels use it.

`Plan.selection_accuracy` records the criterion that selected the shots. `Result.facts` records an eligible evaluated radius and its own failure probability. The method supports absolute sampling selection for raw counts and rejects mitigation, Beta posterior selection, provider estimates and a competing explicit shot count. Sampling facts remain conditional numerical estimates. Preparation, simulation and physical-model errors have separate evidence, so a conditional radius below epsilon need not produce an unconditional accuracy PASS.

Before each original submission the connector rejects known reused fixed streams, including successful unused and uncertain attempted work. Actual PCG64 draws alone do not establish independence or stationarity.

Even when that radius is below the requested tolerance, `result.assess(component="sampling", absolute_tolerance=...)` can remain `INCONCLUSIVE` with `unverified=("sampling",)`. The Method's fact is a numerical estimate under the declared sampling premise, and its binary64 evaluation is not a proven numerical enclosure. Choosing `iid_bernoulli` does not prove that premise for measured data. Read the conditional interval/radius and its original confidence alongside `unverified`, rather than interpreting execution success as an unconditional accuracy PASS. See [error evidence](../error_evidence.md).

For an identity-only observable, there are no measurement settings. `plan.shots` can still hold a positive requested configuration, which is not an executed shot count. The Result's observations and trace show the actual measurement-free work.

## What the statistical fields mean

For `n0` zero outcomes and `n1` one outcomes, `n=n0+n1`, the empirical mean is `z=(n0-n1)/n`. Outcome 0 of a pivot bit, or even parity of a label's support bits in a group outcome, is the +1 eigenvalue, so under ideal readout z estimates `<Pj>`. For n>1, the empirical sample-mean variance is `4*n0*n1/[n*n*(n-1)]`, under an iid within-population model. A zero empirical variance after identical outcomes does not establish a known physical mean.

For a qubit-wise commuting group g with the weighted score `Y_g=sum_(j in g) c_j X_j`, where `X_j` is the decoded +/-1 parity of label j, the empirical variance of its mean over `n_g>1` shots is `(mean(Y_g**2)-mean(Y_g)**2)/(n_g-1)`. Within a group, covariance is essential, and per-label means alone cannot recover it. For Bell-state labels ZI and IZ with coefficients one, the weighted score has variance 4, while the sum of the individual variances is 2. The output variance adds one such contribution per group, so the variance sum assumes independent group populations.

The selected inference remains visible in `result.statistics`. Each population keeps its original counts, source/observation identities, empirical mean and variance, and any selected posterior/interval. `result.value` is the requested sum of selected estimates. `statistics.raw_value` separately keeps the uncorrected empirical sum. In the table, delta is `failure_probability` and `alpha=delta/F` is its share for each of the F predeclared populations, which are the L labels without mitigation or the science and calibration settings with mitigation.

| `inference.method` | Returned statement |
| --- | --- |
| `point` | Empirical point and available empirical variance; no interval. |
| `hoeffding` | Conditional fixed-time interval with radius `sqrt(2*log(2/alpha)/n)` for a binary mean. |
| `anytime_hoeffding` | Conditional time-uniform interval using `alpha_n=alpha/[n*(n+1)]`; its radius is `sqrt(2*log(2*n*(n+1)/alpha)/n)`. |
| `beta` | Positive-prior Beta posterior for `p=P(bit=0)`, with shapes `prior_alpha+n0`, `prior_beta+n1`; posterior mean, variance and equal-tail interval are mapped to `2*p-1`. Credibility is not frequentist coverage. When the shape total exceeds 5e11 the interval is unavailable and the mean and variance remain, because SciPy's quantile endpoints were validated to 1e-3 posterior standard deviations only up to that total (`MAX_BETA_SHAPE_TOTAL` in [ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md#binary-inference-representation)). |

The family is the `Plan`'s predeclared labels, or with mitigation its science and calibration settings. The failure probability is divided across that family before interval evaluation. Frequentist linear/rectangle propagation does not require independence across labels or settings, including labels that share a group's shots. A joint Beta family requires the selected independent-population and product-prior model, and `independent_populations=True` declares independent groups rather than independent parities sharing shots. The joint posterior model of parities that share a group's shots is unspecified. Each such label keeps its marginal Beta point and interval, and the aggregate posterior variance is unavailable with that reason. Without a compatible joint posterior, a weighted sum of marginal Beta points remains a selected estimator, but a joint credibility statement lacks its model. These are explicit model premises, not facts established by an option, a seed, or successful execution. With `sampling_model="unknown"`, raw empirical values remain useful and the corresponding interval is unavailable. Bayesian posterior output requires its likelihood model.

Fixed-time coverage additionally requires one complete original measurement for every setting, with exactly the prescribed returned shots and actual preparation records. Partial returns, extra/reused measurements and recorded stopping leave the selected fixed-time interval unavailable. They never switch the method automatically to the anytime option. The latter uses the stated union bound at every n under a fixed-target, constant-conditional-mean model. It does not claim a sharper confidence-sequence rate or cross-`Plan` winner coverage.

Accumulated statistical inference requires a chronological prefix of complete fresh populations for each setting. Omitting an earlier population, using an incomplete/uncertain population, or selecting a favorable subset cannot inherit the interval or posterior. Raw empirical points remain available; fitted empirical correction still depends on its actual channel applicability. A stop after a complete prefix is supported by the explicitly selected anytime model. Unused attempts after that prefix do not alter its kept data. An imported fixed-seed population from another run has no validated fresh history in the current run. Reusing it keeps raw data and execution cost, but cannot supply an anytime interval or Beta posterior from the new trace alone. A fresh hardware measurement from an imported circuit uses the current job's history. Existing reports keep their original history and remain readable.

Preparation records keep `CountsSampling`: fixed-seed, fresh measurement or unknown producer semantics. Same-context fixed-seed repetitions do not add sample size, even when a preparation UUID or RuntimeOptions ancestry changes. Conflicting counts from such a frozen source reject. Different queries using the same fixed stream remain distinct raw data. Across settings this withholds independent covariance and product-Beta inference, while marginal Hoeffding intervals and their union bound remain valid under their own model premises. Within a setting, incompatible same-stream accumulation cannot supply a fresh marginal population. Hardware sources use the original provider job, PUB, child job and result coordinates. One preparation can therefore supply several fresh populations. `BinaryPopulation.preparation_ids` associates every source with its actual preparation records, including those of replays. Equal histograms alone never merge data. Unknown cross-covariance remains unknown; `independent_populations=True` is a disclosed assumption for the covariance fold, not a deduction from different identifiers.

## Provider estimates

`ExpectationMethod(estimate_precision=0.1)` explicitly selects an estimate of the entire physical weighted Pauli sum. It excludes positive `shots`, binary inference and binary calibration. The precision has the units of the original coefficients. Identity-only problems remain exact scalar reductions without a job, even when this option is selected.

`IBMRuntimeBackend` compiles the original state preparation to the selected device's ISA and applies its kept layout to the complete observable, including identity coefficients. Qiskit's default observable coercion can drop small nonzero terms. This path uses exact-zero sparse simplification and its public prevalidated-array API after the common term/domain checks, preserving all nonzero coefficients without constructing a dense operator.

The result keeps each `EstimateValue` in `result.estimates`, with the original observable/precision identity, signed finite value, requested primitive options and returned provider metadata. For one measurement, `result.value` is that value for normalized output. A quadratic form scales that point by s. `result.provider_output_estimates` provides computed output-frame values and standard errors, each linked to the identifier of its raw estimate's saved record. It stores no second provider payload. The raw `result.estimates` values remain in the normalized-state observable frame. Multiple supplied measurements have an arithmetic-mean point. Their uncertainties remain separate because no covariance law was selected.

`standard_error` keeps a reported SEM when its meaning is established. For IBM ZNE, `stds` describes extrapolation-fit uncertainty, so that raw value remains in `provider_metadata_json` with an explicit sampling-SEM unavailability reason. The requested precision, reported uncertainty and physical bias are separate. Estimates can lie outside the physical observable's spectrum after finite sampling or mitigation. They never become bounded `PauliValue` statistics, exact trajectories or a zero-sampling error witness. IBM's statistical meanings are described in its [Estimator input/output documentation](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/estimator-input-output).

Estimator resilience defaults to zero unless the caller selects it in `IBMRuntimeBackend.options_json`. Effective provider defaults, measurement-basis counts, extra variants and billing remain unknown unless returned with a stated scope. `ConsumptionEvent.provider_managed_sampling` exposes that unknown cost; the run's direct-shot cap is not a provider-spend limit.

## Binary readout calibration

Each actual science pivot has two separate calibration experiments, preparing computational zero and one on that pivot and measuring it in the same logical layout. Analysis requires actual matching backend configuration, target, compiler/readout context and the native physical mapping of that parity pivot. Matching metadata alone does not establish physical channel stationarity. Missing preparation records preserve raw values but cannot establish fitted correction or interval applicability.

The selected model is a stationary single-bit readout channel. It maps the true parity mean mu to the observed pivot mean `z=a*mu+b`, with one contrast a and offset b shared by a pivot's science and calibration settings. Preparing zero (mu=+1) and one (mu=-1) gives the calibration means `z0=a+b` and `z1=b-a`, so `a=(z0-z1)/2`, `b=(z0+z1)/2`, and the corrected point is `mu=(z-b)/a`. Negative nonzero contrast is legal. If `abs(a)` is below `minimum_contrast`, the correction and therefore `value` are unavailable, and the uncorrected sum stays in `statistics.raw_value`. Finite-sample corrected points may lie outside [-1,1] and remain signed in records and JSON.

The optional variance is a first-order delta-method estimate. With `d=z0-z1`, its derivatives in `(z,z0,z1)` are `(2/d, -(1+mu)/d, (mu-1)/d)`. Terms sharing calibration use the same data identities, so their coefficient-weighted derivatives add before squaring. The existing `linear_variance` owner performs that covariance composition; missing joint evidence is not filled with zeros.

The joint confidence rectangle propagates only when its denominator interval excludes zero. Its image is kept before a justified intersection with the physical mean parameter domain [-1,1]. An empty intersection, an unavailable interval, and a signed out-of-range point remain distinct. Beta inference with fitted readout mitigation rejects before measurement because this recipe does not define that posterior model. The scalar correction helper also requires matching kept inference options, interval kinds, probabilities and family declarations. It cannot relabel fixed-time intervals as time-uniform or mix posterior credibility with coverage.

Calibration preparation, stationary channel, spectator transfer and fit bias remain unverified where not independently supplied. Aer can select an explicit noise model for counts. Calibration alone does not demonstrate noise reduction; nonidentity correction is separately tested with supplied/injected affine-channel data. No provider or hardware effectiveness claim follows from those checks.

## Reading the Result

Values are in the requested output frame unless the table says otherwise. That frame is `v†Ov` for `NormalizedExpectation` and s times `v†Ov` for `QuadraticForm`. Per-setting quantities are in the frame of one parity mean, before the coefficients and s are applied. Raw means, `BinaryEstimate` points and per-setting intervals lie in [-1,1], while a corrected point and its corner image can lie outside.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `value` | `c0 + sum(cj*estimate_j)`, times s for a quadratic form, where estimate_j is the exact Pauli mean, the selected binary point, or the corrected point with mitigation. The provider path uses the mean of the provider estimates of the whole weighted sum, and classical execution the matvec value. None when a label has no data, when a selected per-term point is unavailable (a Beta posterior whose premises fail, or a correction below `minimum_contrast`, without preparation records or without calibration counts), or when the value is unrepresentable. |
| `missing`, `unavailable` | Nonidentity labels without data, and the reason for an unavailable value. `value` is None exactly when `missing` is nonempty or `unavailable` is set. |
| `physical_scale` | `norm(psi)` as a mantissa and binary exponent, the source of s. |
| `statistics.populations` | Without mitigation, one `BinaryEstimate` per nonzero nonidentity label, named `<group setting>:<label>` in group and member order. Its counts are the label's marginal parities in its group's histogram, and the labels of one group share that group's source, observation and preparation-record identities, which count as one measurement. With mitigation, one `BinaryEstimate` per science and calibration setting, in `plan.reconstruction.settings` order. Its `population` holds the counts `zeros` (n0) and `ones` (n1) of the distinct counted sources, with their identities. `raw_mean` is z, `empirical_variance` the exact rational sample-mean variance (None for n<=1), `point` the Beta posterior mean for `beta` inference (None when its premises fail) and z otherwise, `posterior_variance` the Beta posterior variance of 2p-1, and `interval` the per-population interval evaluated at alpha (None for point inference), whose `probability` records the family level 1-delta. |
| `statistics.corrections` | With mitigation, one `BinaryCorrection` per science term. `contrast` is a, `offset` b, `point` the corrected mu, `derivatives` the Jacobian in `(z,z0,z1)`, `interval_image` the corner image before intersection with [-1,1], and `interval` that intersection. |
| `statistics.raw_value` | The same weighted sum over the uncorrected raw means. |
| `statistics.variance`, `variance_kind` | A `VarianceAssessment` of the estimator variance in squared output units, composed by `linear_variance`. Without mitigation it has one contribution per group, the empirical variance of the group's weighted score mean, which includes the covariance of labels in the group. With mitigation it composes the per-setting variances with the output-frame coefficients times the calibration Jacobian. `variance_kind` is `empirical` for sample-mean variances, `delta_method` for the linearized correction, or `posterior` for Beta variances. The assessed value is unknown when a needed variance is missing (n<=1, or no posterior), or when several groups or populations enter and `independent_populations=True` has not declared their covariances zero. `variance` is None, with `variance_unavailable`, when preparation records are missing, a population or stream reason exists, a coefficient, fitted point or Jacobian is unavailable, or Beta inference meets a group of several labels. |
| `statistics.interval` | Linear image, through the signed coefficients, of the simultaneous per-term intervals (the corrected ones with mitigation). None for point inference. Otherwise its `status` is `conditional`, `unavailable` or `empty`, and a conditional interval has coverage at least 1-delta under the sampling model and, with mitigation, the calibration channel model, or for Beta posterior credibility under the product-prior model. When labels share a group's shots, its assumptions record that a joint credibility statement lacks its model. |
| `statistics.fixed_time`, `applicability_reason`, `independence_reason` | Whether the data are one complete original measurement per setting, why missing preparation records leave the target, compiler and layout unverified, and why a shared fixed sampling stream leaves independence across settings unverified. |
| `estimates`, `provider_output_estimates` | Provider path only. The original `EstimateValue` records in the normalized frame, and their output-frame values and standard errors. |
| `facts` | The evaluated sampling radius with its own failure probability, as described under "Selecting absolute sampling accuracy". It is empty with mitigation, with Beta inference, when `value` is None, when a `hoeffding` or `anytime_hoeffding` interval is not conditional, and for point inference unless the shots were accuracy-selected, the data qualify as fixed-time and no fixed sampling stream is shared across settings. |

## Saved data, reanalysis and numerical scope

`result.save(path)` writes the actual `Plan`, Result, observations, preparation records and kept arrays. `nwqlib.load_result(path)` restores those original owners without replanning, native preparation or reanalysis. `result.analyze(inference=...)` explicitly applies another supported inference to the same data and keeps a new AnalysisOrigin. `result.assess(...)` applies a stated criterion without measuring anything. A criterion-free Result has no accuracy verdict.

The binary64 count domain is at most `2**53-1` per accumulated population, keeping integer and n+1 inputs exact. Positive Beta priors, posterior shapes and tail probabilities must remain representable before the scalar SciPy kernel is called. Unrepresentable selected posterior or correction quantities remain unavailable, with raw empirical data kept. Method inference uses finite scalar/record controls, while Run storage follows `ExecutionLimits.max_data_bytes`. These do not bound all CPU/RSS or opaque vendor workspace.

Statistical intervals are conditional numerical evaluations, not proven floating-point enclosures or total physical accuracy. The common ErrorModel continues to keep unresolved preparation, simulation, physical model, sampling and reconstruction errors, plus calibration-transfer error when applicable. A useful statistical interval does not automatically make that physical claim PASS.

| Classical matvec on `O 2^(-e)` with exact recovery of `2^e` | NWQLib design | `algorithms/expectation.py::ExpectationMethod._host_blocks`, `algorithms/expectation.py::_classical_value` |
| Stored Result checked against its `Plan` without new inference | NWQLib design | `algorithms/expectation.py::ExpectationAnalysis.validate_plan` with `_validate_measured_family`, `_validate_interval_meaning` and `_validate_corrections_and_value` |

With mitigation, each measured term has its own parity experiment, so its single measured bit is exactly the +/-1 population that binary inference and pivot calibration consume. Without mitigation, each label's marginal parity comes from its group's histogram, and the labels of one group refer to that one measurement. The derivations above assume the stated sampling model and calibration channel. They do not establish those premises for measured data.
