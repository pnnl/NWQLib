# GCiM and ADAPT

The Hermitian target belongs to `Eigenproblem`; the method owns its trial basis or adaptive pool.

```python
from nwqlib import Eigenproblem, solve
from nwqlib.algorithms.gcim import FixedGCIM

problem = Eigenproblem(A=[[1, 0], [0, -1]])
result = solve(problem, method=FixedGCIM(basis=([1, 1], [1, 1j])), seed=7)
print(result.eigenvalue)
```

FixedGCIM uses normalized preparation columns. Exact quantum FixedGCIM and ADAPT obtain `H_ij=<phi_i|A|phi_j>` and `S_ij=<phi_i|phi_j>` once per basis pair, sampled FixedGCIM measures them with grouped joint-state counts, and sampled ADAPT with complex Hadamard transition amplitudes, followed by the shared generalized Hermitian eigensolver. Each exact pair publishes the offset-free Hamiltonian entry and overlap entry. An off-diagonal pair uses the two branches of one phase-faithful joint preparation. The reducer applies the selected H0 once to the right branch with the grouped Pauli kernel, then contracts with the left branch. It computes overlap directly from the two branches. The Hamiltonian entry's error combines preparation error, grouped-action rounding and complex-dot rounding. The overlap entry has only preparation and dot error. A diagonal pair uses one system preparation and returns its raw squared norm as the obtained overlap. Pencil assembly uses unit diagonal overlap under the normalized-basis convention, while the raw obtained scalars and selected padded coordinates remain associated with the readout that produced them. For `A=cI+A0`, the same overlap observations supply `H=cS+H0`. No independently measured identity term or coordinate-identity substitution is made. The solve uses `H0` and adds c to the Ritz values afterwards, and `result.pencil` records `H`. Inside the solve, `c*S` would enter the product `X†HX` of the canonical orthogonalization, whose roundoff grows like `u*|c|/s_min` for the smallest kept overlap eigenvalue `s_min`, so a large offset on a nearly dependent basis would shift the Ritz values by far more than roundoff. Missing real or imaginary data leaves the pencil incomplete. A legal rank-deficient trial space is distinguished from an invalid deterministic Gram. The solve refuses a deterministic Gram only when an overlap eigenvalue lies below minus the derived error of its entries plus the eigensolver roundoff. For entries obtained classically, that error comes from the length-d inner products, `sqrt(2)*gamma_(2d)*trace(S)` to first order. For exact quantum FixedGCIM and ADAPT it is the largest row sum of the off-diagonal overlap entry bounds, each from the saved-state error (`saved_state_error`) of its own pair's preparation record ([engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md)). An unresolved preparation record leaves it unavailable, and the solve then allows no input error beyond its own roundoff. Exact FixedGCIM pairs use native X gates for the ancilla flips. Their preparation records supply a first-order state-error budget when the executed native operations and parameters satisfy the backend's roundoff model, with the pair reducer accounting for the host contractions. Supplied matrix gates are synthesized when controlled for off-diagonal pairs, whose finite bounds assume the library's exact-to-rounding synthesis convention. An uncontrolled matrix in a diagonal pair can leave that pair's bound unavailable, while unresolved native parameter exclusions, such as an excessive U-gate phase sum, can also leave off-diagonal bounds unavailable. Only off-diagonal overlap bounds enter the Gram error budget. A sampled overlap uses the positive-subspace policy instead. Here u = 2**-53 is the unit roundoff, `gamma_n = n*u/(1-n*u)` (Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., doi:10.1137/1.9780898718027, Lemma 3.1) and m is the number of basis states.

The default quantum path reduces each pair's exact saved state when obtained. Sampled FixedGCIM uses shots=N per qubit-wise-commuting system group, with separate ancilla X and Y settings for an off-diagonal pair and one system setting per group on a diagonal pair. A group supplying overlap also records its overlap–Hamiltonian cross-moment, so the physical-Hamiltonian variance includes the covariance of their shared shots. ADAPT's sampled pair queries use N shots per elementary Hadamard setting. The processed-operator enclosure is a numerical diagnostic of the sampled Ritz estimate. Exact quantum FixedGCIM and ADAPT therefore need a target that runs registered reducers, such as Aer or NWQ-Sim's CPU/SV target. On other targets, use `shots=N` or `execution="classical"`. `execution="classical"` obtains `V†(A-cI)V` and `V†V` directly from the actual input access, with `c=trace(A)/d`, the identity coefficient of a Pauli input. It removes c from the Pauli table or from the stored diagonal before any product. It performs no reference eigensolve or unnecessary Pauli conversion. Both paths share projected analysis. Quantum dense/sparse conversions use method-owned mean-trace padding. Classical FixedGCIM keeps original dimensions. Every supplied column remains defined in original coordinates.

`FixedGCIM(max_admission_steps=1_000_000)` bounds the kept field slots of each Program that FixedGCIM builds and the admission work of one check of that Program. A larger Program is refused with the field name, the refused stage and its count. For the kept field slots that count is complete, and raising the field to it passes the slot count. For admission work it is the count reached when the check stopped, a lower bound. In both cases later admission, preparation and lowering can need more. The field changes the metadata-work allowance, not the selected quantum operations.

`result.eigenvalue` is the smallest obtained projected value. It does not establish the full-space ground target. `result.analyze(overlap_cutoff=...)` uses the same obtained H/S with a new absolute cutoff. The default is `1e-12`. `result.pencil` exposes the raw complex Gram, projected Hamiltonian, kept spectrum and numerical diagnostics.

For sampled quantum FixedGCIM and ADAPT, the current pencil also records the processed Pauli L1 spectral enclosure, the Ritz estimate's distance outside it, and the relative numerical comparison window. A noisy pencil need not obey the exact operator's Rayleigh–Ritz enclosure even when its own eigensolve has small backward error. This is a physical plausibility diagnostic, not a joint sampling bound, a proven accuracy bound or a ground-state test. The raw estimate is kept; no clipping, resampling or additional full-space eigensolve is performed. An unrepresentable enclosure stays unavailable.

## Inspect numerical diagnostics and error bounds

The stored `result.pencil` exposes the intermediate quantities needed to inspect a GCiM calculation. These fields describe the selected projected problem and can be examined after loading a saved Result, without obtaining its matrix elements again.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `overlap_eigenvalues`, `overlap_cutoff`, `kept_rank` | Raw Gram spectrum, selected absolute cutoff and number of positive directions used in the solve. |
| `overlap_condition_number` | Largest divided by smallest Gram eigenvalue among the directions above the cutoff. |
| `projected_residual` | Computed `norm2(Hc - ESc)` for the lowest returned projected eigenpair, evaluated as `norm2(H0 c - (E - c_I) S c)` without the identity coefficient `c_I`, which gives the same value in exact arithmetic. |
| `projected_backward_error` | Computed residual divided by `(normF(H) + abs(E) * normF(S)) * norm2(c)`, with the physical `H = H0 + c_I S` and E. This is dimensionless and unchanged by a rescaled energy unit, but adding an identity term to the operator changes it. Lanczos states its value on a normalized pencil instead ([Projected quantities](../verification.md#projected-quantities)). The reported value is zero when the computed residual is zero. |
| `overlap_normalization_error` | Computed `abs(c†Sc - 1)` for the lowest returned coefficient vector. |

Here H and S are the projected matrices and c is the corresponding coefficient vector, distinct from the identity coefficient c of the paragraphs above, which the table writes `c_I`. `norm2` is the Euclidean vector norm and `normF` is the Frobenius matrix norm. The numerical diagnostics report floating-point evaluations. Their sizes help explain the solve and its conditioning, but they do not by themselves bound trial-space approximation or full-space ground-energy error. A zero Hermiticity defect in a matrix assembled by conjugate completion describes that representation rather than an independent validation of its obtained entries. [Projected verification](../verification.md#projected-quantities) exposes selected stored diagnostics as numerical facts without obtaining the entries again or running another eigensolve.

A numerical error bound must identify both the quantity it bounds and its premises. For example, if S is exactly the identity and two Hermitian projected Hamiltonians have a justified difference bound `||H_hat - H_star||_F <= delta`, their minimum eigenvalues differ by at most `delta` by the [Hermitian eigenvalue perturbation bound](https://nhigham.com/2021/03/09/eigenvalue-inequalities-for-hermitian-matrices/). This bounds the matrix-perturbation contribution to the projected energy. It does not include error in the subsequent eigensolve or error from the chosen trial space. With a perturbed or ill-conditioned S, an H-only bound is insufficient.

FixedGCIM's default total-error model declares six unresolved sources, and its analysis does not automatically produce the corresponding physical-output error bounds. Thus the default total `result.assess(absolute_tolerance=...)` remains `INCONCLUSIVE` even for a full trial basis. Additional compatible `facts=` can supply established bounds, but a reference energy alone does not fill the missing contributions. The absolute-error criterion does not require a known ground energy. The implementation work is tracked in [the roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#gcim-physical-output-error-bounds-and-automatic-assessment).

## Adaptive generator coordinates

```python
from nwqlib.algorithms.gcim import ADAPT
from nwqlib.operators import ingest_pauli

pool = (ingest_pauli((("Y", 1j),), num_qubits=1),)
result = solve(problem, method=ADAPT(initial_state=[1, 1], pool=pool), seed=7)
print(result.selected, result.stop_reason, result.eigenvalue)
```

ADAPT screens the current product state with `<[H,A_j]>`, chooses the largest absolute gradient, then expands the actual generator-coordinate basis. Classical ADAPT screening reuses the cached action of H minus its identity coefficient on the current normalized product state. The exact quantum full-chain query supplies both its Hamiltonian diagonal values and the active commutator expectations. The matrix stage obtains this query before projected analysis, and later screening reuses its observation. The diagonal overlap is one under the normalized-preparation contract. Shared values refer to one readout, whose actual labels, preparation record and contribution identity survive continuation and loading. A round that stops after its matrix stage has still paid for the screening values of that query. Pool order decides only between absolute gradients that are equal as binary64 numbers, and the lowest index wins. Gradients that are equal in exact arithmetic, for example because a symmetry of H, the reference and the pool maps one generator to another, usually differ in binary64 by roundoff, so the summation order, such as the order of the Hamiltonian's Pauli terms, can decide which of them wins. For example, take six qubits on a ring, `H = sum_j (h1 Z_j + h2 X_j X_(j+1) + h3 X_j)` with `h1 = sqrt(2)/3`, `h2 = pi/7` and `h3 = e/5` on every site, the product reference `cos(0.3)|0> + sin(0.3)|1>` on every qubit and the pool `iY_j`. With the Pauli terms listed for j = 0, ..., 5 in the order of this sum, the six gradients agree to 50 digits but take four different binary64 values. Reversing the order of the terms gives five values and changes the winner. When a symmetry relates the tied generators, each choice gives the same energies in exact arithmetic. For `H=Z`, `A=iY`, `[Z,iY]=2X`, so the initial `|+>` gradient is 2. A projected Ritz solution does not replace this product-state screening rule. The basis order is reference, individual selected generators, then the current full product and earlier prefixes without duplicate first products.

Defaults are `theta=pi/4`, gradient floor `1e-8`, energy-change tolerance `1e-6`, overlap cutoff `1e-12`, flat-counter stopping and eight iterations. These are operational settings, not convergence or ground-coverage guarantees. The flat counter follows the published minimum rule described below, counting consecutive changes from the reference energy. With a fixed angle, the selected generators and the energy after a given iteration depend on the sign of each generator. Replacing a generator A by -A changes the product states exp(theta A), so the subspace spanned and the later screening differ. Optional optimization requires a period, iteration count and finite limit on total energy evaluations, which counts distinct evaluated parameter points. Quantum optimization uses the existing bounded generalized or Pauli-insertion shift rules. Classical product optimization evaluates energy and gradient together with a forward and backward sweep, sharing one Hamiltonian action per parameter point. The derivative follows the selected generator action and normalization. BFGS iteration limits, query accounting and the distinction between the surrogate product energy and the projected GCiM energy remain explicit. Completed query points are reused without new seeds. Restarted SciPy BFGS begins from its completed incumbent and records that its search trajectory may change.

With both default limits, `max_iterations=8` and `t_user=10`, the flat counter can fire only if the original pool has at most 42 generators. After k selections a pool of P generators has P-k unselected members and a flat count of at most k, so the stop needs k >= .2(P-k). The iteration cap is checked first and ends the run at step 8, which makes step 7 the last step for this stop and gives P <= 42. Larger pools reach the iteration cap before this particular stop can fire. This does not affect the other stopping conditions.

Quantum queries use the actual generator compiler data, chain parameters, controlled phase and readout. ADAPT checks a family-level logical construction envelope against the limits before querying. A generator's angle-independent compiler data is constructed when that generator is selected and reused across its later angles. The envelope uses its system width, Pauli term count and supported compiler family. Logical construction slots and later native synthesis costs are reported separately. Classical queries use the same processed input and controller, with explicit state/action accounting. Only a quantum plan forms the commutators `[H,A_j]` whose Pauli labels its screening queries read. Only a quantum plan with shots also groups those labels and the Hamiltonian's into qubit-wise commuting readout groups, whose comparisons are charged to `max_products`. Like classical FixedGCIM, a classical pair query removes the identity component `c=trace(H)/d` before it projects, from the Pauli table or from the stored diagonal of a dense or sparse matrix, and the solve adds c back, so an identity offset of H does not enter the rounding of the projected entries. The named spin-adapted, UCCSD-SD, QEB-SD and OVP-CEO pools preserve their established ordering and preparation restrictions. Processing cutoffs and any accepted pool symmetry correction are recorded with their removed mass and original input identity.

The default spin-adapted pool uses the signed operators of [Zheng et al., arXiv:2312.07691v3, Appendix E.1, Eqs. (E1)–(E3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07691v3). It enumerates spatial pairs with `p <= q`, `r <= s` and the first pair no later than the second in lexicographic order. The double builders write interleaved ladder operators, so their signs follow from anticommutation, adjoint subtraction and positive coefficient normalization together. The independent scalar test `test_generalized_pool_has_the_signed_operators_of_zheng_table_5` checks every operator in Table V (Table 5 in the npj version), including signs and repeated indices. Eq. (E4)'s `q < p` convention describes elementary circuit decomposition and does not prescribe the spin-adapted pool's enumeration.

`result.history` preserves the settings, choices and observations that actually drove each decision. Post-hoc cutoff analysis cannot rewrite that history. `run.resume()` retrieves known jobs before new work; `run.resume(reanalyze=True)` explicitly recovers an interrupted projected analysis from complete saved data. Unknown jobs are not replaced. Before a quantum matrix stage runs its first query, the Run checks the stage's new pencil queries against its circuit and shot limits, counted by `pencil.matrix_element_count` from the basis size and the pairs of basis states that earlier stages obtained. A stage that those limits cannot finish is refused before any of its work, and the message gives the counted, requested and needed totals. After `run.extend_limits(...)`, `run.resume()` runs the stage with the same seeds as a Run that was never refused. The stage in progress when a Run resumes may be partly run, so its queries are checked one at a time. `result.save(...)` and current-format loading preserve the selected pool, the compiler data of the selected generators and sufficient observations without replanning.

ADAPT validates each saved chunk against its selected query during reanalysis. The `Plan` (the selected construction and its costs, computed before any circuit exists) keeps only its most recent resolution, so a forward pass over Q distinct queries normally resolves Q points again, even when a query supplies several adjacent observation chunks. Saving a quantum Result also derives the query constructions needed to validate its saved evidence. These passes use the obtained data and do not run the quantum circuits again.

The summary describes the current pencil's enclosure diagnostic, including after cutoff reanalysis, rather than attaching an earlier round's warning to a later estimate. Solver failure and the controller's operational stop reason remain separate; an enclosure excursion does not change generator selection, stopping or previous-energy updates. The matrix stage validates each original query before assembly and passes one current matrix to projection. That temporary matrix is released after use and is not serialized as another history; restoring an interrupted projection rebuilds it from the original observations.

Explicit `AdaptVerificationOptions` selects residual, sector or supplied-reference-energy checks through `result.verify(checks=...)`. Native verification constructs the actual selected gate suffixes and records that construction work. Classical verification reuses immutable prefix vectors from the Result's scientific snapshot, including after loading an archive. Both accumulate the Ritz state in basis order and release each basis or prefix state after its last contribution and successor evolution. Their check against `max_bytes` covers these states, for native verification the largest gate matrix applied at once, and the arrays the saved Result already owns. Native residual and sector checks rebuild each basis state as a full statevector of 2**n amplitudes on n qubits. Before any state exists, verification builds and decomposes each circuit it will simulate exactly once. Each dense unitary in a supplied reference circuit is first replaced by its exact synthesis, whose work (`_dense_synthesis.dense_synthesis_size`) is charged to `max_products` and `max_bytes` before it starts and counts toward the total. The limit check then charges each decomposed k-qubit gate `1 + 4**k + 2**n * (2**k + 2)` operations, for its instruction copy, its matrix, its application and two state copies, and adds 2**n for the reference's zero state and for each nonzero global phase. The decomposition itself and Qiskit's workspace are outside this count. With an occupation reference, a native check on the spin-adapted pool at 8 qubits needs at most 91.1 million operations for any selection of up to eight generators, plus the shared Pauli action law of the Hamiltonian for a residual. At 12 qubits one shared-index double needs up to 104 million, one four-distinct double up to 56.7 million and one pair-split double up to 10.3 million. A direct-vector reference costs far more, about 335 million operations alone at 12 qubits. The default `AdaptVerificationOptions.max_products=1_000_000_000` is a fuse against runaway planning work, sized so that no example or test workload of this release meets it. A check above it needs an explicitly raised `max_products`. Mutable native Gate definitions stay in the private Run cache for continuation and are excluded from public Result data. A zero residual can belong to an excited eigenstate. Sector expectations are not membership proofs, and signed spin contamination stays signed. Sampled results cannot inherit unsupplied joint-coverage or ground claims. `ProjectedVerificationOptions` performs only checks on stored projected data.

The default flat-counter stopping rule follows [Zheng et al., arXiv:2312.07691v3, Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.07691v3): compare consecutive projected-energy changes with `energy_change_tolerance` and stop after `min(t_auto_fraction * unselected_count, t_user)` flat rounds. The defaults are `t_auto_fraction=.2` and `t_user=10`. An integer counter reaches a fractional threshold at its ceiling. Counting starts with the reference-to-first-basis energy change and resets after a large or unavailable change. This heuristic does not prove a bound on the physical energy error. [Replacing it with better-supported stopping evidence](../ROADMAP.md#adapt-gcim-stopping-beyond-the-flat-counter) is planned research work.

The paper's accompanying [QuGCM implementation, `adapt_gcim.py`, lines 303–398](https://github.com/pnnl/QuGCM/blob/c5efcb0171eaed8a51b75b2dff78440a820f903c/ADAPT-GCIM/adapt_gcim.py#L303-L398), selects each pool index at most once. NWQLib follows this ADAPT-GCIM selection convention. Reusing a fixed-angle generator would repeat its single-generator reference basis vector, although its cumulative product vector could be new, so allowing reuse would change the trial-space construction and pool-exhaustion rule. Grimsley's ADAPT-VQE step 7 (arXiv:1812.11173v2) addresses a different ansatz with optimized parameters. The same QuGCM source uses `max(10, floor(.1*unselected))` and an initial warmup for stopping, which differs from Zheng's published Methods rule (arXiv:2312.07691v3). NWQLib's flat counter follows the published rule above.

`build_gcim_chemistry_problem(...)` returns the molecular Hamiltonian, occupation ordering and explicit reference panel. Molecular-orbital signs from the eigensolver are arbitrary and can differ between LAPACK builds, and each orbital's sign fixes the sign of the generators that contain it. The builder therefore makes the first AO coefficient of each orbital above `ORBITAL_PHASE_RELATIVE_THRESHOLD` of its largest magnitude positive, so the same geometry gives the same Hamiltonian and the same fixed-angle ADAPT trajectory on every platform. This fixes signs only. Degenerate orbitals can still be returned in another rotation of their subspace. Its `eigenproblem()` preserves Hartree units and the recorded constant offset; `adapt_method(...)` uses its occupation preparation. MP2, CCSD and CASCI remain explicit selected computations.

To show the stored reference panel next to an obtained energy, build a Chemistry References `ReportSection` from the chemistry input's `metadata` and the Result's `eigenvalue`.

```python
from nwqlib.algorithms.gcim import (
    chemistry_reference_diagnostic,
    chemistry_reference_report_section,
)

diagnostic = chemistry_reference_diagnostic(data.metadata, energy=result.eigenvalue)
section = chemistry_reference_report_section(diagnostic)
print("\n".join(section.lines))
```

Here `data` is the value returned by `build_gcim_chemistry_problem(...)`, and `result` is a Result for `data.eigenproblem()`. The diagnostic reads only the stored reference panel and the supplied energy E, and the section only formats that diagnostic. Neither starts an RHF, MP2, CCSD or CASCI calculation or a simulation. The section lists E_HF, E_MP2 and E_CCSD, plus E_CASCI for an active space. A missing value shows its recorded status and reason, such as `not_requested`, `failed` or `non_converged`, or `unavailable` when no status is stored. When the needed energies are present, separate lines give the full-space CCSD correlation fraction `(E - E_HF) / (E_CCSD - E_HF)` and the active-space CASCI fraction `(E - E_HF) / (E_CASCI - E_HF)`. A zero denominator is reported as `undefined: E_CCSD == E_HF`, or `undefined: E_CASCI == E_HF` for the CASCI fraction. `section.data` holds the same diagnostic as a mapping, and `correlation_fraction(E, E_HF, E_CCSD)` evaluates one ratio directly, raising `ValueError` for a zero denominator. These references give application context. They neither prove a bound on the total error nor identify the ground state. `result.report()` does not include this section, so print or store it next to the report.

## Matrix access and repeated basis inputs

Classical FixedGCIM keeps dense/CSR/CSC inputs and all basis columns in their original dimension. Within one host invocation, repeated uses of the same immutable state input share its materialized vector and `(A-cI)@vector`. Classical GCiM fills normalized trial columns V and shifted actions W=(A−cI)V in two preallocated complex arrays. Their payload is 32bN bytes for b columns of length N. Conjugating inner products form H₀=V†W and S=V†V without an additional state block. The projection's byte charge adds the b-by-b matrices and the actual preparation, operator and action workspace. The stored physical Hamiltonian is H=H₀+cS. Pauli input acts on all columns with one grouped Pauli action. Dense input is shifted in row blocks refilled in one buffer. CSR/CSC input is shifted once per Run into one read-only sparse payload, which holds a full copy of the stored values and shares the index pattern only when every diagonal entry is stored. Its bytes are given in the [engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md). The ordered columns and their complete `V†(A-cI)V,V†V` pencil remain present, including legal rank deficiency. Distinct state inputs are not merged by shape, declared name or circuit equivalence. Save/load preserves the selected duplicate binding. Known preparation products join the projected-work limit check before materialization. Opaque custom circuit simulation work remains unknown.

Quantum FixedGCIM and ADAPT use the same `input_conversion="auto"|"dense_pauli"` and `max_conversion_work=100_000_000` options as [Lanczos](lanczos.md#choose-input-access-and-understand-its-cost). Automatic conversion accepts only explicit dense original dimension at most 16, and compact Pauli inputs do not receive that limit. Choose `dense_pauli` explicitly for a sparse quantum matrix or a sparse custom ADAPT generator. For example, `ADAPT(initial_state=[1, 1], pool=(csr_matrix([[0., 1.], [-1., 0.]]),), input_conversion="dense_pauli")` accepts that finite custom generator under the same transform-work and byte caps. Classical ADAPT keeps sparse Hamiltonian action. For a Pauli Hamiltonian its pair queries act with the nonidentity terms and the solve adds the identity coefficient back, as in FixedGCIM. An original dense or sparse Hamiltonian acts with its mean diagonal `c=trace(A)/d` removed, and the solve adds c back. Its qubit generator domain uses mean-diagonal sparse block padding for non-power-of-two targets and zero-padded original reference states. It does not introduce a dense sparse-Hamiltonian conversion. A quantum ADAPT `Plan` with a supplied reference circuit charges the exact synthesis of its dense unitaries, and Qiskit's control of them in the Hadamard-test and joint-state queries, to `max_products` and `max_bytes` at planning ([selected blocks](../blocks.md#admission-of-the-exact-synthesis)).

`EnergyShiftOptions.for_result` also supports FixedGCIM's original dense/CSR/CSC classical inputs. Its explicit matrix-entry evidence uses the existing [energy-shift owner](lanczos.md#choose-input-access-and-understand-its-cost), preserves complex off-diagonals and implicit zeros, and performs no Pauli conversion or solve.
