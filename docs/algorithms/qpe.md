# Quantum phase and energy estimation

`QCELS`, `SPE`, `RFE` and `RWPE` are concrete Methods with their own estimator controls. They share selected power construction and the execution lifecycle. Natural input is an `Eigenproblem` with Method-owned preparation, or `SpectralEstimation` with its explicit Hamiltonian/unitary and preparation. The preparation defines the observed spectral population. A single-mode estimate does not establish ground identity or dominant-component coverage.

```python
from nwqlib import Eigenproblem, solve
from nwqlib.algorithms.qpe import QCELS, SPE, RFE, RWPE

problem = Eigenproblem(A=[[0.2, 0.0], [0.0, 0.7]])
result = solve(problem, method=QCELS(initial_state=[1, 0]), seed=7)
print(result.eigenvalue, result.phase)
```

The default QCELS example performs a real two-qubit Aer execution. Other choices include `SPE(initial_state=[1, 0], overlap_lower_bound=1.)`, `RFE(initial_state=[1, 0])` and `RWPE(initial_state=[1, 0])`. SPE requires a declared lower bound on the target component's probability. Static methods default to exact readout of one controlled trajectory, and positive `shots` sets the population per elementary QCELS setting and per random SPE or RFE draw, so an SPE or RFE setting drawn m times receives m times `shots`. Backend limits on shots per circuit apply to that combined population. RWPE uses one shot per feedback step and defaults to 14 steps. It rejects a different requested shot count because its update is defined for one Bernoulli datum.

The eigenvalue notebook (`examples/gcim_lanczos_qpe_eigenvalue_intro.ipynb`) applies QCELS to a stretched H4 chain together with ADAPT-GCIM and Lanczos.

## Meaning of the result

`result.phase` is in turns, in [0,1), with U v = exp(2 pi i phase) v. For a Hamiltonian, U = exp(-i tau H), so `result.eigenvalue` is in original matrix units and includes the minus sign in the conversion. Unitary-only input has no Hamiltonian eigenvalue without a chosen logarithm/branch; accessing that field raises AttributeError. `result.value` follows the requested output.

Static exact QPE prepares the control and system once and reads ancilla X and Y at the declared power positions of a controlled trajectory. These expectations give the real and imaginary parts of `z_p`, with `〈Y_a〉=Im(z_p)`. A product-formula trajectory uses one ordered step and an integer time grid whose bounds meet every requested power's error budget. The `Plan` (the selected construction and its costs, computed before any circuit exists) records cumulative step counts and per-power errors. SPE and RFE keep their original random-draw multiplicities. Sampled X and Y settings use separate preparations, and RWPE follows its feedback-dependent measurement schedule. Each query records the Experiment that measures it and, on the trajectory, its point, where the real and imaginary queries of one power share one measurement. A missing point leaves its queries missing, and analysis and loading never replay a prefix to replace it.

SPE returns the lowest value in the prepared spectral support. For a Hamiltonian that is the lowest energy. For a unitary it is the lowest principal eigenphase angle `theta`, with U v = exp(i theta) v and `theta` scanned on [-pi/2, pi/2). When `tau*|E| < pi/2` for every prepared energy E of a unitary U = exp(-i tau H), `theta = -tau*E`, so SPE on U returns the phase of the highest energy of the prepared support. Supply H itself, or U† = exp(i tau H), to target the lowest energy.

The stored `estimator_value` is the authoritative kernel value: energy for Hamiltonians, principal radians for unitary input. Phase and interval are converted once. Hamiltonian energy bounds multiply by `-tau/(2*pi)`, reverse endpoints and are recentered around the displayed phase. The interval stays unwrapped; wrapping each endpoint separately would change its meaning. It is a method interval, not composed total-error or ground-identification evidence.

Exact trajectory readout saves the ancilla X and Y expectations at every point. A saved expectation may exceed one in magnitude only within the binary64 roundoff window of the executed trajectory, `exact_probability_window(G, n) = max(1e-12, (c*G + 2**(n+1) + 10)*u)`. Here G is the native operation count recorded in the preparation record (on Aer, without save instructions), n the circuit width and u = 2**-53. c is the executing simulator's derived first-order bound on the change of the squared state norm per instruction, about 6957 for Aer and 164 for NWQ-Sim CPU/SV. Any other target uses Aer's constant, the larger one. [Engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md) derives both and names what they exclude. A preparation record lists the exclusions present in its own execution in `probability_window_exclusions`. An unknown instruction count keeps the 1e-12 floor. Deep controlled-evolution trajectories need this scaling. One whole-trajectory G gives a conservative window for every point, and neither G nor the window is multiplied by the number of saved labels or positions. The observation is checked against its preparation record when it is created and again before a Result is saved. Raw saved expectations remain unchanged. If one exceeds ±1 within the window, QPESample stores the original value in `raw_mean` and uses the corresponding exact endpoint as `mean` for bounded likelihoods. The adjustment is `mean - raw_mean`, and the sample's contribution ID identifies the point chunk. The classical scalar-signal route uses the fixed `NUMERICAL_RELATION_RTOL=1e-12` window of a host kernel, preserving its original scalar in `raw_mean` and its source contribution ID. In-range means are unchanged and counts never use this exception. The window bounds roundoff in the saved expectation, apart from the preparation record's listed exclusions. It is not an accuracy bound for the estimate. Comparing a trajectory value with a separately prepared value needs the sum of their two justified error bounds, not one preparation record's window.

For static Hamiltonian queries, integer powers identify energy only modulo `2*pi/tau`. Automatic selection uses the existing enclosure without another eigensolve. QCELS and RFE use a principal radius of pi. SPE also needs a filter transition margin delta, since its scan evaluates F(x-tau*E) near the periodic wrap boundary. A sufficient condition is `tau*R + delta <= pi/2`, where R bounds the spectrum. Its automatic radius is `min(pi/3, pi/2-delta)`, followed by the common .9 interior margin. Theorem 3 of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, supplies delta from beta and the declared overlap, and its degree premise is checked separately. An unavailable premise or a supplied time outside that domain appears in the Result. A crossing at the scan boundary is unidentified, rather than an endpoint energy. Raw-unitary SPE has no established support enclosure. RWPE uses continuous times and a Gaussian prior, whose finite reach is checked against the existing enclosure.

A supplied Eigenproblem remains the same target when the Method preparation changes. Preparing an excited eigenstate can return its energy while the problem still requests the smallest eigenvalue. The result states that limitation. Small dense non-power-of-two input uses a mean-trace Hermitian dummy block and a zero-padded preparation; the original problem and preparation identities remain recorded. A unitary uses an identity dummy block. Scalar targets use one control qubit and no system register or preparation block.

## Scientific choices and actual cost

| Method | Estimator controls and defaults | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `QCELS` | `num_times=16`, `max_time=None`, `grid_size=4096` | Integer powers plus zero and paired quadratures. `None` selects maximum power 10; supplied `max_time` selects `floor(max_time/tau)` (unitary: `floor(max_time)`). Complex least squares uses a finite grid with bounded bracket refinement. |
| `SPE` | Required `overlap_lower_bound`, `num_samples=32`, `fourier_degree=11`, `filter_beta=6`, `grid_size=4096` | Sample positive odd Fourier frequencies proportional to their coefficient magnitudes, measure paired quadratures, and locate the first ACDF crossing of eta/2. |
| `RFE` | `num_samples=97`, `num_frequencies=49` | Draw M independent uniform powers from 0 through K-1, measure both quadratures for each draw, and select the largest sampled Fourier coefficient. |
| `RWPE` | `max_steps=14`, `prior_mean=0`, `prior_std=pi` | Basic Gaussian random walk with one datum per continuous-time experiment. Zero steps reports the prior without measurement. |

Each Method also accepts the shared `tau`, `controlled_power_backend`, `controlled_power_error_budget` and `pauli_pruning_rtol` controls. The estimator identity follows the concrete Method and cannot be changed by an `estimator` keyword. SPE's maximum Fourier frequency is `2*fourier_degree+1`. The common `max_power` separately caps the absolute evolution time in units of tau, including RWPE's noninteger times.

The default shot workload is 22 elementary settings for QCELS and 14 one-shot measurements for RWPE. Quantum RWPE plans `max_steps` circuit preparations, acquisitions and one-shot observations. It checks all three totals before preparing the first feedback query. A caller cancellation can end the run earlier. The feedback angle remains dependent on preceding outcomes, so only the next circuit is prepared at a time. SPE and RFE draw 32 and 97 paired frequencies respectively, so their 64 and 194 quadrature draws request 64 and 194 times `shots` coherent shots. SPE and RFE make one query per distinct drawn (power, quadrature), so U distinct drawn powers give 2U queries, and at RFE's defaults the expected number of distinct settings is about 84.7. Exact readout observes every query once, on one controlled trajectory. SPE and RFE keep the original random-power draw multiplicities. A repeated sampled query receives the combined number of fresh shots. Each SPE or RFE query contributes its mean over the shots actually returned, weighted by its original random-power draw multiplicity. At least one valid shot is required for every count query. Under outcome-independent sampling with stationary independent shots, the expected weighted contribution is the multiplicity times the selected query signal, and its shot variance scales as the square of the multiplicity divided by the received population. The Result records the requested and received populations in `result.exposure`, one `QPEExposure` per count query with its `multiplicity`, `requested` and `received` shots, and `result.requested_exposure_complete` states whether every query returned its requested population. `result.minimum_effective_shots_per_draw` gives the smallest `received/multiplicity` over the queries as an exact (numerator, denominator) pair, and `result.rfe_coordinate_variance_upper` gives the received-count variance proxy of the sampled Fourier coefficients that the record describes. Sampling bounds use those populations and keep the uncertainty from drawing Fourier powers separate. Complete requested populations preserve the separate equal-shot draw distribution. A short return uses a different shot-noise distribution. The finite CDF crossing and Fourier peak do not provide an energy confidence interval. Measurements and seeds describe the pooled populations. Draw-level batch records require separate measurements. The public two-qubit checks count actual calls, verify multiplicities and check dense synthesis reuse without automatic reference projection. They do not establish mixed-spectrum accuracy or interval coverage.

For a trajectory realized by repeated controlled applications of one base unitary, the number of applications is the largest requested power. A dense gap-power realization has its own matrix-construction and native gate costs. Resource reports identify the selected realization and count each executed increment once. An exact QCELS, SPE or RFE `Plan` therefore makes one Aer submission, and its preparation record's G counts the native operations through the last point once.

`initial_state` is required on each Method for an Eigenproblem. SpectralEstimation already owns that input, so a second Method initialization is rejected. `seed` records separate PCG64 method/backend streams, and reanalysis does not draw from them. Post-hoc `result.analyze(grid_size=...)` changes the QCELS or SPE analysis grid using existing observations. RFE's K defines both the power distribution and Fourier frequencies, so changing it requires a new `Plan`. Sample counts, preparation and every RWPE controller setting also require a new one.

RFE uses the randomized Fourier estimator of [Kshirsagar, Katabarwa and Johnson, Sec. 2, Eqs. (5)–(7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11322v3#S2). A draw k contributes `z_k*exp(-2*pi*i*k*j/K)/M` to frequency j. Grouping the identical draws of one power into one exact or pooled observation replaces their terms by the draw multiplicity times that observation's term. Uniform draws make the result an unbiased estimate of the finite Fourier coefficient. The measured quadrature convention fixes `z_k=exp(i*k*phase)`, so postprocessing returns `2*pi*j/K` and Hamiltonian energy `-phase/tau`. The arXiv:2209.11322v3 note on p. 1 records that Eq. (4) and the output line of Algorithm 1 were corrected for a missing sign. With the paper's S gate before the final Hadamard, outcome 0 has probability `(1-sin(k*theta))/2`, so the raw imaginary outcome has mean `-sin(k*theta)` and the printed output is `2*pi-2*pi*j/K`. NWQLib's imaginary quadrature uses ancilla phase -pi/2 and measures `+Im z_k` directly, so its output needs no reflection. Work is O(UK) for the U distinct drawn powers, with O(K) workspace. These sizes depend on the selected estimator budget, not the Hilbert-space dimension.

The default K gives phase spacing `2*pi/49` and maximum power 48. M sets the random-draw count. Its 194 quadrature draws become one query per distinct (power, quadrature) in both modes, and shot mode requests 194 times `shots` coherent shots. The paper's precision theorem is Theorem 2.1 of arXiv:2209.11322v3, p. 6. For an eigenstate it gives an epsilon-accurate estimate with probability above 1-delta when `K >= ceil(2*pi/epsilon)` and `M >= ceil((81*pi**2/2)*ln(8*pi/(delta*epsilon)))` single-shot draws. For any epsilon < pi/2 and delta < 1 that bound exceeds 1100 draws, so the default M = 97 is a finite workload without the theorem's guarantee. A successful finite Fourier fit establishes neither the eigenstate premise nor the sample budget and supplies no RFE uncertainty interval. A dominant peak in a mixed spectrum need not represent its ground energy.

SPE uses the Fourier filter of [Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.12071v2), PDF Eqs. (A1)–(A3), corresponding to HTML Eqs. (16)–(18). Write `F_j=-i*a_j` for positive odd frequencies and `S=sum(a_j)`. Drawing j with probability `a_j/S` and measuring complex overlap z_j gives `1/2 + (2*S/M)*sum(Im(exp(i*j*x)*z_j))`. Negative frequencies follow by conjugation. Grouping repeated draws multiplies each exact or pooled contribution by its draw count. The terminal coefficient contains I_d alone, as specified by PDF Eq. (A2), HTML Eq. (17). Algorithm 1 instead samples j over all of `{0, ±1, ±3, ...}` in proportion to `|F_j|`. NWQLib keeps `F_0=1/2` exactly and pairs each positive draw with its conjugate, which also gives an unbiased estimate of the approximate CDF.

The declared overlap lower bound eta is a caller premise. Planning does not diagonalize an operator or expand a preparation to verify it. For the filter domain, Theorem 3 of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, uses epsilon1=epsilon2=eta/8 and epsilon3=eta/2 in PDF Eqs. (A6)–(A7) and (A12), HTML Eqs. (21)–(22) and (27). This gives a conditional filter error of at most 3eta/8 away from a transition half-width delta, leaving eta/8 below the eta/2 decision threshold for sampling error. The Result reports delta separately from grid spacing. These analytic bounds are evaluated in binary64 and do not include coefficient roundoff or controlled-evolution error. NWQLib scans a fixed grid using one shared sample set, which permits measurement-free reanalysis. The paper also reuses one sample set, but it makes only s = O(log(1/delta)) decisions chosen as in binary search (Sec. III, p. 4) and gives each decision failure probability xi/s, so a union bound limits the total failure probability of Theorem 1 to xi. A scan over G grid points would need failure probability xi/G per decision in Eq. (11). The default sample count is not derived from Eq. (11), so the search guarantee does not transfer. Degree and beta are tested against Theorem 3 as described above, and a failed premise appears in the Result. The paper's randomized LCU compiler and gate-complexity claim are also outside this controlled-evolution implementation.

RWPE implements the basic Gaussian update of [Granade and Wiebe, arXiv:2208.04526v1, Eq. (7) and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.04526v1). Its coordinate is the unwrapped phase `phi=-tau*E`. At step k, `sigma_k=prior_std*((e-1)/e)**(k/2)`, the relative evolution time is `1/sigma_k`, and one bit d changes the mean by `(1-2*d)*sigma_k/sqrt(e)`. The physical evolution is `exp(-i*tau*H/sigma_k)`, so a bare unitary with integer-power access is insufficient. All times are known from the prior and step count, while the feedback depends on previous bits. The controller update uses constant scalar workspace at every Hilbert-space dimension.

The Hadamard circuit uses feedback `-mean/sigma-pi/2`, giving `P(0)=(1+sin((phi-mean)/sigma))/2`. Integrating this likelihood against the Gaussian prior gives the positive d=0 mean shift in Eq. (7a) of arXiv:2208.04526v1, because `E[x*sin(x)]=exp(-1/2)` for a standard normal x. In the paper's likelihood, Eq. (2), `P(d|omega)=cos(t*(omega-omega_inv)/2+d*pi/2)**2`, this feedback is `omega_inv=mean+pi*sigma/2`. Algorithm 1 prints `omega_inv=mean-pi*sigma/2` alongside the same update, which gives `P(0)=(1-sin((omega-mean)/sigma))/2` and would lower the mean after d=0. At `t=1/sigma` the paper's own exact update, Eq. (6a), p. 4, reproduces Eq. (7a) with `omega_inv=mean+pi*sigma/2` and gives the opposite sign with `omega_inv=mean-pi*sigma/2`. The sentence that introduces Eq. (7) on p. 5 states `omega_inv=mean`, for which Eq. (6a) leaves the mean unchanged. The independent Gaussian-integral test fixes the sign from the circuit probability. This implementation omits Algorithm 2's consistency checks and unwinding. As discussed after Eq. (8), the basic walk has a finite exploration range. The mean can move at most `prior_std/(sqrt(e)-sqrt(e-1))`, about 2.96 prior widths (the paper prints 2.95), so shrinking Gaussian width alone does not establish convergence. The Result reports when the scaled spectral radius and prior mean reach that range.

`max_power=100000` and `max_trotter_steps=1000000` cap selected finite evolution work. `max_work=1000000000` bounds known numerical, schedule and controller size units, and `max_bytes=10_000_000_000` bounds known arrays. They do not bound LAPACK/SDK CPU or RSS. The RWPE controller charges 2 units when it starts and 32 for each choice and each update, and its last update follows the last measurement, so planning checks this whole charge, `2+64*max_steps` units, against `max_work` before the first query. On its product-formula route, RWPE also checks the independent exact recheck of all `max(1, max_steps)` selected relative times during planning. That recheck, the census, and the controller each have their own comparison against `max_work`, so the controller total alone does not fund every planning stage. On resume, an interrupted choice or update is charged again. Shared ExecutionLimits own actual preparation, measurement and data charges. Symbolic selected evolutions and Repeat remain compact until explicit native lowering.

QCELS fits `z_p ≈ a exp(-i p theta)` with `theta=E*tau`, and the raw-unitary phase is `-theta`. Eliminating `a=mean(z_p exp(i p theta))` (Ding–Lin arXiv:2211.11973v2, Eqs. (11)–(12)) reduces the complex least-squares objective in [Ding–Lin Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.11973v2#S1.E2) to one variable. The point estimator is dimensionless, so changing energy and time units together preserves its physical meaning for the same powers. The default integer schedule is independent of units. With supplied `max_time`, the schedule is preserved when the floored dimensionless ratios agree. Strict floor has discontinuities, and binary64 unit conversion at an integer boundary can change the final power by one. Inputs immediately below that boundary must remain below it, since no near-integer snapping or decimal reinterpretation is applied. `grid_size` is a requested minimum. The effective grid `G=max(grid_size, 8*(max(p)-min(p))+1)` covers one phase period with at least eight points per period of the fastest objective term. Every discrete local minimum receives `QCELS_BRACKET_EVALUATIONS=64` objective evaluations of golden-section search inside its adjacent periodic bracket, keeping all original grid candidates. This finite search is not a global-optimum theorem. A supplied `max_time` below one positive power is rejected. The QCELS accuracy results, Ding–Lin Theorems 1 and 2, assume `p0 > 0.71`, where `p0` is the squared overlap of the preparation with the target eigenvector. Smaller overlaps need their Fourier-filtered variant (Sec. IV), which this Method does not implement. NWQLib does not check `p0`, and it fits one schedule rather than the multi-level schedule of their Algorithm 1, whose times double as in their Eq. (35). Their data set, Eq. (6), uses consecutive powers 0, ..., N-1. When the maximum power exceeds `num_times`, NWQLib spreads `num_times` integer powers over 1 through that maximum, a schedule their theorems do not describe. If the powers with a nonzero summed signal share a common spacing g > 1, the objective has period 2*pi/g and the Result reports the aliasing. The planned schedule always contains powers 0 and 1, so this happens only when the signal vanishes at some powers. A power's summed signal counts as zero when it lies within the error that its accepted exact means and the summation allow, `sqrt(2)*mean_window*W` plus the rounding of the sum for W summed samples. `mean_window` is the largest `probability_window` of the preparation records for exact probabilities and `NUMERICAL_RELATION_RTOL` for classical execution, and count data use an exact-zero test ([Engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md)). Exact data whose signal vanishes in exact arithmetic therefore report aliasing or a flat objective despite their roundoff, and RFE applies the same test before it calls a sampled Fourier spectrum flat. A spread schedule can still make the objective nearly periodic, and the Result does not report that. For maximum power P its positive powers are `p_j=floor(1+j*gbar)` with `gbar=(P-1)/(num_times-1)`. At `theta+2*pi/gbar` each term `exp(i*p_j*theta)` turns by `2*pi*(1-f_j)/gbar`, where `f_j` is the fractional part of `1+j*gbar`, and the power-0 term does not turn. Every turn lies in `[0, 2*pi/gbar]`, about one radian for `gbar` near 6, so the rotated terms stay nearly aligned and the objective there keeps most of the depth of the minimum at theta. With a mixed spectrum, finite shots or evolution error, such a replica minimum can win the fit and move the energy by `2*pi/(gbar*tau)`. On the H4 problem of the eigenvalue example notebook (Hartree–Fock reference, `tau=0.2`/Ha, `num_times=32`), `max_time=38`/Ha gives P = 190 and `gbar` = 6.10, and the fit lands 5.156 Ha above the ground energy, next to the replica spacing `2*pi/(gbar*tau)=5.153` Ha, while 36 and 40/Ha stay within 7 mHa. Consecutive powers 0 through P, which the schedule uses when `num_times >= P`, have no such near period.

The analysis limit check counts at most `(1 + QCELS_BRACKET_EVALUATIONS)*G = 65*G` objective evaluations, the G grid points plus one bracket for each possible local minimum. Each evaluation is charged 32 scalar size units per complex point, so the QCELS law is `32*65*G*N` units plus 64 per sample for the reduction. Here `N` counts distinct complex points after quadrature reduction. These are conservative operation-size units, not measured CPU instructions. Grid and scores use O(G) storage and row temporaries use O(N), without a G×N matrix. Limits are checked before schedule construction/measurement and again before reanalysis. The default has `N=11`, `G=4096` and 22 quadrature queries. The default 100-million limit rejects this schedule before measurement:

```python
import numpy as np

result = solve(
    Eigenproblem(A=np.diag([.4, -.7])),
    method=QCELS(initial_state=[0, 1], tau=.2, num_times=16,
                 max_time=100),
    execution="classical", seed=7,
)
print(result.eigenvalue)  # approximately -.7 from the selected complex signal
print(result.fit)        # Stored effective grid, evaluations, residual and interval method.
refined = result.analyze(grid_size=4096)  # same observations, no acquisition
```

Increasing `grid_size` is checked against the original Method's limits. If the intended later analysis exceeds them, construct a new `Plan` with the needed limits before measurement. `result.analyze(grid_size=4096)` reuses the current observations within this example's `max_work`. No reanalysis measures new samples.

Limit-check errors report prospective work/bytes and the supplied `max_work` and `max_bytes`. The shared `NUMERICAL_RELATION_RTOL` mentioned above is the internal `nwqlib._validation` input convention, not a tunable Method accuracy option.

RWPE's planned times do not specify its future feedback points. Profile estimates and comparisons keep those points unpredicted, and a shot-population scan keeps the unresolved population unavailable. Forecasting does not select feedback or run the controller.

`execution="classical"` evaluates the selected estimator signal from an exact eigendecomposition and prepared spectral weights. Obtaining static signals takes one spectrum/projection and O(D) scalar work per unique power. Classical RWPE then emulates one Bernoulli datum from each obtained exact signal using the saved point seed. Its raw exact signal remains in the observation, and the emulated bit does not count as a quantum shot. RWPE and native dense powers share matching existing spectra within the Run. Actual setup/projection counts stay separate from conservative work declarations. Supplied native preparations are not implicitly expanded by classical execution. Structured Pauli input has no hidden dense classical route.

Exact static QPE trajectories use one common Suzuki step and cumulative step counts at their queried powers. Sampled QCELS, SPE and RFE settings prepare separate circuits and select a step count for each power independently. Both routes reuse one Pauli commutator census. Every selected power must fit its controlled-evolution allowance after adding pruning, product-formula error at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, leaf-angle formation and identity-phase discrepancy. Sampled reconstruction records each power's count and selected block, with no common-step record. The exact trajectory counts its step increments once, while resource estimates for sampled Plans multiply each setting's own count by its requested shots.

On the exact trajectory, nonnegative integer power p targets the exact time `t_p=p*val(tau)`, where `val(tau)` is the real value of the stored binary64 time unit. A displayed evolution time may round this product. Each power records its cumulative step count and a bound that includes pruning, the product formula at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, and applicable phase and rotation-angle formation errors. Planning checks that subtotal at every queried power. The identity term acts on the control through phase increments targeting `-c_I*(p_k-p_(k-1))*val(tau)`. Program contains the shared controlled evolution and its X/Y observation points. Sampled Hadamard settings include their own phase and final Hadamard readout tails. Native construction, inspection and logical folding consume those same choices. `prepare(plan).circuits` exposes the actual circuit of the setting that the Method submits first. `prepare(plan, settings="all")` exposes the circuits of every setting of a QCELS, SPE or RFE `Plan` on the local backends that [Prepared execution](../prepared_execution.md) names, and RWPE refuses it, because each feedback angle depends on the outcomes before it. Printing or estimating does not build another circuit.

Before the census, exact-trajectory product-formula planning sets aside a common-step work and logical-byte envelope from the powers, term count and input exponents. The census uses the full Pauli-triangle expression when its work plus that envelope fits `max_work` and each stage's bytes fit `max_bytes`. Otherwise it uses the second-order suffix relaxation, which needs quadratic pair work and can select more steps. The selected record names the expression and the outward coefficient arithmetic. For p terms on q qubits with $q\le64$, define $P=p(p-1)/2$ and $J=p(p-1)(2p-1)/6$. Charging each packed test and each magnitude-contribution visit once gives uniform census envelopes $2pq+2(P+J)$ for the full path and $2pq+3P+p$ for the relaxed path. With `max_work=100000000` and 20 system qubits, the census alone allows initial full and relaxed envelopes of at most 531 and 8151 terms. Sampled settings and RWPE set aside no common-step envelope. Their independent exact recheck is checked against `max_work` as its own stage before its exact arithmetic runs, with the work law in [Engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md).

On the product-formula route, default sampled schedules select 11 distinct powers for QCELS, at most 12 for SPE, and at most 49 for RFE. For K selected powers, including zero, and L kept nonidentity terms, the independent recheck admits `32L(K+1)C_T + (320K+128)C_S + 128K_a C_I <= max_work`, where `K_a` counts nonzero powers when L is positive and `C_T`, `C_S`, and `C_I` are the term, scalar, and candidate-inversion width costs defined in [Engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md). With costs bounded by `(1,27,100)`, a `max_work` of 100000000, a tenth of the default, admits this recheck throughout the default census term range, including schedules through 64 powers. At costs exactly `(8,27,100)` and that limit, an RFE schedule containing all 49 allowed powers admits at most 7,731 terms at this stage. Wider arithmetic can reduce that limit further. The actual coefficients, selected times, and pruning mass determine the widths, so K and L alone do not guarantee admission. Census and the other planning requirements remain separate checks.

An exact-trajectory `Plan` must also fit the common-step envelope determined by its power schedule, coefficient exponents, pruning loss, identity coefficient, time scale, per-power error budgets and step cap, so these census-only figures are not its term limits. Above the full-envelope threshold, an accepted relaxed initial envelope can still lead to a full census after the actual pair structure is known. Other planning work, storage and step limits also apply.

The exact trajectory's common-step selection checks exact arithmetic against the limits before constructing candidate counts. Its work charge uses the actual input exponent ranges and includes candidates later refused by `max_trotter_steps`. It computes the kept coefficient mass and one-step rotation-angle discrepancy once, then adds each emitted identity phase once to the exact running prefix. The arithmetic reductions take `O(L+K)` visits for `L` kept terms and `K` requested powers. The limit check also includes sorting, a conservative `K²` charge for integer-key collisions, and an integer-width factor. Both census checks include the same common-step envelope in `max_work`, whose default is `1_000_000_000`, so an accepted census variant also fits the later exact-arithmetic work limit. The full expression is preferred when the combined charge fits, otherwise planning uses the accepted relaxation. Each prefix is accepted only when its complete exact subtotal fits its error budget. `max_bytes` separately checks the logical live-object envelope of the exact stage.

RWPE independently selects a step count for each continuous power and checks the complete bound at the exact signed target time `val(p)*val(tau)`. Its `total_error` is the upward rounding of the exact sum of pruning, product-formula error at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, leaf-angle formation and the emitted identity-phase discrepancy. The step block's epsilon includes its own product-formula and leaf-angle terms. The complete subtotal must fit the controlled-evolution error budget, and a failed recheck refuses planning without retry. Native synthesis and execution roundoff remain outside this structural bound.

Each selected Suzuki step carries operation and CX laws for its bound terms and represented `step_time`. Common-grid power positions refer to that shared step and record cumulative step counts. In the `selected_logical` basis it stores 2L operations for L terms, one controlled Pauli rotation per term in the forward sweep of the symmetric formula and one in the reverse sweep. In the `cx` basis it costs `4*sum_j w_j` CX, where w_j is the number of nonidentity symbols of term j (`powers.suzuki_step_cx`). A controlled rotation of support w lowers to a parity chain and its inverse, 2(w-1) CX, around a controlled RZ of 2 CX. The CX count is exact for Qiskit 2.5.2 lowering to `cx` and one-qubit gates at optimization level 0 without a coupling map, before cancellation, rotation merging or routing. The identity phase P, the feedback RZ and the Hadamard gates are one operation each and cost no CX. With a preparation that has no CX, such as an occupation state, a Hamiltonian made only of the identity term therefore has an exact zero CX total. Repeat multiplies a step by the number of applications actually executed. A common-grid exact trajectory counts its increments through the final position once, while sampled experiments count their own preparations and shots. Thus `estimate(plan)` returns the whole-workload operation count and `estimate(plan, context=ResourceContext(basis="cx"))` its CX count. The whole-workload counts stay unavailable when the preparation has no applicable law. A dense power supplies neither law, so its operation and CX counts are unavailable.

Dense input uses `controlled_power_backend="dense_exact"` by default. A Hamiltonian power is the exact spectral power, formed from the shared eigendecomposition, so its selected block records algorithmic error 0. For a dense unitary input A, the selected base is its unitary polar factor V. Every nominal power and trajectory gap uses this same base. A gap of k powers is constructed by repeated squaring of V and realized as a controlled dense block. The represented target is V^p at power p. Polar selection, floating-point power formation and controlled synthesis contribute numerical error. Each gap's limit check covers its actual matrix work, synthesis and live cache storage. Its controlled matrix is synthesized as its unitary polar factor to binary64 rounding ([Controlled dense unitaries](../blocks.md#controlled-dense-unitaries)). A supplied unitary is accepted when every entry of U†U − I is at most 1e-8 in magnitude. Planning forms V once per `Plan` from one SVD, charged `9·D³ + 8·D²` units of planning work, and the classical route and verification diagonalize V, not A. The exact trajectory constructs one block per distinct positive gap between consecutive powers, and a sampled `Plan` one block per distinct nonzero power. Planning charges each block its matrix arithmetic in D³ units per product of two D-square matrices, where D is the dimension of the system register. A unitary gap or power k is charged the `bit_length(k) + popcount(k) - 2` products of an independent repeated squaring, although the squares of V are shared within the `Plan` and a later block forms only the squares it lacks. Its bytes include every cached square, `16·J·D²` for the largest square index J of the `Plan`'s exponents. A Hamiltonian power takes one product and is also charged the eigendecomposition, `8·D³ + 32·D²` units, because the first power that is constructed computes it and the others reuse it. Planning adds `11·8**m + (m² + 5m + 256)·4**m` units for the synthesis on m = n + 1 qubits with n system qubits. Each block is checked against `max_work` on its own, so the default `max_work` accepts dense blocks on up to six system qubits. The `Plan`'s total over its blocks is not compared with `max_work`.

## Intervals and verification

QCELS returns `interval=None` because the finite complex fit has no matched uncertainty model for the actual measurement. `result.fit` keeps its residual, effective grid and objective-evaluation count. A flat objective has no identified estimate. SPE and RFE also return `interval=None`. A CDF crossing or a Fourier peak does not supply a confidence interval. RWPE reports the nominal 95-percent interval of its moment-matched Gaussian model, with its phase mean and standard deviation in `result.gaussian`. The Gaussian approximation, prepared spectral population and controlled-evolution error limit its interpretation. It is not a frequentist coverage or total physical-error guarantee.

The `Plan`'s error model lists every QPE error source as unknown ([Error evidence](../error_evidence.md)). With exact readout, `result.facts` states a zero `sampling` error for QCELS, whose schedule is fixed, and for an RWPE Result that reports its prior without an update. SPE and RFE average over randomly drawn frequencies or powers, and each RWPE update uses one random bit, which classical execution emulates from the exact signal. Their estimates can therefore change with the seed even with exact readout. The sample-count conditions of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, Eq. (11), and of Kshirsagar, Katabarwa and Johnson, arXiv:2209.11322v3, Theorem 2.1, control this error, and NWQLib attaches no bound derived from them, so `result.assess(component="sampling", ...)` stays INCONCLUSIVE with `sampling` among the remaining sources.

`result.verify(checks=QPEVerification(tolerance=...))` explicitly computes a nominal spectrum/cluster comparison with a QR projector. SPE compares the lowest cluster and uses its declared overlap bound unless `minimum_overlap` is supplied. Other estimators compare the largest prepared cluster, with default minimum overlap .9. The comparison reports discrepancy, overlap, numerical adjustment, resolution and actual reference calls. It returns `(receipt, facts)`, where `facts` holds the two checked facts, `component_error` against `tolerance` in the output unit and `overlap_deficit = max(0, minimum_overlap - weight)` against zero, for `Certificate.with_verification` ([Verification](../verification.md#evidence-and-receipts)). `group_atol` clusters in the unit of the compared eigenvalues, the Problem's energy unit for a Hamiltonian and radians of eigenphase angle for a unitary, whatever the output. RWPE's reported method resolution is its Gaussian standard deviation in output units. `reuse_only=True` requires existing spectral and reference data whose target and preparation identities match, including after save/load. The saved arrays themselves are not rechecked ([Saved folders are read-only](../run_archives.md#saved-folders-are-read-only)). Supplied-circuit reference materialization separately requires `materialize_preparation=True`. These are explicit, bounded dense comparisons, and there is no automatic Pauli expansion.

For the static estimators, `method_resolution` is the Result's actual grid spacing in output units. It includes a later supported `analyze(grid_size=...)` call and is not a fit-error bound. RWPE uses its current Gaussian standard deviation instead. Neither quantity is a proven total-error bound, and verification leaves the original Result and `Plan` unchanged.

## Continuation and saved evidence

`prepare` binds only RWPE's actual next query. Before new work, the controller saves its Gaussian mean, processed-step count, evolution time, feedback, backend seed and pending checkpoint association. `submit` and `Run.wait` advance the same controller through the shared backend. Completed pending measurements update the Gaussian once. Unresolved intent is not resubmitted, and cancellation leaves actual charges visible.

The pre-submit boundary rejects known repeated fixed-seed streams from completed, uncollected, uncertain and same-batch measurements. Returned provider coordinates remain checked after completion. Re-reading the same completed measurement during recovery is legal, but it does not authorize another posterior update.

`result.save(path)` and `load_result(path)` preserve the concrete Method, selected evolutions, observations, Gaussian moments and existing numerical caches. Archives use `qpe/<estimator>/7`, which also store the selected polar base of a unitary input. QCELS keeps analysis source `qpe.qcels.analysis` version 3, and the other estimators use `qpe.analysis` version 3. Earlier development formats require their original source/environment. Archives reject a conflicting Method before loading state data. `run.save(path)` and `load_run(path, backend=...)` preserve the live frontier, RNG state and native caches. Loading performs no schedule search, eigensolve, estimator replay or native construction. Reanalysis shares unchanged data.
