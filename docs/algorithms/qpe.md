# QPE

<a id="quantum-phase-and-energy-estimation"></a>The QPE methods estimate an eigenvalue of a Hamiltonian $H$, or an eigenphase of a unitary $U$, from the signal $z_p=\langle\psi|U^p|\psi\rangle$ of a prepared state $\psi$, with $U=e^{-i\tau H}$ for a Hamiltonian. Real and imaginary Hadamard tests give $z_p$ (Ding and Lin, arXiv:2211.11973v2, Fig. 1 and Eqs. (4)–(5)). Each method estimates a different quantity.

- `QCELS` fits one complex exponential $z_p\approx ae^{-ip\theta}$ at integer powers $p$ and returns the energy $\theta/\tau$ (Ding and Lin, arXiv:2211.11973v2, Eqs. (2) and (9)–(12), [Result 3](../mathematics.md#r3)).
- `SPE`, statistical phase estimation, returns the lowest energy in the prepared spectral support, at the first point where a filtered estimate of the spectral cumulative distribution crosses half a declared lower bound $\eta$ on the target component's probability (Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, Eqs. (2) and (6) and Algorithm 1, [Result 4](../mathematics.md#r4)).
- `RFE`, randomized Fourier estimation, draws powers uniformly, forms sampled Fourier coefficients of the signal at $K$ frequencies $j=0,\dots,K-1$ and returns the grid angle $2\pi j/K$ of the frequency whose sampled coefficient has the largest magnitude (Kshirsagar, Katabarwa and Johnson, arXiv:2209.11322v3, Eqs. (5)–(7) and Algorithm 1, [Result 4](../mathematics.md#r4)).
- `RWPE`, random-walk phase estimation, updates a Gaussian estimate of the phase from one measured bit per step and reports its mean with a Gaussian interval (Granade and Wiebe, arXiv:2208.04526v1, Eq. (7) and Algorithm 1, [Result 5](../mathematics.md#r5)).

The preparation determines which part of the spectrum is observed. A single-mode estimate establishes neither that the value is the ground energy nor that it covers the dominant component. The four methods share the controlled-power construction and the execution steps. For the lowest eigenvalue in a trial space built from one initial state, use [Chebyshev Lanczos](lanczos.md), and for supplied or adaptively grown trial states, use [GCiM](gcim.md).

```python
from nwqlib import Eigenproblem, solve
from nwqlib.algorithms.qpe import QCELS, SPE, RFE, RWPE

problem = Eigenproblem(A=[[0.2, 0.0], [0.0, 0.7]])
result = solve(problem, method=QCELS(initial_state=[1, 0]), seed=7)
print(result.eigenvalue, result.phase)
```

```text
0.20000000000000012 0.8714285714285713
```

The prepared state $(1,0)$ is the eigenvector of eigenvalue 0.2, and `result.phase` is the same eigenvalue as an eigenphase of $U=e^{-i\tau H}$ in turns ([Read the result](#meaning-of-the-result)). This default QCELS example runs a two-qubit circuit on the Aer simulator. Other choices include `SPE(initial_state=[1, 0], overlap_lower_bound=1.)`, `RFE(initial_state=[1, 0])` and `RWPE(initial_state=[1, 0])`. Replace `A` and `initial_state` to treat your own problem. The eigenvalue notebook (`examples/gcim_lanczos_qpe_eigenvalue_intro.ipynb`) applies QCELS to a stretched H4 chain together with ADAPT-GCiM and Lanczos.

## Inputs

The input is either an `Eigenproblem` together with `initial_state` on the method, or a `SpectralEstimation`, which holds its Hamiltonian or unitary and its initial state. With an `Eigenproblem`, each method requires `initial_state`. A `SpectralEstimation` already holds its initial state, so the method then rejects an `initial_state` of its own. Changing the method's `initial_state` does not change the target of the `Eigenproblem`. An initial state that is an excited eigenstate can return that excited energy while the problem still asks for the smallest eigenvalue, and the Result states this limitation. RWPE needs a Hamiltonian, because its evolution times are not integer powers of a unitary ([RWPE](#rwpe)).

A small dense Hamiltonian $A$ whose dimension $d$ is not a power of two is padded to the next power of two with a Hermitian dummy block with diagonal entries $\operatorname{tr}(A)/d$, and the initial state is padded with zeros. The Result still refers to the original problem and initial state. A unitary is padded with an identity block. Scalar targets use one control qubit and no system register or state-preparation block.

Dense input uses `controlled_power_backend="dense_exact"` by default. A Hamiltonian power is the exact spectral power, formed from one eigendecomposition shared by all powers, so the algorithmic error recorded for its controlled block is 0. For a dense unitary input $A$, the powers are built from its unitary polar factor $V$. Every requested power, and every gap between consecutive powers of the controlled trajectory ([How the signal is measured](#how-the-signal-is-measured)), is a power of this same $V$, so the target operator at power $p$ is $V^p$. A supplied dense unitary $A$ of dimension $D$ is checked with the computed singular values of the one SVD that also forms $V$. It is accepted when $\|A^\dagger A-I\|_2\le10^{-8}$, refused when $\|A^\dagger A-I\|_2>D\cdot10^{-8}$, and in between accepted exactly when every entry of $A^\dagger A-I$ is at most $10^{-8}$ in magnitude. Every entry of a matrix is at most its spectral norm, which is at most $D$ times its largest entry, so the decision is that of the entrywise rule, except for an input whose defect lies within the rounding error of $10^{-8}$ or $D\cdot10^{-8}$. The value $10^{-8}$ is a screening window for matrices that are not unitary. The accuracy statement concerns $V$. QPE estimates the eigenphases of $V$, and for $\eta=\|A^\dagger A-I\|_2<1$ the distance from $A$ to $V$ satisfies $\|A-V\|_2\le\eta/(1+\sqrt{1-\eta})$. The polar decomposition, the floating-point matrix powers and the synthesis of the controlled gates contribute numerical error. Each controlled matrix is synthesized as its unitary polar factor to binary64 rounding ([Controlled dense unitaries](../development/dense_synthesis.md#controlled-dense-unitaries)). With an explicit `max_work=1_000_000_000`, each dense block fits on up to seven system qubits ([Numerical scope and planning limits](#numerical-scope-and-planning-limits)).

## Read the result {#meaning-of-the-result}

`result.phase` is in turns, in $[0,1)$, with $Uv=e^{2\pi i\,\mathrm{phase}}v$. For a Hamiltonian, $U=e^{-i\tau H}$, so `result.eigenvalue` is in the original matrix units and includes the minus sign of the conversion. Unitary-only input has no Hamiltonian eigenvalue without a chosen logarithm branch, and accessing that field raises `AttributeError`. `result.value` is `phase` for an `Eigenphase` output and `eigenvalue` otherwise.

SPE returns the lowest value in the prepared spectral support. For a Hamiltonian that is the lowest energy. For a unitary it is the lowest principal eigenphase angle $\theta$, with $Uv=e^{i\theta}v$ and $\theta$ scanned on $[-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. When $\tau\lvert E\rvert<\pi/2$ for every prepared energy $E$ of a unitary $U=e^{-i\tau H}$, $\theta=-\tau E$, so SPE on $U$ returns the phase of the highest energy of the prepared support. Supply $H$ itself, or $U^\dagger=e^{i\tau H}$, to target the lowest energy.

`estimator_value` is the value the estimator computes, an energy for a Hamiltonian and a principal eigenphase in radians for a unitary. The phase and the interval are converted from it once. For an `Eigenphase` output from a Hamiltonian, the energy endpoints of the interval are multiplied by $-\tau/(2\pi)$, which reverses their order, and the interval is recentered around the reported phase. For an `Eigenvalue` output, the interval stays in energy units. The interval is not wrapped, because wrapping each endpoint separately would change its meaning. It is the estimator's own interval and neither bounds the total error nor identifies the ground state.

Exact readout, the default of QCELS, SPE and RFE when `shots` is not given, computes each ancilla expectation without shot sampling, on the simulator or by classical execution, subject to numerical rounding. For quantum execution with exact readout, each saved ancilla expectation of the executed trajectory must have magnitude at most 1 up to a binary64 roundoff window, or the observation is rejected. The window bounds roundoff in the saved expectation and is not an accuracy bound for the estimate ([Readout tolerance branches](../error_evidence.md#readout-tolerance-branches)). It does not cover the operations or conditions that the circuit's [preparation record](../glossary.md#preparation-record), the saved record of the circuit as it was built with its operation counts, lists as [roundoff exclusions](../glossary.md#roundoff-exclusion), because the roundoff derivation does not treat them.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `eigenvalue`, `phase`, `value` | The energy in the original units, the eigenphase of $U$ in turns, and the requested output, as described above. |
| `estimator_value` | Energy in operator units for a Hamiltonian, or principal eigenphase in radians for a unitary. None when no estimate was identified. |
| `interval` | RWPE's nominal model interval, None for the other estimators ([Intervals and sampling error](#intervals-and-verification)). |
| `fit` | QCELS search record: effective grid, objective evaluations, residual and interval method. |
| `gaussian` | RWPE's Gaussian phase mean and standard deviation. |
| `exposure` | One `QPEExposure` per sampled SPE or RFE query, that is, per measured (power, quadrature) setting, with its draw `multiplicity` and its `requested` and `received` shots. |
| `requested_exposure_complete` | Whether every sampled query returned its requested shots. |
| `minimum_effective_shots_per_draw` | The smallest `received/multiplicity` over the queries, as an exact (numerator, denominator) pair. |
| `rfe_coordinate_variance_upper` | An upper bound, in units of squared ancilla expectation, on the received-count variance proxy of each real and imaginary coordinate of the sampled Fourier coefficients. It concerns the conditional shot contribution under independent stationary shots, not the random-draw term, and gives no energy confidence interval. |
| `stop_reason`, `missing`, `complete` | Why measurement stopped or no estimate is identified (for QCELS, also that the fit is aliased), the planned queries without data, and whether all planned data and an estimate are present (for RWPE, whether every planned step was processed). |

Each sampled SPE or RFE query contributes its mean over the shots returned, weighted by its draw multiplicity, the number of times the random schedule drew its power, and every sampled query needs at least one valid shot. Under outcome-independent sampling with stationary independent shots, the expected weighted contribution is the multiplicity times the query signal, and its shot variance scales as the square of the multiplicity divided by the received shots. Sampling bounds use these shot numbers and keep the uncertainty from drawing Fourier powers separate. When every query receives its requested shots, the pooled data have the distribution of separate draws measured with equal shots, while a query that returns fewer shots has a different shot-noise distribution. The finite cumulative-distribution crossing and the Fourier peak do not provide an energy confidence interval. The recorded measurements and seeds describe the pooled shots of each query, and a record for each individual draw would require separate measurements. NWQLib's two-qubit tests count the circuit submissions to Aer and their shots, verify multiplicities and check dense synthesis reuse without computing reference spectral weights. They do not establish mixed-spectrum accuracy or interval coverage.

## Intervals and sampling error {#intervals-and-verification}

QCELS returns `interval=None`, because the finite complex fit has no matched uncertainty model for the measurement. `result.fit` keeps its residual, effective grid and objective-evaluation count, and a flat objective has no identified estimate. SPE and RFE also return `interval=None`, because a cumulative-distribution crossing or a Fourier peak does not supply a confidence interval. RWPE reports the nominal 95-percent interval of its moment-matched Gaussian model, with its phase mean and standard deviation in `result.gaussian`. The Gaussian approximation, the prepared spectral weights and controlled-evolution error limit its interpretation. It is not a frequentist coverage or total physical-error guarantee.

The error model of the [`Plan`](../glossary.md#plan), the construction and its costs computed before any circuit exists, lists every QPE error source as unknown ([How a PASS is reached](../verification.md#how-a-pass-is-reached)). With exact readout, `result.facts` states a zero `sampling` error for QCELS, whose schedule is fixed, and for an RWPE Result that reports its prior without an update. SPE and RFE average over randomly drawn frequencies or powers, and each RWPE update uses one random bit, which classical execution emulates from the exact signal. Their estimates can therefore change with the seed even with exact readout. The sample-count conditions of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, Eq. (11), and of Kshirsagar, Katabarwa and Johnson, arXiv:2209.11322v3, Theorem 2.1, control this error. NWQLib attaches no bound derived from them, so `result.assess(component="sampling", ...)` stays INCONCLUSIVE with `sampling` among the remaining sources.

## Settings and measurement work {#scientific-choices-and-actual-cost}

| Method | Estimator controls and defaults | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `QCELS` | `num_times=16`, `max_time=None`, `grid_size=4096` | Integer powers plus zero and paired quadratures. `None` selects maximum power 10. A supplied `max_time` selects `floor(max_time/tau)` (unitary: `floor(max_time)`). Complex least squares uses a finite grid with bounded bracket refinement. |
| `SPE` | Required `overlap_lower_bound`, `num_samples=32`, `fourier_degree=11`, `filter_beta=6`, `grid_size=4096` | Sample positive odd Fourier frequencies proportional to their coefficient magnitudes, measure paired quadratures, and locate the first crossing of $\eta/2$ by the approximate cumulative distribution. |
| `RFE` | `num_samples=97`, `num_frequencies=49` | Draw M independent uniform powers from 0 through K-1, measure both quadratures for each draw, and select the largest sampled Fourier coefficient. |
| `RWPE` | `max_steps=14`, `prior_mean=0`, `prior_std=pi` | Basic Gaussian random walk with one datum per continuous-time experiment. Zero steps reports the prior without measurement. |

In the RFE row, M is `num_samples` and K is `num_frequencies`. Each method also accepts the shared controls `tau` (default `None`, chosen as [Time step and energy range](#time-step-and-energy-range) describes), `controlled_power_backend` (default `"auto"`, which picks `"trotter_error_budgeted"` for Pauli input and `"dense_exact"` otherwise), `controlled_power_error_budget` (default `1e-3`, the operator-norm error allowed for each power) and `pauli_pruning_rtol` (default `1e-12`). SPE's maximum Fourier frequency is `2*fourier_degree+1`. `max_power` and `max_trotter_steps` default to `None`, no limit. When set, `max_power` caps the absolute evolution time in units of tau, including RWPE's noninteger times, and `max_trotter_steps` caps the product-formula steps of each power ([Numerical scope and planning limits](#numerical-scope-and-planning-limits)).

QCELS, SPE and RFE, the static methods, fix their measurement schedule when the `Plan` is made and by default use exact readout of one controlled trajectory ([How the signal is measured](#how-the-signal-is-measured)). A positive `shots` sets the shots per elementary QCELS setting and per random SPE or RFE draw, so an SPE or RFE setting drawn m times receives m times `shots`, and backend limits on shots per circuit apply to that combined number. RWPE uses one shot per feedback step and defaults to 14 steps. It rejects a different requested shot count, because its update is defined for one Bernoulli datum.

The default shot workload is 22 elementary settings for QCELS and 14 one-shot measurements for RWPE. SPE and RFE draw 32 and 97 paired frequencies respectively, so their 64 and 194 quadrature draws request 64 and 194 times `shots` coherent shots. SPE and RFE make one query per distinct drawn (power, quadrature), so U distinct drawn powers give 2U queries, and at RFE's defaults the expected number of distinct settings is about 84.7. With quantum execution and exact readout, every query is observed once, on one controlled trajectory. A repeated sampled query receives the combined number of fresh shots.

With quantum execution, RWPE plans `max_steps` circuit preparations, measurements and one-shot observations, and it checks all three totals before preparing the first feedback query. The caller can cancel the [Run](../glossary.md#run), the execution of the `Plan` that records its submissions and observations and can be saved and reopened, before all steps are done. The feedback angle depends on the preceding outcomes, so only the next circuit is prepared at a time.

When a trajectory repeats controlled applications of one base unitary, the number of applications is the largest requested power. A trajectory of dense gap powers has its own matrix-construction and gate costs. Resource reports name the construction and count each executed increment once. A quantum QCELS, SPE or RFE `Plan` with exact readout is therefore submitted to Aer once, and the operation count in its preparation record counts the executed circuit operations through the last measurement point once.

### Classical execution {#classical-execution}

`execution="classical"` evaluates the estimator signal from an exact eigendecomposition and the prepared spectral weights. The signals of the static methods take one eigendecomposition, one projection of the initial state and $O(D)$ scalar work per distinct power. Classical RWPE then emulates one Bernoulli datum from each exact signal with the seed saved for that step. The exact signal remains in the observation, and the emulated bit does not count as a quantum shot. Within a Run, RWPE and the dense powers built for circuits reuse an eigendecomposition already computed for the same operator. The classical routine (a host kernel in the code) records how many eigendecompositions and projections it performed, separately from the conservative work estimate declared before it runs. Classical execution expands neither a supplied preparation circuit nor Pauli input into dense form, and it rejects both.

For unitary input the signal at power $p$ is $z_p=\sum_jw_je^{ip\theta_j}$, where $w_j$ is the prepared spectral weight and $\theta_j$ the angle of the computed eigenvalue $\lambda_j$. A computed eigenvalue of a unitary has the form $\lambda_j=(1+\eta_j)e^{i\theta_j}$, and forming $\lambda_j^p$ would carry the rounding of its modulus as $(1+\eta_j)^p$, about $p\eta_j$ above one while that product is small. Using the angle alone removes this radial factor and gives $\lvert z_p\rvert\le1$ for $w_j\ge0$ and $\sum_jw_j=1$. Rounding of the weight sum, of the phases and of the off-diagonal part of the Schur form remains, and so does any departure of the input from a unitary. This form therefore does not establish the accuracy of the signal at a large power. In particular, the window that [When a signal counts as zero](#qcels-zero-signal) uses for a classical signal is not established at a large power of a prepared state with several eigencomponents.

### Inspect the circuits and their resources {#inspect-the-circuits-and-their-resources}

`prepare(plan).circuits` exposes the circuit of the setting that the method submits first. `prepare(plan, settings="all")` exposes the circuits of every setting of a QCELS, SPE or RFE `Plan` on the local backends that [Run on a backend](../prepared_execution.md) names. RWPE rejects it, because each feedback angle depends on the outcomes before it. Printing or estimating does not build another circuit.

Each Suzuki step records its operation and CX counts for the Pauli terms kept after pruning and for its `step_time`. On the common grid of an exact trajectory, each power position refers to that shared step and records its cumulative step count. In the `selected_logical` basis a step counts 2L operations for L terms, one controlled Pauli rotation per term in the forward sweep of the symmetric formula and one in the reverse sweep. In the `cx` basis it costs $4\sum_jw_j$ CX, where $w_j$ is the number of nonidentity symbols of term $j$ (`powers.suzuki_step_cx`, [Proposition 7](../mathematics.md#r7)). A controlled rotation of support w becomes a parity chain and its inverse, 2(w-1) CX, around a controlled RZ of 2 CX. The CX count is exact for Qiskit 2.6.0 decomposition into `cx` and one-qubit gates at optimization level 0 without a coupling map, before cancellation, rotation merging or routing. The identity phase P, the feedback RZ and the Hadamard gates are one operation each and cost no CX. With a preparation that has no CX, such as an occupation state, a Hamiltonian made only of the identity term therefore has an exact zero CX total. The counts of a repeated step are multiplied by the number of times it is executed. A common-grid exact trajectory counts its increments through the final position once, while sampled experiments count their own preparations and shots. Thus `estimate(plan)` returns the whole-workload operation count and `estimate(plan, context=ResourceContext(basis="cx"))` its CX count. The whole-workload counts stay unavailable when the initial-state preparation has no counting formula. A dense power supplies neither formula, so its operation and CX counts are unavailable.

## Time step and energy range {#time-step-and-energy-range}

For static Hamiltonian queries, integer powers identify energy only modulo $2\pi/\tau$. The automatic choice of $\tau$ uses a bound on the spectrum that needs no eigensolve. Without a supplied `tau`, planning chooses $\tau=0.9\,r/R$, where $r$ is the principal radius of the estimator, stated below, and $R$ is the dense row-sum bound or the Pauli L1 norm. The factor 0.9 is an untuned default that sets a nominal target with a 10 percent margin against aliasing. For a dense Hamiltonian of dimension $D$, the computed row sums and the rounded quotient can put $\tau R$ above $0.9\,r$ by a relative $O(Du)$, with unit roundoff $u=2^{-53}$, and the margin keeps $\tau|E|<r$. When a dense row sum overflows binary64, $R$ is not recorded, $\tau$ is a finite positive planning value, and the `Plan` does not claim that aliasing is excluded (`aliasing_bound_sufficient` is False). Subnormal entries give a time above the binary64 maximum, which is clamped to that maximum. The clamped $\tau$ is smaller, so $\tau|E|$ stays within the margin, but the eigenphases need not be resolved. A unitary has no $\tau$. QCELS and RFE use a principal radius of $\pi$. SPE also needs a filter transition margin $\delta$, since its scan evaluates $F(x-\tau E)$ near the periodic wrap boundary. A sufficient condition is $\tau R+\delta\le\pi/2$, where $R$ bounds the spectrum, and SPE's automatic radius is $\min(\pi/3,\pi/2-\delta)$, followed by the common 0.9 interior margin. Theorem 3 of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, supplies $\delta$ from beta and the declared overlap, and its degree assumption is checked separately. An unavailable assumption or a supplied time outside that domain appears in the Result. A crossing at the scan boundary is unidentified and is not reported as an endpoint energy. For raw-unitary input, SPE has no established interval that encloses the prepared spectrum. RWPE uses continuous times and a Gaussian prior, whose finite exploration range ([RWPE](#rwpe)) is checked against the same spectral bound $R$.

## How the signal is measured {#how-the-signal-is-measured}

With quantum execution and exact readout, the static methods prepare the control and system once and then apply one controlled trajectory, a sequence of controlled increments of $U$ whose cumulative powers reach each planned power in turn. The ancilla X and Y expectations at each power position give the real and imaginary parts of $z_p$, with $\langle Y_a\rangle=\operatorname{Im}z_p$. Sampled X and Y settings use separate preparations, and RWPE follows its feedback-dependent measurement schedule. SPE and RFE keep the draw multiplicity of each power. Each query records the experiment that measures it and, on the trajectory, its measurement point, which the real and imaginary queries of one power share. When a point has no data, its queries remain missing, and neither analysis nor loading reruns part of the trajectory to replace them.

For a Pauli-sum Hamiltonian the controlled powers are product formulas. Exact static QPE trajectories use one ordered common Suzuki step on an integer time grid whose bounds meet every requested power's error budget, and the `Plan` records cumulative step counts and per-power errors. Sampled QCELS, SPE and RFE settings prepare separate circuits and choose a step count for each power independently, and the `Plan` records each power's count and block, with no common step. Both routes reuse one count of Pauli commutators. Every power must fit its controlled-evolution error budget (`controlled_power_error_budget`) after adding pruning, product-formula error at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, rotation-angle formation and identity-phase discrepancy. The exact trajectory counts its step increments once, while resource estimates for sampled `Plan`s multiply each setting's own count by its requested shots.

On the exact trajectory, the nonnegative integer power p targets the exact time $t_p=p\cdot\mathrm{val}(\tau)$, where $\mathrm{val}(\tau)$ is the real number that the binary64 time unit $\tau$ represents. A displayed evolution time may round this product. Each power records its cumulative step count and a bound that includes pruning, the product formula at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, and applicable phase and rotation-angle formation errors. Planning checks that subtotal at every queried power. The identity term acts on the control through phase increments targeting $-c_I(p_k-p_{k-1})\,\mathrm{val}(\tau)$, because removing it would change the measured interference signal ([Proposition 7](../mathematics.md#r7)). The circuit description contains the shared controlled evolution and its X and Y measurement points. Each sampled Hadamard setting ends with its own phase gate and final Hadamard before measurement, and circuit construction, inspection and resource totals use these same gates.

RWPE independently chooses a step count for each continuous power and checks the complete bound at the exact signed target time $\mathrm{val}(p)\,\mathrm{val}(\tau)$. Its `total_error` is the upward rounding of the exact sum of pruning, product-formula error at the represented step time, represented-time displacement, an a priori bound on rotation-angle formation and the discrepancy of the identity phase that the circuit uses. The angle bound follows from the halving of the step and the one multiplication that form each angle, so it needs no per-angle rational arithmetic. The step block's epsilon includes its own product-formula and rotation-angle terms. The complete subtotal must fit the controlled-evolution error budget. The first count inverts only the product-formula term, so the time and angle terms can push its subtotal above the budget once the margin is small. Planning then proposes two more counts from a conservative envelope of the terms that do not depend on the step count, which takes at most two scalar evaluations and no new commutator count, and it accepts a count only when its complete subtotal fits the budget. Otherwise it refuses, and the message names the power and each contribution and asks for a larger `controlled_power_error_budget`, or for rescaled units when a time or step falls below the normal binary64 range. Such a refusal shows that this rule confirmed no count, not that no count exists. The powers of sampled QCELS, SPE and RFE settings follow the same rule. Circuit synthesis and execution roundoff remain outside this structural bound.

## QCELS

### How the fit works {#qcels-fit}

QCELS fits $z_p\approx ae^{-ip\theta}$ with $\theta=E\tau$, and the raw-unitary phase is $-\theta$. Eliminating $a=\operatorname{mean}_p(z_pe^{ip\theta})$ (Ding–Lin arXiv:2211.11973v2, Eqs. (11)–(12)) reduces the complex least-squares objective in [Ding–Lin Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.11973v2#S1.E2) to one variable ([Result 3](../mathematics.md#r3)). `grid_size` is a requested minimum. The effective grid `G=max(grid_size, 8*(max(p)-min(p))+1)` covers one phase period with at least eight points per period of the fastest objective term. Every discrete local minimum receives `QCELS_BRACKET_EVALUATIONS=64` objective evaluations of golden-section search inside its adjacent periodic bracket, and the estimate is the best point among all grid points and all refined points. This finite search does not guarantee the global minimum.

### When the accuracy theorems apply {#qcels-validity}

Ding–Lin's accuracy results, Theorems 1 and 2 (arXiv:2211.11973v2, p. 14), assume $p_0>0.71$, where $p_0$ is the squared overlap of the preparation with the target eigenvector. NWQLib does not check $p_0$. Smaller overlaps need their Fourier-filtered variant (Sec. IV, p. 15), which this method does not implement. NWQLib fits one schedule rather than the multi-level schedule of their Algorithm 1, whose times double as in their Eq. (35). Their data set, Eq. (6), uses the consecutive powers $0,\ldots,N-1$. When the maximum power exceeds `num_times`, NWQLib spreads `num_times` integer powers over 1 through that maximum, a schedule their theorems do not describe.

### Choose num_times and max_time {#qcels-schedule}

With `max_time=None` the maximum power is 10. A supplied `max_time` $T$ gives the maximum power $P=\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor$, or $\lfloor T\rfloor$ for a unitary, and a `max_time` below one positive power is rejected. When `num_times` is at least $P$, the schedule holds the consecutive powers 0 through $P$. Otherwise it keeps power 0 and spreads `num_times` integer powers over 1 through $P$.

The point estimator is dimensionless, so changing energy and time units together preserves its physical meaning for the same powers. The default integer schedule is independent of units. With a supplied `max_time`, the schedule is preserved when the floored dimensionless ratios agree. The strict floor has discontinuities, and binary64 unit conversion at an integer boundary can change the final power by one. Inputs immediately below that boundary must remain below it, since no near-integer snapping or decimal reinterpretation is applied.

!!! warning "A spread schedule can fit a replica of the energy"

    A spread schedule can make the objective nearly periodic, and the Result does not report that. For maximum power $P$, its positive powers are $p_j=\lfloor1+j\bar g\rfloor$ with $\bar g=(P-1)/(N_t-1)$, where $N_t$ is `num_times`. At $\theta+2\pi/\bar g$ each term $e^{ip_j\theta}$ turns by $2\pi(1-f_j)/\bar g$, where $f_j$ is the fractional part of $1+j\bar g$, and the power-0 term does not turn. Every turn lies in $[0,2\pi/\bar g]$, about one radian for $\bar g$ near 6, so the rotated terms stay nearly aligned and the objective there keeps most of the depth of the minimum at $\theta$. With a mixed spectrum, finite shots or evolution error, such a replica minimum can win the fit and move the energy by $2\pi/(\bar g\tau)$. On the H4 problem of the eigenvalue example notebook (Hartree–Fock reference, $\tau=0.2$/Ha, `num_times=32`), `max_time=38`/Ha gives $P=190$ and $\bar g=6.10$, and the fit lands 5.156 Ha above the ground energy, next to the replica spacing $2\pi/(\bar g\tau)=5.153$ Ha, while 36 and 40/Ha stay within 7 mHa ([Measured evidence](#measured-evidence)). Consecutive powers 0 through $P$, which the schedule uses when `num_times` $\ge P$, have no such near period.

### Aliasing report {#qcels-aliasing}

If the powers with a nonzero summed signal share a common spacing $g>1$, the objective has period $2\pi/g$ and the Result reports the aliasing. The planned schedule always contains powers 0 and 1, so this happens only when the signal vanishes at some powers.

### When a signal counts as zero {#qcels-zero-signal}

A power's summed signal counts as zero when it lies within the error that its exact means and the summation allow, `sqrt(2)*mean_window*W` plus the rounding of the sum for $W$ summed samples. `mean_window` is the largest `probability_window` of the preparation records for exact probabilities and `NUMERICAL_RELATION_RTOL` for classical execution, and count data use an exact-zero test ([Engineering constants](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md)). Exact data whose signal vanishes in exact arithmetic therefore report aliasing or a flat objective despite their roundoff, and RFE applies the same test before it calls a sampled Fourier spectrum flat.

Post-hoc `result.analyze(grid_size=...)` changes the QCELS or SPE analysis grid using the existing observations, and no reanalysis measures new samples. A changed `grid_size` is checked against the limits of the original method, before the analysis runs. `grid_size` is the only setting that reanalysis accepts, so `result.analyze(max_bytes=...)` is refused. When a larger grid exceeds a limit, a smaller `grid_size` still works on the same observations, and a larger limit needs a new `Plan`, with new measurements. `result.analyze(grid_size=8192)` reuses the current observations within this example's `max_work`. RFE's K defines both the power distribution and the Fourier frequencies, so changing it requires a new `Plan`, as do sample counts, the initial state and every RWPE setting. `seed` records separate PCG64 streams for the method and the backend, and reanalysis does not draw from them.

## SPE

SPE uses the Fourier filter of [Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.12071v2), PDF Eqs. (A1)–(A3), corresponding to HTML Eqs. (16)–(18). Write $F_j=-ia_j$ for its Fourier coefficient at each positive odd frequency $j$, where $a_j=|F_j|$, and let $S=\sum_ja_j$. Drawing $M$ frequencies $j_1,\ldots,j_M$ with probabilities $a_j/S$ and measuring their complex overlaps gives the estimate $\widehat C(x)=1/2+(2S/M)\sum_{m=1}^M\operatorname{Im}(e^{ij_mx}z_{j_m})$ of the approximate cumulative distribution at the dimensionless threshold $x$. For a Hamiltonian threshold energy $E$, $x=\tau E$ and $z_j$ estimates $\langle\psi|e^{-ij\tau H}|\psi\rangle$, with the Hamiltonian $H$, prepared state $\psi$ and time step $\tau$ defined above. For raw-unitary input, $x$ is the phase threshold and the formula uses the complex conjugate of the measured overlap. Negative frequencies follow by conjugation. Grouping repeated draws multiplies each exact or pooled contribution by its draw count. The terminal coefficient at frequency $2d+1$ contains $I_d(\beta)$ alone, where $d$ is `fourier_degree`, $\beta$ is `filter_beta` and $I_d$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order $d$, as specified by PDF Eq. (A2), HTML Eq. (17). Algorithm 1 instead samples $j$ over all of $\{0,\pm1,\pm3,\ldots\}$ in proportion to $\lvert F_j\rvert$. NWQLib keeps $F_0=1/2$ exactly and pairs each positive draw with its conjugate, which also gives an unbiased estimate of the approximate cumulative distribution under unbiased overlap measurements.

The declared overlap lower bound $\eta$ is an assumption supplied by the caller. Planning does not diagonalize an operator or expand a preparation to verify it. For the filter domain, Theorem 3 of Wan, Berta and Campbell, arXiv:2110.12071v2, uses $\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2=\eta/8$ and $\epsilon_3=\eta/2$ in PDF Eqs. (A6)–(A7) and (A12), HTML Eqs. (21)–(22) and (27). This gives a conditional filter error of at most $3\eta/8$ away from a transition half-width $\delta$, leaving $\eta/8$ below the $\eta/2$ decision threshold for sampling error. The Result reports $\delta$ separately from the grid spacing. These analytic bounds are evaluated in binary64 and do not include coefficient roundoff or controlled-evolution error. Degree and beta are tested against Theorem 3, and a failed assumption appears in the Result.

NWQLib scans a fixed grid using one shared sample set, which permits reanalysis without measurement. The paper also reuses one sample set, but it makes only $s=O(\log(1/\delta))$ decisions chosen as in binary search (Sec. III, p. 4), where $\delta$ is the transition half-width defined above, and gives each decision failure probability $\xi/s$, where $\xi$ is the paper's total failure probability, so a union bound limits the total failure probability of Theorem 1 to $\xi$. A scan over $G$ grid points would need failure probability $\xi/G$ per decision in Eq. (11). The default sample count is not derived from Eq. (11), so the search guarantee does not transfer. The paper's randomized LCU compiler and gate-complexity claim are also outside this controlled-evolution implementation.

## RFE

RFE uses the randomized Fourier estimator of [Kshirsagar, Katabarwa and Johnson, Sec. 2, Eqs. (5)–(7)](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11322v3#S2). A draw $k$ contributes $z_ke^{-2\pi ikj/K}/M$ to frequency $j$. Grouping the identical draws of one power into one exact or pooled observation replaces their terms by the draw multiplicity times that observation's term. Uniform draws make the result an unbiased estimate of the finite Fourier coefficient. For an eigenstate with $U|\psi\rangle=e^{i\phi}|\psi\rangle$, where $\phi$ is the eigenphase angle in radians, the measured quadrature convention fixes the ideal signal $z_k=e^{ik\phi}$. Postprocessing returns the estimate $\widehat\phi$, the angle $2\pi j/K$ of the frequency $j$ of the largest-magnitude sampled coefficient reduced to $[-\pi,\pi)$. The reported `result.phase` is $j/K$ turns, so $\widehat\phi$ is $2\pi\,$`result.phase` reduced to $[-\pi,\pi)$, and the Hamiltonian energy estimate is $-\widehat\phi/\tau$. The arXiv:2209.11322v3 note on p. 1 records that Eq. (4) and the output line of Algorithm 1 were corrected for a missing sign. With the paper's S gate before the final Hadamard, outcome 0 has probability $(1-\sin(k\phi))/2$, so the raw imaginary outcome has mean $-\sin(k\phi)$ and the printed output is $2\pi-2\pi j/K$. NWQLib's imaginary quadrature uses ancilla phase $-\pi/2$ and measures $+\operatorname{Im}z_k$ directly, so its output needs no reflection. Work is $O(UK)$ for the U distinct drawn powers, with $O(K)$ workspace. These sizes depend on the estimator budget, not the Hilbert-space dimension.

The default K gives a grid spacing of $2\pi/49$ in $\widehat\phi$, or $1/49$ turn in `result.phase`, and maximum power 48. M sets the random-draw count. Its 194 quadrature draws become one query per distinct (power, quadrature) with both exact and sampled readout, and sampled readout requests 194 times `shots` coherent shots. The paper's precision theorem is Theorem 2.1 of arXiv:2209.11322v3, p. 6. For an eigenstate it gives an $\epsilon$-accurate phase estimate with probability above $1-\delta_{\mathrm{RFE}}$, where $\epsilon>0$ is the phase tolerance and $0<\delta_{\mathrm{RFE}}<1$ is the theorem's failure probability, when $K\ge\lceil2\pi/\epsilon\rceil$ and $M\ge\lceil(81\pi^2/2)\ln(8\pi/(\delta_{\mathrm{RFE}}\epsilon))\rceil$ single-shot draws. For any $\epsilon<\pi/2$ and $\delta_{\mathrm{RFE}}<1$ that bound exceeds 1100 draws, so the default $M=97$ is a finite workload without the theorem's guarantee. A successful finite Fourier fit establishes neither the eigenstate assumption nor the sample budget and supplies no RFE uncertainty interval. A dominant peak in a mixed spectrum need not represent its ground energy.

## RWPE

RWPE implements the basic Gaussian update of [Granade and Wiebe, arXiv:2208.04526v1, Eq. (7) and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.04526v1). Its coordinate is the unwrapped phase $\phi=-\tau E$. At step $k$, $\sigma_k=\sigma_0((e-1)/e)^{k/2}$, where $\sigma_0$ is `prior_std`, the relative evolution time is $1/\sigma_k$, and one bit $d$ changes the mean by $(1-2d)\sigma_k/\sqrt e$. The physical evolution is $e^{-i\tau H/\sigma_k}$, so a bare unitary with integer-power access is insufficient. All times are known from the prior and the step count, while the feedback depends on previous bits. The update uses constant scalar workspace at every Hilbert-space dimension.

The Hadamard circuit uses the feedback $-\mathrm{mean}/\sigma-\pi/2$, giving $P(0)=(1+\sin((\phi-\mathrm{mean})/\sigma))/2$. Integrating this likelihood against the Gaussian prior gives the positive $d=0$ mean shift in Eq. (7a) of arXiv:2208.04526v1, because $\mathbb E[x\sin x]=e^{-1/2}$ for a standard normal $x$. In the paper's likelihood, Eq. (2), $P(d|\omega)=\cos^2(t(\omega-\omega_{\mathrm{inv}})/2+d\pi/2)$, this feedback is $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{mean}+\pi\sigma/2$. Algorithm 1 prints $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{mean}-\pi\sigma/2$ alongside the same update, which gives $P(0)=(1-\sin((\omega-\mathrm{mean})/\sigma))/2$ and would lower the mean after $d=0$. At $t=1/\sigma$ the paper's own exact update, Eq. (6a), p. 4, reproduces Eq. (7a) with $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{mean}+\pi\sigma/2$ and gives the opposite sign with $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{mean}-\pi\sigma/2$. The sentence that introduces Eq. (7) on p. 5 states $\omega_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathrm{mean}$, for which Eq. (6a) leaves the mean unchanged. An independent Gaussian-integral check fixes the sign from the circuit probability.

This implementation omits Algorithm 2's consistency checks and unwinding. As discussed after Eq. (8), the basic walk has a finite exploration range. The mean can move at most $\sigma_0/(\sqrt e-\sqrt{e-1})$, about 2.96 prior widths (the paper prints 2.95), so a shrinking Gaussian width alone does not establish convergence. The Result reports when the scaled spectral radius and the prior mean reach that range.

RWPE's planned times do not determine its future feedback angles. A device-profile estimate (`estimate(plan, profile=...)`, see [Check device fit and run time](../profiles.md)) and a `compare(...)` row therefore give no forecast for the circuits with those angles, and a `scan` over Plans with different shot counts reports an objective that sums the forecasts of the individual circuits as unavailable, with the reason. These forecasts neither choose a feedback angle nor run the update.

`estimate(plan)` of a quantum RWPE `Plan` adds up its `max_steps` circuits. Its settings, its shots and, with product-formula powers, its operations do not depend on a feedback angle, so these totals are exact, and `estimate(plan, context=ResourceContext(basis="cx"))` gives the CX total in the same way. Dense powers leave the operation and CX totals unavailable. T gates, logical depth and arbitrary rotations stay unavailable, and a feedback angle can change the T count of a circuit. With `max_steps=0` every total is zero. `adaptive_rounds` and `classical_work` count only the `AdaptiveLoop` rounds and classical stages that the circuit description declares. The Gaussian update between circuits and the analysis run outside that description, so a zero in either total does not mean that RWPE has no feedback or host computation. To count the T gates of one RWPE circuit, `compile_logical(prepared, index=...)` compiles that circuit at its current feedback angle ([Estimate fault-tolerant resources](../fault-tolerant-resources.md)), and another angle can give another T count.

## Verify a result {#verify-a-result}

`result.verify(checks=QPEVerification(tolerance=...))` explicitly computes a nominal comparison of the spectrum and its eigenvalue clusters with a QR projector. SPE compares the lowest cluster and uses its declared overlap bound unless `minimum_overlap` is supplied. The other estimators compare the largest prepared cluster, with default minimum overlap 0.9. The comparison reports discrepancy, overlap, numerical adjustment, resolution and how many reference computations it performed or reused. It returns `(receipt, facts)`, a verification record and the two checked facts, `component_error` against `tolerance` in the output unit and `overlap_deficit = max(0, minimum_overlap - weight)` against zero, for `Certificate.with_verification` ([Check accuracy and verify a result](../verification.md#evidence-and-receipts)). `group_atol`, the absolute tolerance for clustering eigenvalues, is in the unit of the compared eigenvalues, the Problem's energy unit for a Hamiltonian and radians of eigenphase angle for a unitary, whatever the output. Its default `None` uses $10^{-8}(E_{\max}-E_{\min})$ for a Hamiltonian, which in exact arithmetic follows the scale of $H$ and does not change under $H+cI$, and $10^{-8}$ radians of principal angle difference for a unitary. A spectrum with a single value has tolerance 0 and forms one cluster, and an explicit value is used as given. The fact `group_tolerance` reports the tolerance used. The fact `estimated_resolved` is 0 when that tolerance is at or below $Du\max\lvert E\rvert$ for the $D$ eigenvalues and $u=2^{-53}$, where rounding may have split a degenerate eigenspace, and an explicit `group_atol` then chooses the clusters. This scale is a heuristic and not a LAPACK error bound, so it changes no cluster, a 0 does not prove a split and a 1 does not prove the clusters right. No tolerance recovers a gap that was lost when the matrix was formed. `reuse_only=True` requires spectral and reference data already computed for the same target and initial state, including data reloaded after save and load, instead of computing them. The saved arrays themselves are not rechecked ([Saved folders are read-only](../saved_evidence.md#saved-folders-are-read-only)). A reference for a supplied circuit is computed only with `materialize_preparation=True`. These dense comparisons run only on request and never expand Pauli input automatically. `QPEVerification` has its own `max_work` and `max_bytes` and compares each of its steps with them before the step runs. Its steps are the eigendecomposition, counted only when it is computed rather than reused, and then the QR and projection of the eigenvalue clusters at their sizes. A refusal names the step, what it needs and, for an explicit limit, the value to raise it to.

For the static estimators, `method_resolution` is the Result's grid spacing in output units. It includes a later supported `analyze(grid_size=...)` call and is not a fit-error bound. RWPE's method resolution is its current Gaussian standard deviation in output units. Neither quantity is a proven total-error bound, and verification leaves the original Result and `Plan` unchanged.

## Continue and save {#continuation-and-saved-evidence}

For RWPE, `prepare` builds only the circuit of the next query. Before each new step, RWPE saves in the Run its iteration state, which consists of the Gaussian mean, the number of processed steps, the next evolution time, feedback angle and backend seed, and the sequence number of the Run checkpoint of the pending step. `submit` and `Run.wait` advance this state on the backend of the Run. Each completed measurement updates the Gaussian exactly once. A submitted measurement whose outcome is not yet known is not submitted again, and after cancellation its recorded cost remains visible.

Before submission, the Run rejects a measurement whose fixed seed would repeat the sampling stream of a known measurement, whether completed, uncollected, uncertain or in the same batch. After completion, the job identifiers that the provider returned are checked as well. During recovery the same completed measurement can be read again, but it gives no second posterior update.

`result.save(path)` and `load_result(path)` keep the concrete method, the evolutions it chose (including the polar factor of a unitary input), the observations, the Gaussian moments and the existing numerical caches. Loading rejects a conflicting method before it loads state data. [Saved formats](../development/execution.md#saved-formats) lists the archive format names and versions. `run.save(path)` and `load_run(path, backend=...)` keep the Run's progress, the random-number state and the circuit caches. Loading performs no schedule search, eigensolve, estimator replay or circuit construction, and reanalysis reuses the unchanged data.

`result.analyze(...)` measures nothing new. For RWPE it reads the Gaussian mean and the number of processed steps from the saved run state, and its width follows Granade and Wiebe, arXiv:2208.04526v1, Eq. (7b), without replaying observations. Without saved run state, the RWPE estimate is reported as missing. Its stop reason is the one the run recorded when it ended.

## Limits

- A single-mode estimate establishes neither the ground energy nor coverage of the dominant component, and the preparation determines which part of the spectrum is observed.
- QCELS, SPE and RFE return no interval, and RWPE's interval is a nominal Gaussian model interval ([Intervals and sampling error](#intervals-and-verification)).
- The papers' accuracy theorems need conditions that NWQLib does not check or does not meet by default ([Choose an estimator](#choose-an-estimator)). QCELS's theorems assume $p_0>0.71$, which is not checked, and consecutive powers. SPE's search guarantee assumes the paper's binary-search decisions, not a grid scan. RFE's theorem needs more than 1100 draws, against a default of 97.
- A spread QCELS schedule can fit a replica of the energy, and the Result does not report it ([Choose num_times and max_time](#qcels-schedule)).
- The basic RWPE walk has a finite exploration range and omits Algorithm 2's consistency checks and unwinding ([RWPE](#rwpe)).
- Dense powers are explicit finite constructions, not scalable oracles. With an explicit `max_work=1_000_000_000`, each dense block fits on up to seven system qubits.
- The default sampling assessment stays INCONCLUSIVE, because NWQLib attaches no bound from the papers' sample-count conditions.
- `max_bytes` covers the schedule, the analysis arrays and a coarse estimate of each circuit, not the records of a `Plan`, so a very large number of distinct queries can exhaust memory during planning ([Memory during planning and preparation](#memory-during-planning-and-preparation)).
- For unitary input, the classical signal removes the radial rounding of each eigenvalue and no other rounding ([Classical execution](#classical-execution)).

[Limitations and open work](../ROADMAP.md#qpe) lists the open items for these methods.

## Numerical scope and planning limits

`max_power` and `max_trotter_steps` default to `None`, so no default limit applies to the length of an evolution. An explicit `max_power` refuses a schedule whose largest power or RWPE time exceeds it, before any draw, and an explicit `max_trotter_steps` refuses a power whose step count exceeds it, and each refusal names the field and the count needed. `max_work`, when set, bounds the [work units](../glossary.md#work-units) of each planning stage whose work grows faster than its arrays, where a work unit is one scalar operation in a count that planning derives from the problem sizes by formula before the stage runs. These stages are the SVD that forms the polar factor of a dense unitary input, each dense block, the pair scan of the Pauli commutation table and the commutator count, the analysis of QCELS, SPE and RFE, the classical routine and the dense-to-Pauli conversion. Each stage is compared with `max_work` on its own, and their sum is not. `max_work=None` is the default and sets no work cap. `max_bytes`, by default the physical memory of this computer, bounds the arrays whose sizes are known in advance and a coarse estimate of each circuit's memory ([Memory during planning and preparation](#memory-during-planning-and-preparation)). For the power schedule, the padding of a non-power-of-two input, the automatic choice of $\tau$ and each Suzuki step, the work grows no faster than these arrays, so `max_bytes` alone bounds them. The RWPE update after each measured bit is scalar arithmetic, and `max_steps` bounds the number of updates. Neither limit bounds the CPU time or memory of LAPACK or Qiskit. The `ExecutionLimits` shared by all methods cover circuit preparation, measurement and data. Symbolic evolutions and repeated steps remain compact until the Qiskit circuits are built explicitly.

The QCELS analysis limit check counts at most `(1 + QCELS_BRACKET_EVALUATIONS)*G = 65*G` objective evaluations, the $G$ grid points plus one bracket for each possible local minimum. Each evaluation counts 32 scalar size units per complex point, so the QCELS formula is $32\cdot65\cdot GN$ units plus 64 per scalar quadrature sample for the reduction. Here $N$ counts distinct complex points after quadrature reduction. These are conservative operation-size units, not measured CPU instructions. Grid and scores use $O(G)$ storage and row temporaries $O(N)$, without a $G\times N$ matrix. Limits are checked before the schedule is built or measured and again before reanalysis. The default has $N=11$, $G=4096$ and 22 quadrature queries, giving $32\cdot65\cdot4096\cdot11+64\cdot22=93{,}717{,}888$ units. A limit of 90 million rejects this analysis count before measurement, while 100 million and the uncapped default `max_work=None` accept this check. Other planning stages have separate limit checks.

When a limit check fails, the error names each exceeded limit with its current value and the value the step needs. `NUMERICAL_RELATION_RTOL` is a dimensionless tolerance of the library's input checks, defined in `nwqlib._validation`, and not an accuracy setting of the method.

### Memory during planning and preparation {#memory-during-planning-and-preparation}

Before it draws a schedule, planning compares the size of that schedule with `max_bytes`, and for QCELS, SPE and RFE then the size of the later analysis arrays. Each refusal names the stage, its bytes and the settings that set them. Here $S$ is `num_samples`, $K$ is `num_frequencies`, $d$ is `fourier_degree`, $P$ is the largest QCELS power from [Choose num_times and max_time](#qcels-schedule) and $Q$ is the number of logical queries.

| Estimator | Schedule size | Analysis size | Settings that lower them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `QCELS` | `64*2*(1 + min(num_times, P))` | `32*G + 256*Q + 128*N`, with `G = max(grid_size, 8*(max(p) - min(p)) + 1)` for the powers $p$ and $N$ complex points | `num_times`, `max_time/tau` while $P$ sets the minimum or the grid, `grid_size` while it sets `G` |
| `SPE` | `64*(2*S + 2*d + 1)` | `160*grid_size + 256*S` | `num_samples`, `fourier_degree`, `grid_size` |
| `RFE` | `64*(2*S + K)` | `160*K + 256*S` | `num_samples`, `num_frequencies` |
| `RWPE` | `64*max(1, max_steps)` | None | `max_steps` |

The schedule size covers the schedule entries, plus the SPE filter frequencies or the RFE frequencies as an allowance. Planning allocates the samples and the SPE filter weights, while $K$ only bounds the powers that RFE draws. The analysis arrays are not allocated until the analysis runs. A `Plan` used only for its resource estimates can therefore set a larger `max_bytes` for an array it does not hold, and a refusal against the physical memory offers this route, with the amount, when the part of the analysis size that follows the samples fits that memory. This covers the analysis of QCELS and SPE when the grid drives its size, and RFE when $K$ drives it. RFE's offer in a schedule refusal gives its later analysis size, so one new `Plan` passes both checks, as `RFE(num_samples=8, num_frequencies=10**9, max_bytes=160000002048)` does. A refusal driven by samples, and a schedule refusal of QCELS, SPE or RWPE, offers smaller settings instead, because planning holds those samples and entries. A refusal against an explicit `max_bytes` also names the size of its own check, so the next check can ask for more.

An explicit `max_bytes` takes the place of the physical memory in every later check of that `Plan`, including the circuit-memory check of `prepare`. A value chosen to make a large schedule plan therefore also lets those checks pass at sizes the computer may not hold, and it does not make execution affordable. Solving such a `Plan` still needs the analysis arrays.

`max_bytes` does not count the `Plan`, query, power and `Program` records, and it does not bound them or the memory of the process. Nothing refuses for these records, so a very large query count can exhaust memory during planning, while the limit stays satisfied. The count of logical queries is at most `2*min(S, K)` for RFE, `2*min(S, d + 1)` for SPE and `2*(1 + min(num_times, P))` for QCELS, and it equals `max(1, max_steps)` for RWPE. These bounds hold before the schedule is drawn. Whole-plan measurements of sampled RFE and QCELS, with Pauli input and the product-formula route, gave these traced heap totals divided by $Q$:

| Input | $Q$ | Peak during planning | Kept after planning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RFE, two qubits, $S=500$ | 1000 | 30,434 bytes | 23,488 bytes |
| RFE, two qubits, $S=2000$ | 4000 | 30,411 bytes | 23,370 bytes |
| QCELS, two qubits, `num_times=1000` | 2002 | 30,101 bytes | 23,368 bytes |
| RFE, ten qubits, $S=500$ | 1000 | 30,107 bytes | 23,545 bytes |

That is about 30 to 31 KB at the peak and 23 to 24 KB kept, per logical query, on these inputs ([Measured evidence](#measured-evidence)). They are not sizes of isolated objects or bounds on process memory, and they do not describe the exact trajectory, classical execution or RWPE. The process footprint of these runs gave no clean slope, and no exhaustion has been measured, so the query count at which memory runs out is not known. [Open work](../ROADMAP.md#qpe-plan-record-scale-on-the-query-count-axis) records this limit.

A schedule that plans can still refuse at `prepare`. When a circuit is prepared, and before it is built, the Run compares a coarse estimate of its memory with `max_bytes`, 200 bytes for each Aer native position, with each product-formula step counted through `powers.step_memory_bytes` and each dense power through `powers.dense_memory_bytes`. The estimate is calibrated on Aer only ([Prepared-circuit memory estimate](../ENGINEERING_CONSTANTS.md#prepared-circuit-memory-estimate)), and it does not bound process memory.

### Commutator count and common step {#commutator-count-and-common-step}

The pair scan of the Pauli commutation table counts the nested tests that the full Pauli-triangle expression would make for each anticommuting pair, every later term included. Planning uses the full expression when that count, times the number of 64-bit mask words, is at most 1,300,000,000 (`nwqlib.subroutines.trotterization.error_budget.EXACT_CENSUS_WORD_TESTS`), and otherwise the second-order degree-capped suffix relaxation, with pair tests and weighted degrees only. The `Plan` names the expression it used ([Proposition 36](../mathematics.md#r36)). The work and byte limits are checked for that expression and refuse it with the amount it needs, and QPE has no option to request the relaxation instead.

The work depends on the anticommuting pairs of the table, not on its number of terms alone. If all $L$ terms on $q\le64$ qubits anticommute pairwise, $N_{\rm pair}=L(L-1)/2$ and $N_{\rm nested}=L(L-1)(2L-1)/6$, and packed tests and magnitude visits give the work $2Lq+2(N_{\rm pair}+N_{\rm nested})$ for the full expression and $2Lq+5N_{\rm pair}+2L$ for the relaxation. Such a table has at most $2q+1$ terms. To see this, write each nonidentity Pauli string as a vector in $\mathbb F_2^{2q}$. The symplectic Gram matrix of $m$ pairwise anticommuting strings has a zero diagonal and ones elsewhere, so its rank over $\mathbb F_2$ is $m$ for even $m$ and $m-1$ for odd $m$, and that rank is at most $2q$. At $q=20$ this gives at most 41 terms, 820 pairs, 22,140 nested tests and 47,560 work units for the full expression. A table with more terms has commuting pairs and makes no more tests than the formulas give at its $L$, so at a larger $L$ they are upper bounds on its counts and do not describe a real table. The limit checks use the counts of the table you supply.

An exact-trajectory `Plan` must also meet every power's error budget with one common step, within `max_trotter_steps` when that is set, so the commutator count does not set its term limit. Other planning work, storage and step limits also apply.

The exact trajectory's common-step selection computes the kept coefficient mass and the one-step rotation-angle discrepancy once for each step count it checks, and it adds each identity phase that the circuit uses once to the exact running prefix. The arithmetic reductions of each check take $O(L+K)$ visits for $L$ kept terms and $K$ requested powers, and neither these rechecks nor those of independent powers are compared with `max_work`. Each prefix is accepted only when its complete exact subtotal fits its error budget. The first step count inverts only the product-formula and pruning terms, so the rounding terms of the emitted step, phases and angles can exceed the margin those terms leave, as happens at tight budgets and long evolution times. Planning then checks the next count on the same grid and, if that also fails, one count from a conservative upper bound on the rounding terms, which takes at most two more checks and no new commutator count or circuit. That count holds the product-formula term to at most half of the budget left after an upper bound on the terms that do not depend on the step count, and it is a sufficient count, not the smallest one that fits. The default exact QCELS trajectory of $0.34\,ZI-0.11\,IZ+0.57\,ZZ+0.09\,XX+0.09\,YY$ at `controlled_power_error_budget=1e-11`, for example, uses 5,350,300 steps, where the first count of 3,782,040 and the next count of 3,782,050 fail their checks. The powers of one trajectory share one propagated state, so the correction changes their common grid and never one power's count alone. When no checked count fits, planning refuses, and the message names the power, each contribution and the margin. Such a refusal shows that this rule confirmed no count, not that the target error is unattainable. A larger `controlled_power_error_budget` can be tried, with no guarantee that it passes, because each budget has its own counts and its own exact check.

### Dense powers {#dense-powers}

Planning forms the polar factor V once per `Plan` from one SVD, counted as $9D^3+8D^2$ units of planning work, and the classical route and verification diagonalize V, not A. The exact trajectory constructs one block per distinct positive gap between consecutive powers, and a sampled `Plan` one block per distinct nonzero power. A gap of k powers is constructed by repeated squaring of V and built as a controlled dense block. Each gap's limit check covers its matrix work, its synthesis and the cached matrices held in memory. Planning counts each block's matrix arithmetic in $D^3$ units per product of two $D$-square matrices, where $D$ is the dimension of the system register. A unitary gap or power k counts the $\mathrm{bit\_length}(k)+\mathrm{popcount}(k)-2$ products of an independent repeated squaring, although the squares of V are shared within the `Plan` and a later block forms only the squares it lacks. Its bytes include every cached square, $16JD^2$ for the largest square index $J$ of the `Plan`'s exponents. A Hamiltonian power takes one product and also counts the eigendecomposition, $8D^3+32D^2$ units, because the first power that is constructed computes it and the others reuse it. Planning adds $11\cdot8^m+(m^2+5m+256)\cdot4^m$ units for the synthesis on $m=n+1$ qubits with $n$ system qubits. Each block is checked against `max_work` on its own, so an explicit `max_work=1_000_000_000` accepts dense blocks on up to seven system qubits. The `Plan`'s total over its blocks is not compared with `max_work`.

## Source and code map

The code is in `src/nwqlib/algorithms/qpe/`:

- `method.py`: method settings, original inputs, the circuit description, readout and static reconstruction.
- `records.py`: queries, powers and results, with their input, schedule and error-allowance associations.
- `powers.py`: the signed-power numerical and circuit constructors.
- `numerical.py`: the QCELS, SPE, RFE and RWPE scalar routines and their source conventions.
- `controller.py`: RWPE's iteration state between steps, its Gaussian mean and pending measurement point, saved in the Run.
- `archive.py`, `verification.py`: saving and loading of the data and caches, and the nominal reference comparison of `result.verify`.
- `subroutines/trotterization/error_budget.py`: commutator bounds and the exact choice of an integer step count for a given evolution time. On the common grid, QPE uses those bounds to choose sufficient cumulative counts of the shared step and to check the represented steps against them.
- `subroutines/qpe/coherent.py`: the separate coherent phase-register circuit.

The QPE methods build their controlled product-formula steps in `powers.py` from Qiskit Pauli evolution gates. They use only the bounds of the Trotterization subroutine, not its circuit builders.
